The organization X-COM directs global defense and turn-based ground operations against UFOs. A strategic macro layer and destructive tactical missions with permanent soldiers create emergent stories from the mechanics.

On the Geoscape you direct the organization X-COM, send interceptors against UFOs, and after a shoot-down you go to the ground, where every soldier can die for good. Research, manufacturing, and the panic of nations are the macro layer. The tactical map is the micro layer, which receives coordinates from the macro, not a mission written in advance.

Julian Gollop and Mythos Games designed the game in 1994, and MicroProse published it. In Europe it is called UFO: Enemy Unknown, in America X-COM: UFO Defense; both names point to the same rule. The engine is a turn-based map of the globe and a turn-based dungeon on the ground. There is no real-time base; time is scarce because soldiers panic inside it.

The decision that the strategic layer generates the tactical one has a consequence. A story about who got scared and who did not survive is born from the mechanics, not from a script. In 1994 real-time strategy is becoming fashionable. X-COM insists on the turn and on the fog, so it is a counterweight, not a late fashion.

Countries pay you for as long as you protect them. When you fail, they leave the council, so the economy is trust, not just harvested spice. What has aged is the inventory and some shots into the dark, where the visibility rule feels like an interface puzzle.

What has not aged is the tension of a drop. Opening a UFO's door still hurts, because behind it a soldier you named can end. Gollop continues from his Laser Squad. X-COM adds a planet above the squad, so a tactical death has a budget and a politics.

Compare the permanent loss with Fire Emblem. There the army belongs to a fairy tale; here the army is an office that has to buy new armor when the old armor does not return. In a seminar, let a match run until someone falls into a panic. That sentence of the system says more about the game than a retelling of the invasion.
