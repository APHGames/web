Tommy Vercetti builds a criminal empire in a stylized 1980s. Music radio stations, period fashion, and the city created an open world as a cultural time capsule.

Tommy Vercetti, voiced by Ray Liotta, arrives in a city styled on the 1980s and assembles his own business out of street jobs. Between missions you switch radio stations, change suits, and drive through a city built as a period mood, not as a map with icons.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/310-cover-1.jpg)

Rockstar North, with Dan Houser's writing and Leslie Benzies's production, published it in 2002 on the PlayStation 2. Technically it continues on RenderWare, like the game before it. Motorcycles, aircraft, and the purchase of businesses were added after the third game. The city thereby stops being only a track between cutscenes and becomes property you occupy.

Licensed music on the stations turns a drive into a time capsule. Switch off the radio and half of what distinguished the game in 2002 from other open cities disappears. Mafia, from Illusion Softworks, comes out in the same year. Vice City bets on pace, style, and exaggeration where the Brno game bets on traffic law and a slower walk.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/310-screenshot-2.jpg)

Individual missions are often strict and linear. The freedom is between them, in the city, not inside an assignment that wants a specific car and a specific survival. The look of the characters and the sharpness of the textures have aged. The radios, the colored districts, and the feeling that an open world can be a cultural capsule have not.

The references to gangster film and to televised Miami are not an accidental coat of paint. The economy of the missions is built so the player feels a rise inside one decade. For teaching open worlds it is the second step after the previous game. A map is no longer enough; the city needs taste, music, and a dress code.

The voice acting holds a scene even where the animation is laughed at today. Voice has aged more slowly here than the body. Without the stations a capable action game remains. With the stations there remains the reason that year is remembered as the year of a city, not the year of a single mission.
