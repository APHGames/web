Michael, Franklin, and Trevor in Grand Theft Auto V plan heists in Los Santos, and the player switches among them, often in the middle of the city, so the same street has three lives. Shooting, driving, and cover are still the language of the series, but preparing a heist adds a choice of crew and tactic. Beside that the world offers tennis, stocks, and an aimless drive.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/432-screenshot-1.jpg)

Rockstar North released the game in September 2013 on PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360, with Dan Houser on the script and Leslie Benzies leading the studio. The later move to newer consoles and to PC is part of a long life, not a one-off release. The RAGE engine and Euphoria animation carry three characters, a dense city, and car physics. Switching between heroes is a technical trick that has to keep three simulations inside one Los Santos.

The decision to give the campaign three protagonists means a mission can show the same heist from several positions. The consequence is a parallel that one body in the previous games could not do: a satirical rich man, a young man from the neighborhood, and a violent man without a brake. GTA Online, launched the same year, gradually turns the city into a service. The campaign remains an authored sentence; online becomes an economy that outgrows the series.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/432-screenshot-2.jpg)

In 2013 this is the peak of the open city at the end of one console generation. The satire of Los Angeles, the ads, and the talk shows matter as much as the number of guns. The heists are the peak of the mission design, because they finally give the open city a plan, not only a path from marker to marker. Even so they remain carefully directed.

That directorial corridor of the missions has aged beside the freer world, and so has some of the humor that belongs to 2013. Online monetization is a different history from the campaign, and it spoils the memory if you mix them. The density of the city and the characters of the three men hold. It is still the scale by which you tell whether a new open city has a society of its own, or only terrain.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/432-screenshot-3.jpg)

Switching character in the middle of the world is a verb, not a menu. Suddenly you are several blocks away in another life, and the city has kept running. In class, separate the 2013 campaign from the service the city became afterward. They are two different arguments about what an open world is for.
