Small units fight over territorial points on destructive battlefields of the Second World War. Cover, suppression, flanking, and deformation of the map shifted RTS from counting armies to terrain tactics.

In Company of Heroes you command small squads on a Norman field, and cover, suppressing fire, and a flank decide more than the number of tanks on the bar. The map also collapses: a wall that covered you may be gone in a minute. The unit is a squad you reinforce, not one soldier as an economic item. Losing a man hurts, and you still have a reason to keep the squad alive instead of writing it off as an icon.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/364-cover-1.jpg)

Relic Entertainment of Vancouver published it in 2006 through THQ on its own Essence Engine. From here the studio takes terrain as the main rule of strategy, not as decoration under a base. The consequence of cover and suppression is that a frontal attack on a machine gun is a loss even with better numbers. The player learns to go around, and a map that offers no flank is a bad map, not a bad army.

In 2006, beside Warcraft III and after Age of Empires III, this is a turn away from counting armies. Relic claims that an RTS can be about a trench. Part of the campaign is scripted and has aged into familiar missions. The multiplayer language of cover, the flank, and points on the map has not aged, and later games in the series still drill it.

Points on the map replace part of the mining. The economy is territorial, and therefore a fight over a crossroads is also a fight over income. For RTS students it is a break that can be set beside Total War. There, a mass in an open field. Here, infantry that cannot live without the bushes.

Destroying buildings changes the plan in the middle of a match. The consequence is that the same map in the twentieth minute is not the one you were given in the first, and therefore a replay makes sense. Able Company in the campaign holds the scale of a platoon, not of an entire front. The game thereby admits that its strength is in a small number of bodies with clear cover.

Later games and Dawn of War II from the same studio take this appetite for terrain further. The first Company of Heroes is still the purest statement of 2006. When you play it today, try to win one firefight without putting a squad in green cover. If that is easy, you are playing a different strategy from the one Relic wrote.

Company of Heroes is a real-time strategy video game series developed by Relic Entertainment. The first stand-alone expansion pack to Company of Heroes, it was released September 25, 2007, in the US and September 28 in Europe.

The second stand-alone expansion pack to Company of Heroes, it was released on April 9, 2009. Company of Heroes Online was a free massively multiplayer online real-time strategy game developed by Relic Entertainment and published by THQ.

The game has no link with the original Company of Heroes multiplayer system and is a stand-alone game, unlike its predecessors. The game uses the same graphics engine as the retail release of Company of Heroes.

Company of Heroes Online was a real-time strategy game which was based on Company of Heroes. A unique feature of Company of Heroes Online was the large number of combinations of hero units, special abilities and doctrinal abilities a player could utilize, giving the potential for diverse and creative strategies.

A sequel, Company of Heroes 2, was released on June 25, 2013, in North America and Europe.
