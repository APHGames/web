A young hero builds a rebellion and recruits the 108 Stars of Destiny. Recruiting dozens of characters and a developing base translate the revolution directly into the progression system.

A young commander collects the hundred and eight Stars of Destiny, builds an army from them, and watches the rebellion's headquarters grow by rooms you unlocked through recruitment, not through money. The great battles of armies are a different mode from the party fight. The revolution is both in the character menu and on the map where the ranks walk.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/191-screenshot-1.jpg)

Yoshitaka Murayama designed the game in 1995 at Konami on the PlayStation. It is an early RPG on the new console that stays two-dimensional and bets on the number of people, not on a polygon of the hero. The engine is a classic Japanese RPG with a base added on. It does not surprise the technology of 1995; it surprises with a system in which progress is a list of names.

The decision that recruitment is the main verb has a consequence. A player who last time collected swords here collects people, and an empty place in the hall is a visible debt. In 1995, beside the 3D launch of the PlayStation, Suikoden claims that the console can also carry a novelistic structure. A hundred and eight characters is an ambition that leaves some of them thin, and still it holds the feeling of a movement.

The castle is not a menu between chapters. It is the measure of how far the revolution has gone, so coming home is a reward, not an interruption. What has aged is the simple combat and the fact that a number of characters never get a character at all. The collection is the design; the depth of every card is not.

What has not aged is the fantasy that you win a war by whom you persuade. The base still turns politics into something you can walk through. Murayama builds a series from this in which the count and the headquarters will return. The first entry matters because it says the rule out loud: revolution is recruitment.

Compare the party with Final Fantasy VI. Square builds an ensemble you know by name in depth; Konami builds a crowd in which the depth belongs to the leadership and the rest belong to a place in the hall. In a seminar, open the castle halfway through the game and at the end. If doors have been added, the progression system is architectural, not just an experience table.

A compilation of the first two Suikoden games, Suikoden I & II, was released for PlayStation Portable in Japan on February 23, 2006. A high-definition English localization version of the compilation under the title Suikoden I & II HD Remaster: Gate Rune and Dunan Unification Wars released worldwide on March 6, 2025 for Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5, Windows, Xbox One and Xbox Series X/S.

Suikoden plays like a traditional role-playing video game, with the player moving characters across a landscape, advancing the plot by completing tasks and talking with other characters. It has been compared to Beyond the Beyond The Hero may recruit up to 107 new characters to his cause, although not all recruited characters are playable characters, and the battle system in Suikoden features six person parties in combat, with each character being individually controllable, with an option to allow the characters to fight automatically.

There are no weapon shops in Suikoden and equipment shopping is limited to armour and items. Suikoden has over 108 allied characters and numerous enemies and neutral characters.

Suikoden was released for the PlayStation in Japan on December 15, 1995.

During the inaugural Interactive Achievement Awards, the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences nominated Suikoden for "Console Game of the Year" and "Console Role-Playing Game of the Year". Suikoden began the Suikoden franchise, which includes video games, light novels, and manga.

Game designer Warren Spector listed Suikoden as his sixth favorite video game of all time. He said the limited choices in Suikoden inspired him to expand on the idea with more meaningful choices, with the development of Deus Ex.

A stage play adaptation of Suikoden was announced during a livestream for the game franchise on March 4, 2025.
