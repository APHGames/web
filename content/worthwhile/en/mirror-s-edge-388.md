Courier Faith flees the city by means of parkour. A first-person body and momentum prove that the FPS view can serve precise movement instead of shooting.

In Mirror's Edge you are the courier Faith, and you flee the city across rooftops in first person. The color red marks the path, the run has weight, and a roll steals speed a fall would otherwise take. Shooting is possible and deliberately clumsy. The game wants you to flee, and therefore a weapon in the hand is a foreign object, not the identity of the genre.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/388-screenshot-1.jpg)

DICE of Stockholm, the Battlefield studio, published it in 2008 through Electronic Arts. It runs on Unreal Engine 3, not on Frostbite, because Frostbite at that moment was still serving Bad Company and this game needed a more finished base. The studio tuned the light itself so the white city would hold. Rhianna Pratchett wrote the script. Faith is not a soldier; she is a body that delivers messages in a city that watches movement, and therefore the parkour is political and physical.

The consequence of first person is that you do not see your own legs as in third person and yet you have to trust the landing. The animation of the hands and the sound of the impact carry information a camera from outside would give for free. In 2008, among the cover shooters, it is a polemic. The FPS view can belong to movement, and DICE says so at the very moment it is known for war maps.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/388-screenshot-2.jpg)

The combat and some of the sterile interiors have aged. The run, the red navigation, and the white city have not, and the sequel Catalyst later opens what was a linear rooftop here. Runner vision is a tutorial painted into the world. Whoever turns it off gets a harder and more honest city and sees how much work the color was doing for the player.

For movement students it is a class about momentum. A jump without a run-up is a different jump, and the game punishes a shortcut with a fall, not with a line of dialogue. The direction of Senta Jakobsen and the producer Owen O'Brien holds the project as a conscious detour for DICE. It is not Battlefield without guns; it is a different verb inside the same publisher.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/388-cover-3.jpg)

The shortness of the campaign is fair. Momentum cannot be stretched into forty hours without becoming a shift, and the game suspects that. When you play it today, do not take a pistol until a level forces you to. Every voluntary firefight is a detour from the sentence DICE wrote in 2008 about flight.
