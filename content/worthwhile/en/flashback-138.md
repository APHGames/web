Conrad recovers his memory and escapes an alien conspiracy. The rotoscoped fluidity of Prince of Persia met sci-fi action and a more extensive adventure structure here.

Conrad B. Hart runs along a rotoscoped platform, collects objects, and pieces together the memory a conspiracy took from him. Movement has weight as in Prince of Persia, but the level is longer and carries an inventory. This is no longer only an escape from a dungeon; it is a science-fiction quest.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/138-cover-1.jpg)

Paul Cuisset directed the game in 1992 at Delphine, the studio that a year earlier had released Another World. The Amiga is the home; the consoles come after it. The rotoscoping is movement copied by hand into sprites. It is not Chahi's vectors; it is a fluidity that on 16 bits looks like an expensive animated film.

The decision to join adventure objects to a platformer has a consequence. A player who can jump may still not be able to solve the puzzle, and the reverse is also true. In 1992 the European school of the cinematic platformer already has two distinct voices. Chahi is silent; Cuisset narrates and gives the hero a shield and a weapon.

Saving is rarer than a player today would want. Death rewinds a long stretch, because the animation and the trap are designed as a scene, not as an endless checkpoint. What has aged is precisely that punishment and some unreadable spots. The beauty of the run does not excuse a fall you could not have read.

What has not aged is the ambition that a platformer can have the pace of a thriller. Conrad's run still looks like something that could be screened without an interface. Memory as a plot holds the levels together. You are not in an abstract world of jumps; you are in a story that unlocks floor by floor.

Compare the inertia with Mechner. Both want a body that does not stop on a pixel; Cuisset adds science fiction and a longer sentence to it. In a seminar, notice where the game stops being about dexterity and starts being about an object in the right pocket. That is the seam of the two genres.

Flashback, released as Flashback: The Quest for Identity in the United States, is a 1992 cinematic platformer developed by Delphine Software and published by U.S. Flashback was ported to the Mega Drive/Genesis, MS-DOS, Acorn Archimedes, and Super NES in 1993.

An updated port titled Flashback: Remastered Edition was released for Nintendo Switch on 7 June 2018, for PlayStation 4 on 20 November 2018, and for Windows on 29 November 2018. The video capture technique of Flashback was invented independently of Prince of Persia, using a more complicated method of first tracing video images onto transparencies.

In 2013, a Flashback remake by VectorCell was released for the PC and consoles. Flashback 2 by Microids, was released on 16 November 2023 for the PlayStation 5, Windows, and Xbox Series X/S, with previous generation consoles getting their release early 2024.

As a cinematic platformer, Flashback features gameplay similar to that of 1989's Prince of Persia, and Delphine's Another World released in 1991. Flashback, along with Another World, shipped as a single retail package by Microids for the PlayStation 4, Nintendo Switch, and Xbox One in Europe on 16 April 2020.

Computer Gaming World approved of Flashback s "superbly rotoscoped graphics", "fluid movement", and sound card audio. They remarked that Flashback is slow-paced at times, but retains the player's interest through its compelling plot and involving puzzles.

Jim Trunzo reviewed Flashback: The Quest for Identity in White Wolf #38 (1993), giving the game a final evaluation of "Very Good" and stated that "Even gamers not including toward arcade entertainment will quickly become engrossed by Flashback. Players will grow fascinated as this sci-fi mystery unfolds screen by screen." Electronic Gaming Monthly commented that the Sega CD version is "virtually identical" to the Genesis version, but that Flashback is still an essential purchase for those who do not already own a different version.

However, he lauded Flashback itself for its graphics, animation and "fiendishly clever puzzles", and said that though the game superficially resembles Another World and Heart of the Alien, it is "in a class by itself that easily surpasses them". Entertainment Weekly similarly said that Flashback held up well, but that the Sega CD version's improvements seem less significant than its frequent load times.

And one last note, the 3DO controller, for once, doesn't interfere (much) with the game's demanding control scheme." Flashback was listed in the Guinness World Records as the best-selling French game of all time. Flashback makes us feel like we're in that alien world, and with its lifelike animation and excellent moves, we can't help but love it." In February 2011, Wirtualna Polska ranked it as the 17th best Amiga game and the Polish edition of CHIP ranked it as the tenth best Amiga game.

In 2004, readers of Retro Gamer voted Flashback as the 65th top retro game. In 1994, PC Gamer UK named Flashback the 17th best computer game of all time.

The editors wrote, "Flashback proves that, when it comes to producing original, stylish and challenging games, there are few more adept than the French." IGN ranked the game 79th on their list of the "Top 100 SNES Games of All Time". They also praised the combination of action and adventure-style puzzle challenges, summarizing: "The game itself is a [..] of an experience and the excellent plot also keeps the whole thing incredibly atmospheric." In the same year, Flux ranked Flashback tenth on its list of the "Top 100 Video Games".

In May 2021, Flashback 2 was announced for personal computers and multiple consoles. Flashback 2 begins in the middle of Conrad's adventure in the original Flashback, before the plot diverges from that of the original game.
