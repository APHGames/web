Colorful armies fight on compact maps governed by Commanding Officers. Extremely readable unit relationships showed that deep tactics can work perfectly in a handheld format.

On a small grid you send infantry, tanks, artillery, and aircraft, and every turn you solve who can hurt whom at all. Commanding officers are not just portraits. Their power overturns the value of units for a few turns, so the same map plays differently under another officer.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/302-cover-1.jpg)

Intelligent Systems brought to the Game Boy Advance a line that began in Japan as Famicom Wars in 1988. For Western players, 2001 was the first meeting with it. Units have readable relationships and the terrain is visible at a glance. Deep tactics fit on a screen you hold in your hands.

The turn-based round forces you to name every move. On a handheld, where play happens in pieces, that is an advantage, not a brake. The campaign teaches unit relationships gradually and then breaks them with the officers' powers. A newcomer sees the rule and an experienced player sees the exception.

In 2001, when the Game Boy Advance was just beginning, the game showed that a portable console could carry the strategic seriousness of a desktop game, only at another pace. The war theme is delivered in loud colors and comic officers. That contrast keeps things readable; it is not an apology for simplicity.

The animations and the officers' stories are of their time. The grid, the movement range, and the units' mutual bonuses have not aged, because they were designed as a language, not as a spectacle. Later entries added more officers and weather. The foundation taught in the first Western game is still the one the series stands on.

Next to today's tactical games on a phone, Advance Wars still wins because every unit has a clear reason to exist. For teaching it is a direct example: limit the screen, make the relationships clear, and the depth does not disappear; it merely stops hiding in a menu.

Nintendo Power awarded the game a perfect 5 star rating, stating that "Advance Wars treads on new ground, taking the strategy genre to a place where gamers of all tastes will be gung-ho for it". AllGame commented: "Ingeniously designed, Advance Wars manages to be both in-depth, and instantly accessible, simply because it presents the game in easily manageable chunks".

During the 5th Annual Interactive Achievement Awards, the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences awarded Advance Wars with "Hand-Held Game of the Year".
