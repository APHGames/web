Marth leads an army in turn-based battles, and units can die permanently. Permadeath joined strategic pieces with individual characters and founded a significant tactical RPG tradition.

Marth leads an army on a grid, and every unit that dies in a turn is gone. There is no reserve team around the corner. Between battles you follow a war for a kingdom. A character has a name and a relation to the plot, so an infantryman is not just an attack number.

Intelligent Systems and the designer Shozo Kaga released the game in 1990 on the Famicom through Nintendo. This first entry did not reach the West for a long time. The engine is a turn-based map with menus. There is no real time; position, weapon, and whether you may risk the unit at all decide.

Permanent death is a decision that changes the genre. The consequence: the player flinches from a favorable trade, because the loss still hurts in the next chapter. In 1990 the Japanese RPG can do a party on a world map. Fire Emblem turns the party into an army and the map into a battlefield on which people end.

The story of Altea is chivalric and straightforward. Political grayness comes to the genre only later; here the novelty is the bond itself between a piece and a person. What has aged is the strictness and the spare interface. A new player can lose a character before understanding a weapon's range.

What has not aged is the price of a mistake. The tactical role-playing game still lives on a chess piece having a funeral. The weapon triangle as you know it from later entries is not yet the main language here. The foundation is permadeath and the fact that you command concrete names.

When you explain why Nintendo kept the series in Japan for a long time, speak about the hardness of the rule, not about the exoticism of the subject. Kaga builds a tradition from it, and Final Fantasy Tactics will follow that tradition too. Without this Famicom army, the branch would not have its first law.

It is the first installment in the Fire Emblem series and was originally released in Japan. Set on the fictional continent of Archanea, the story follows the tale of Marth, prince of the kingdom of Altea, who is sent on a quest to reclaim his throne after being forced into exile by the evil sorcerer Gharnef and his dark master Medeus, the titular Shadow Dragon.

Forming new alliances with neighboring kingdoms, Marth must gather a new army to help him retrieve the sacred sword Falchion and the Fire Emblem shield in order to defeat Gharnef and Medeus and save his kingdom. While initial sales and critical reception were lackluster, it later became popular, launching the Fire Emblem series.

Fire Emblem: Shadow Dragon and the Blade of Light is a tactical role-playing game where players take on the role of Marth and his growing army during their campaign across the continent of Archanea. During the early story draft, there were two dragons that acted as bosses: the Earth Dragon Gaia and the Water Dragon Neptune.

A special edition Fire Emblem 30th Anniversary Edition bundle was available exclusively in North America, which includes a download code for the game as well as various physical items, including a replica NES Game Pak with its own packaging and instruction manual. According to Kaga, upon release Shadow Dragon and the Blade of Light received extensive criticism from Japanese publications.

Despite the slow start, Shadow Dragon and the Blade of Light proved enough of a success that a sequel was commissioned. Shadow Dragon and the Blade of Light was partially remade as part of the third installment, Fire Emblem: Mystery of the Emblem, which also continued the story of Marth.

A full remake for the Nintendo DS was released internationally under the title Fire Emblem: Shadow Dragon, marking the first time the content of the first Fire Emblem was made available outside Japan. Shadow Dragon and the Blade of Light not only launched the wider Fire Emblem series, but is also seen as the reason tactical role-playing genre became popular in Japan.

The basic mechanics within Shadow Dragon and the Blade of Light would form the basis for the gameplay of nearly all subsequent Fire Emblem titles.
