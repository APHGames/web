Stock cars race on colorful polygonal circuits. Model 2 brought extraordinarily smooth textured 3D and the networking of multiple cabinets.

In a stock car you drive a colorful circuit, and a hit into the wall is part of the theater, not the immediate end of the race. The controls have to hold even a player who stepped up to a coin. Several cabinets can be linked, so the opponent beside you is a person in another seat. An arcade network is still an experience in 1993, not a matter of course.

![Poster](/img/worthwhile-games/media/155-poster-1.jpg)

Yu Suzuki and AM2 built the game in 1993 on the Model 2 hardware. Toshihiro Nagoshi also took part in the design at the studio, and Daytona is their textured race in the year when Sega sells smooth 3D as an arcade experience. Model 2 can do smooth textures and a tempo that home machines that year cannot catch. The Saturn version will later show how large the gap was between the arcade and the living room.

The decision to build grip and slide on a banked oval has a consequence. The track is legible from far away, and a crash into the barrier is a spectacle that sells the next coin. In 1993 Sega and Namco open two schools of three-dimensional racing. Daytona bets on mass and color; beside it, Ridge Racer bets on the drift as the basic language.

There are few tracks and each is short. Arcade life is in repetition and in getting better on one circuit, not in a career across a season. What has aged are the low details of the landscape and the pop song, which is deliberately simple. The charm is of its period once you hear it outside the arcade.

What has not aged is the accessibility of speed. It is still clear where the track turns and why you are in the barrier, which many more serious simulations will burden with a menu. The Hornet and the rivals are silhouettes that stick in memory. AM2 designs the car as a mascot of the cabinet, not as a setup in a Papyrus garage.

Compare the controls with Indianapolis 500. Papyrus wants you to build the car; Suzuki wants you to know within a minute what the entry into the banking looks like. In a seminar, talk about linked cabinets as multiplayer before everyone had a modem. A shared track is a social design, not just a network protocol.

Daytona USA was developed by AM2 after a meeting of the heads of Sega's regional offices to decide on a game to debut the Model 2 hardware. The developers researched motorsports extensively; they mapped Daytona International Speedway, and their experience developing Virtua Racing (1992) helped with lighting and camera control.

Daytona USA became one of the highest-grossing arcade games of all time, and was a critical and commercial success, with its graphics, soundtrack and gameplay receiving high praise. Daytona USA 's arcade version on the Model 2 is capable of displaying up to 300,000 texture-mapped polygons per second, Visually, the game uses texture filtering, giving the visuals a smooth appearance.

]] Sega mandated that Daytona USA had to be better than Namco's 1993 racing game Ridge Racer and it had to achieve higher sales. Prior to release, Sega debuted a prototype of Daytona USA at the Amusement Machine Show in Tokyo in August 1993, and it was tested in select Japanese arcades the same month; Petit stated that this was done to measure how the games would be received by the public.

Due to slow progress on the Daytona USA conversion, several members of the Virtua Fighter 2 team were reassigned to Daytona USA. Developed by Sega's consumer software division, An enhanced arcade remake, called Sega Racing Classic, was released in 2010 and is the first title in the series not branded with the Daytona name as Sega no longer owned the rights at the time.

Another enhanced version was released digitally for the PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 in October 2011, titled Daytona USA. Daytona USA 2: Battle on the Edge, an arcade-exclusive sequel using the Sega Model 3 hardware, was released in 1998.

It is the only Daytona game that uses no courses or music from the original. Daytona Championship USA, also referred to as Daytona USA 3, debuted in late 2016 as an arcade exclusive; it was the first Daytona-branded arcade game in 18 years.
