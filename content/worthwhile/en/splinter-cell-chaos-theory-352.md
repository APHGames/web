Sam Fisher infiltrates international targets during an information crisis. Light, noise, multiple routes, and co-op campaigns represent one of the peaks of pure stealth level design.

In Chaos Theory, Sam Fisher is in shadow again, but this time the surface under his boot can give him away too. Light and noise are two meters, and both can be read before a guard sees you. The levels have more than one route, including a nonlethal one, and interrogating a guard is a way to get a code without lighting the alarm.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/352-cover-1.jpg)

Clint Hocking led it creatively at Ubisoft Montreal, and in 2005 it came out on several platforms, again with Michael Ironside. The engine is still an Unreal branch, but the rules are richer than in the first game. The consequence of the two meters is that a sprint in the dark can be as expensive as a light bulb. The player plans speed, not only the cone of light.

The cooperative campaign and the spies-versus-mercs mode are separate games in the box. At its peak the series can be a solo stealth game and a social sport at the same time. In 2005 it is widely taken as the peak of pure stealth, before Conviction pushes the series toward action-oriented target marking. Historically one goes to Chaos Theory as a norm, not as nostalgia.

The geopolitical thriller is period pulp. The level design of light, noise, and multiple routes has not aged, and it is still used to teach what an honest stealth map should look like. The bank, the lighthouse, and the other large levels are lessons in themselves. Each has a different ratio of silence and electronics, and therefore they cannot be played with one habit.

For students of Hocking's line it is the first of a pair with Far Cry 2. Here the system is in careful architecture. There the system will be in open friction. Both come from the same appetite not to leave the player in comfort. A nonlethal passage is not a moral sticker in a menu. It is a different economy of time, because a stunned guard can wake and a dead one cannot.

Ironside's voice holds Fisher as a tired craftsman. When a later game replaces him, the contract with the character changes too, not only the color of the dialogue. When you play it today, turn off the minimap if the level can bear it, and listen to the floor. The game was written for the ear as much as for the light bulb.
