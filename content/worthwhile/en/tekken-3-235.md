A new generation of fighters enters the King of Iron Fist Tournament. A faster sidestep, animation, and an extraordinarily high-quality PS1 adaptation made it one of the main 3D fighters of the decade.

You enter the King of Iron Fist Tournament with a new generation of fighters and with the chance to sidestep into the foreground, not only to block on a line. The sidestep makes the depth of the arena a rule. An attack that in a two-dimensional fighter would have to connect can be stepped around, and the step-around is immediately a punish.

![Poster](/img/worthwhile-games/media/235-poster-1.jpg)

Namco put it into arcades in 1997 on System 12, hardware related to the PlayStation. The consequence is a home port that is not a distant memory of the cabinet but almost the same game in the living room. The animation is a leap against the previous entries. The body carries information about a frame before you read a move list, and Eddy Gordo turns capoeira into a fashion even outside the arcade.

The decision to speed the game up and open a sideways dodge had a consequence for the 3D fighter. The Z axis stopped being a camera and became defense, which separated Tekken from the cleaner axis of Virtua Fighter and from the plane of Street Fighter. Jin Kazama takes the center of the series after Kazuya. The generational change is commercial and dramatic at once: a new body, a new rhythm, the same tournament.

In 1997 the 3D fighter is still an arcade contest. Tekken 3 wins that contest for a while because the home version, which arrives immediately after, does not drive the arcade players away. What has aged is the PlayStation resolution, on which the handsome animation breaks into pixels. What has not aged is the feel of a dash, a throw, and a sidestep that can be practiced even without a story.

In seminar, compare one sidestep with a block. Students will see that space is a rule only when you get a frame for it, not when the arena is nicely deep. The arcade origin means a short round and an immediate rematch. The home modes are an extension; the core is still a match on two coins.

Against Soulcalibur, which will arrive a year later with eight-way run, the sidestep in Tekken 3 is still narrow. Historically it is the sidestep that opened depth as a mandatory skill. The game holds the decade of the 3D fighter together with Virtua Fighter. Anyone who teaches only Street Fighter is teaching half the shop.
