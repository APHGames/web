A hero collects gold and digs into floors in order to escape the guards. A built-in level editor and the combinatorial logic of the environment substantially extended the life of the game.

Lode Runner collects gold in the shafts and digs holes in the floors so the guards will fall, then has to escape before the hole closes again and the guard climbs out. The enemy is not a target to be shot. It is a piece that can climb and that chases you by a simple rule, so a trap is smarter than contact.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/044-cover-1.jpg)

Doug Smith began it as a student game, and Broderbund published it in 1983 on the Apple II, together with a level editor that is not an accessory but part of the product. The picture is a brick grid. A fall, a ladder, and a hole are physics made of few states, and for that very reason they can be composed into hundreds of screens.

The decision to include an editor had the consequence that the game does not end with the author's catalogue. It ends at the moment the player stops making new traps for a familiar rule. The hundred and fifty supplied levels are a textbook: first one hole, then a chain, then a situation that wants you to dig at the wrong time on purpose.

In 1983 Mario Bros. stands beside it as an arcade clearing of a room. Lode Runner is domestic, quieter, and more systematic: the same pleasure in one screen, but with a pencil for new maps. Smith's path from a school project into a Broderbund box is typical of eight-bit America, where one clever mechanic is enough for a company.

What has aged is the severity of a pixel-perfect fall and levels that are a test of patience more than a readable puzzle. What has not aged is digging as a verb that changes the map for a few seconds, and an enemy that can use that change against you.

A direct line runs from the editor in the box to the tools with which players still write levels in place of the authors. In a seminar, have the students build one screen, not just clear one. The design is understood by the person who has to make a guard fall somewhere on purpose.
