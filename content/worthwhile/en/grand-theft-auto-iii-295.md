The criminal Claude rises through the underworld of Liberty City. Transferring the GTA sandbox into a continuous 3D city created the dominant template of modern open-world action.

Claude, who does not speak, does jobs for the mob in Liberty City, and between them the city stays switched on: a radio, a pedestrian, the police, an ambulance, and a mission you refused will wait for you in the district. Three islands open with progress, and still it is one city, not a chapter with loading between corridors. The drive from mission to mission is a part players remember as much as the goal.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/295-cover-1.jpg)

DMA Design, already as Rockstar North, released it in October 2001 on the PlayStation 2. Leslie Benzies produced it, and at Rockstar Dan and Sam Houser hold it dramatically; it is the transfer of the original sandbox into a continuous 3D. The decision to let the city's systems run underneath a criminal career had a consequence that becomes a template. Opening the map, climbing an organization, listening to the radio, and getting chased is for a decade an almost mandatory sentence of open action.

In 2001, beside Halo, this is the second contract about what the new generation of consoles can do. One stabilizes war in the first person, the other gives the third person a whole city and an adult, crude theme without an apology. What has aged is the handling of the car, the silent Claude as a character, and the missions that after death send you far back. What has not aged is the radio as the voice of the city and the wanted level as an enemy that grows from your driving, not from a cutscene.

In seminar, let students drive to a meeting by a detour. If the detour is more fun than the meeting, they see the design; if it is only a delay, they see where the sandbox does not yet have density. Against the first GTA there is less of a board here and more of a body. The contract that a mission can be abandoned remains, and it receives a street in which you can get lost.

Against Driver there is crime as a system here, not as a film job in a garage. Rockstar takes the city that Reflections opened to the camera and puts the ruthlessness of Dundee back into it. The protagonist's silence is both a saving and a choice. The city speaks for him through the radio, and that is why Claude is empty, which later entries will repair with a voice, and here it does not yet matter as much as the structure.

The game is the dominant template not because it was the first open one. It is the template because 3D, the radio, and a career sat in one continuous city in the year the hardware could finally hold it. Anyone who teaches only the missions teaches half. The other half is what happens when you do not finish the mission and the city continues anyway.
