Cybernetic agents carry out violent missions in dystopian cities. Real-time control of a small group, body upgrades, and a destructive city sandbox foreshadowed squad tactics.

In a dystopian city you lead a team of four agents, buy them better bodies from research, and complete missions among civilians who are not just scenery. The Persuadatron and the weapons are tools of the same ethics. You can turn someone, or remove them, and the city remembers both as your trace.

Bullfrog released the game in 1993 through Electronic Arts on the Amiga and DOS. After Populous and before Theme Park, this is their dark real-time tactic, still from a small British studio. The engine is isometric and urban. Streets, elevators, and the crowd are the map, not a pre-rendered background, so a shot has somewhere to land.

The decision to control a small group instead of an army has a consequence. Each agent is an investment in body upgrades, and losing one hurts more than a line tank in Dune. In 1993 real-time strategy is only just codifying bases. Syndicate goes somewhere else: a mission, a squad, and a city that can be used as a weapon.

Research between missions is the campaign. A better leg or a better weapon is progress, so the economy is not harvesting on the map; it is a laboratory. What has aged is the control of the group. The mouse and the commands can be opaque, and some missions are a test of what the game wanted, not of what it said.

What has not aged is the dirt of the tone. You can still feel that success is a corporate crime and that a civilian is part of the tactic. Four silhouettes at the start of a mission are your whole army. With that, Bullfrog anticipates tactical games that do not want to be only about producing dozens of pieces.

Compare the god hand from Populous with this one. There you are a distant deity; here you are a manager sending concrete bodies into a concrete street. In a seminar, ask who in the city is not a target. If the students do not know, the design is working: the city is both a weapon and an obstacle.

Syndicate is an isometric real-time tactics and strategic game from Bullfrog Productions created in 1993, and initially released for MS-DOS and the Amiga. Set in a dystopian future in which corporations have replaced governments, Syndicate puts the player in control of a corporation vying for global dominance.

An expansion pack, Syndicate: American Revolt, a sequel, Syndicate Wars, and a reboot Syndicate have also been released. The original game and expansion pack were re-released together in 1996 as Syndicate Plus.

Gameplay of Syndicate involves ordering a one to four-person team of cyborg agents around cities displayed in a fixed-view isometric style, in pursuit of mission goals such as assassinating executives of a rival syndicate, rescuing captured allies, "persuading" civilians and scientists to join the player's company or killing all enemy agents. The backstory of Syndicate is contained in the manual, instead of the game itself.

During the game, the player establishes worldwide dominance with their established syndicate, one territory at a time, while engaging and eliminating rival syndicates (such as The Tao, Sphinx Inc., and The Castrilos) and putting down internal mutinies. In January 2012, the MS-DOS version of Syndicate was re-released, packaged with pre-configured versions of the DOSBox emulator for modern versions of Windows.

In 2015, Syndicate was available for free on Electronic Arts' Origin platform. An expansion pack named, Syndicate: American Revolt, was subsequently released in 1993.
