Edge travels on a dragon through a disintegrating world. Battles that rotate around the opponent carried the spatial system of a rail shooter into an unusual RPG.

Edge flies a dragon among ruins, and combat is not a menu in an arena. You orbit a creature, look for the zone you are allowed to shoot, and the dragon changes according to how you lead it. Four discs on the Saturn in 1998 are a production bet on a platform that is already losing the market. The print run was limited, and the game became rare before it had time to become ordinary.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/254-screenshot-1.jpg)

Team Andromeda and Yukio Futatsugi build it as an RPG that remembers the rail shooter. The aiming circle from Panzer Dragoon Zwei becomes a tactical space here, and the rail disappears into travel on the map. The decision to turn combat around the enemy had a consequence for a genre that did not spread. The battle is a spatial puzzle and still turn-based, so the studio invented a tool almost nobody inherited after it.

In 1998 the Dreamcast comes out in Japan and the Saturn is finishing. Saga is a farewell: a large game on a machine the market is leaving, and that is why it is also taught as a publisher's decision, not only as a system. The world is melancholy and fragmentary, in the tradition of the series. Towns are not a checklist of services, and the journey is all the more a feeling of disintegration, which the title carries literally.

What has aged is Saturn 3D if you judge it beside the Dreamcast a year later. What has not aged is the orbital combat and the dragon as a partner whose form is the build. In seminar, put a screenshot of the rail shooter beside a diagram of a battle in Saga. Students will see the inheritance of the circle without a lecture on the IP.

The rarity of the physical copy is not a quality of the design. It is the condition under which the reputation separated from ordinary play, and ports are only now leveling that. Against Final Fantasy VII there is less industry here and more of one authorial team on a departing console. Both are RPGs of the period, and each teaches a different ambition.

Losing the dragon would be losing the system, not only a cutscene. The design knows that, and that is why the relationship is mechanical. The game is proof that a linear series can change genre in its last entry and still remain itself, if it keeps the gesture of aiming.

Panzer Dragoon Saga, known in Japan as Azel: Panzer Dragoon RPG, is a 1998 role-playing video game developed by Team Andromeda and published by Sega for the Sega Saturn. It replaces the rail shooter gameplay of previous Panzer Dragoon games with RPG elements such as random encounters, semi-turn-based battles and free-roaming exploration.

Development began in early 1995 alongside Panzer Dragoon II&nbsp;Zwei (1996). The project was arduous and repeatedly delayed; incorporating the Panzer Dragoon shooting elements with full 3D computer graphics and voice acting, both unusual features in RPGs at the time, pushed the Saturn to its technical limits and strained team relations.

Sega released Panzer Dragoon Saga in Japan in January 1998 and in the West in April. Saga received acclaim, with praise for its story, graphics, and combat; it was the highest-rated Saturn game on the aggregator GameRankings.

Several staff members joined a new Sega studio, Smilebit, and developed a fourth Panzer Dragoon game, Panzer Dragoon Orta (2002), for the Xbox. Unlike the other Panzer Dragoon games, which are rail shooters, Panzer Dragoon Saga is a role-playing video game (RPG).

Boosting one attribute diminishes another; for example, boosting the attack attribute reduces the spiritual attribute, which increases the cost of berserks, Sega's Team Andromeda studio split into two teams: one worked on Saga while the other developed a shooter sequel, Panzer Dragoon II&nbsp;Zwei. European and North American releases followed on 17 April and 5 May, making Saga one of the last Saturn games published by Sega outside Japan.

Sega planned for Panzer Dragoon Saga to compete with the PlayStation RPG Final Fantasy VII, believing that having the best RPG would win the console war, and aimed to sell 1.5 million copies. Electronic Gaming Monthly wrote that whereas other RPGs had players methodically selecting menu options, Panzer Dragoon Saga s system forced them to account for time expenditures and positioning, producing "much more exciting" battles.

GamePro said it solidified Panzer Dragoon as the Saturn's "coolest franchise" and was a satisfying completion of the series. In 2007, Game Informer called Panzer Dragoon Saga one of the greatest games and praised the use of dramatic camera angles and animations to make the battles cinematic "even by modern standards".

In 2008, IGN named it the greatest Saturn game and wrote: "Panzer Dragoon Saga was the swan song to end all swan songs ... It is the gem of the generation." In its 2009 list of games with the greatest gameplay, IGN named Panzer Dragoon Saga the "best game no one played".

Sega disbanded Team Andromeda after Panzer Dragoon Saga 's completion. Given the opportunity to remake Panzer Dragoon Saga, Futatsugi said he would make it less linear, with an open world, branching choices and online communication.
