Heroes pursue the Dark Wanderer across several continents. Skill trees, loot rarity, and long-term online build-crafting created the ARPG standard for the following decade.

You click a hero across several acts after the Dark Wanderer, and the real game often begins only once you know the campaign and go again for a rare item on a higher difficulty. The skill tree is a commitment. In the original game points come back poorly, and the character is therefore a build you live with, not a costume you switch in the evening.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/283-cover-1.jpg)

Blizzard North, again Brevik and the Schaefer brothers, released it in 2000 on Windows and Mac. Battle.net is the place where an item is traded and where the fragility of an online economy also shows, including item duplication, which there is no point in romanticizing. The decision to tie loot to rarity and to the difficulties Normal, Nightmare, and Hell had a consequence for a decade of action RPGs. The campaign is the textbook, and the hunt for a unique is the reason a character stays on a ladder long after the credits.

In 2000, beside Deus Ex, which wants a unique path, Diablo II wants a repeatable one. Both are at the top of the PC year, and each answers differently the question of whether a game should be thrown away after you finish it. The expansion Lord of Destruction a year later adds an act and further systems. Teach it as a continuation of the contract, not as a repair of a game that would not exist without it; the trees and the rarity already hold in the base.

What has aged is the Tetris-like packing of the inventory and the run back to town. What has not aged is the rhythm of the click and the loot drop, and the class fantasy by which players still name their builds. In seminar, open one tree and one unique item. Students will see that progress is in two currencies, the point and the item, and that an item can rewrite the rule of the character.

Against the first Diablo there is less randomness of the dungeon here and more authorship of the acts. The generator remains in the maps and in the loot, and the campaign gets a spine the first game did not have. The online game is a social machine and a security problem. A seminar should say both: the community arose because an item has value, and the cheat arose for the same reason.

The skill tree is a pedagogical tree. You read it as a map of a future character, and that is why it can be taught even on paper, without killing a single demon. The game sets the action-RPG standard for ten years. The standard is not the clicking itself; it is the combination of rarity, difficulty, and a character you are not allowed to rebuild without a cost.
