Link explores almost all of Hyrule without a firmly prescribed route. Climbing, the glider, physics, chemical elements, and geographic visibility replace a large part of traditional quest-marker design.

Link in Breath of the Wild, after a short plateau, can go almost anywhere in Hyrule, and the player climbs while stamina lasts, glides from a tower, and decides whether the rain has just forbidden the path they saw from the hill. The Sheikah runes, magnesis, stasis, ice, and bombs, are physical verbs. Fire, metal, and wind are not quest items; they are behaviors of the world that apply outside a shrine as well.

Nintendo EPD under director Hidemaro Fujibayashi and producer Eiji Aonuma released the game in March 2017 on the same day on Wii U and on the new Switch. It is a deliberate break with the linear temples of the previous 3D Zelda games. The chemistry and the physics have to be reliable enough that the player can invent a solution the designer did not film. That is the technical promise of an open Hyrule: a system instead of one correct cutscene of a lever.

The decision to hide the traditional dungeon inside four Divine Beasts and many short shrines means freedom right after the tutorial. The consequence is a dizzying orientation by landscape, and also an argument about whether the series misses large temples. Breakable weapons force you to treat a sword as a consumable. The studio thereby stops the player from finding one eternal answer after an hour and ceasing to read what is lying on the ground.

In 2017 the game rewrites the expectation of an open world even for studios that make shooters and RPGs. Towers reveal the map, but the real guidance is a hill, smoke, and curiosity. Cooking and weather make survival a soft layer. You are not in a hard hunger simulation; you are in a game that wants the rain to be an event, not a filter on the camera.

Weapon breaking has aged for some players, as has the similarity of the four Divine Beasts. Some shrines are also by then only a test of one rune, not a place. The plateau holds as a tutorial that lets you go, and so does the feeling that a forbidden shortcut often works. That is a contract many games have cited since and few have kept.

The horse, the towers, and the glider are three speeds of reading the map. On foot you see detail, from a tower a plan, on horseback a compromise. In class, compare it with an open world full of icons. Ask which information the terrain carries and which another game would write onto the minimap.

Set near the end of the Zelda timeline, it follows Link as he sets out to save Princess Zelda and prevent Calamity Ganon from destroying the world. Breath of the Wild world is unstructured and encourages exploration and experimentation; the story can be completed in a nonlinear fashion.

The five-year development commenced after the release of The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword (2011). Led by the director Hidemaro Fujibayashi and the producer Eiji Aonuma, EPD sought to rethink Zelda conventions and introduced elements such as detailed chemistry and physics engines.

Breath of the Wild was released on March 3, 2017, as the final Nintendo-published Wii U game and as a launch game for the Switch. Breath of the Wild won numerous year-end accolades, including Game of the Year at the 2017 Game Awards and the 21st Annual D.I.C.E.

It broke sales records for a Nintendo launch game and sold 35.76 million copies by June 2026, making it the bestselling Zelda game and one of the bestselling video games. Breath of the Wild is considered one of the greatest video games of all time; journalists described it as a landmark in open world design for its emphasis on experimentation, physics-based sandbox, and emergent gameplay.

Numerous developers cited Breath of the Wild as inspiration, and it is a popular point of comparison among open-world games. A spinoff, Hyrule Warriors: Age of Calamity, was released in 2020, and a sequel, The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom, was released in 2023.

Breath of the Wild encourages nonlinear gameplay, which is illustrated by the lack of defined entrances or exits to areas, scant instruction given to the player, and encouragement to explore freely. Breath of the Wild introduces a consistent physics engine to the Zelda series, letting players approach problems in different ways rather than trying to find a single solution.

Additionally, players can scan Amiibo figures with their controller to summon items or call Link's horse Epona from previous Zelda games and Wolf Link from The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess. Breath of the Wild takes place at the end of the Zelda timeline in the kingdom of Hyrule.

During this time, Princess Zelda trained vigorously to awaken the sealing magic needed to defeat Ganon while trying to maintain her personal research into the Divine Beasts. After being sought out by Zelda, the Champions—Mipha, princess of the aquatic Zora; Revali, archer of the birdlike Rito; Daruk, warrior of the mountainous Gorons; and Urbosa, chief of the desert-dwelling Gerudo—assembled to pilot the Divine Beasts while Zelda and Link would face Ganon directly.

When Ganon appeared on Zelda's seventeenth birthday, he possessed the Guardians and Divine Beasts, turning them against Hyrule. King Rhoam and the Champions were killed, and Link was gravely wounded protecting Zelda.

Zelda had Link taken to the Shrine of Resurrection for him to heal, hid the Master Sword in Korok Forest under the protection of the Great Deku Tree, and used her light magic to seal herself and Ganon in Hyrule Castle. Zelda gives Link the Bow of Light, which allows him to weaken Ganon enough for Zelda to seal him away, restoring peace and allowing the spirits of King Rhoam and the Champions to depart to the afterlife.

If the player completes an optional quest that involves Link regaining all of his memories, they unlock a post-credits cutscene in which Zelda declares that Hyrule must be rebuilt and that she and Link must begin the process by themselves. As Link and Zelda survey Hyrule, the princess confides in him that although her powers may have dwindled over time, she has come to terms with it.

According to series producer Eiji Aonuma, the development team aimed to "rethink the conventions of Zelda". Development started immediately upon the release of The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword (2011).

In 2013, Nintendo experimented with nonlinear gameplay in The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds, which was a common point of praise from many outlets. As Nintendo had never developed an open-world game on the scale of Breath of the Wild, they looked at The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim and Shadow of the Colossus, the latter of which has similar climbing mechanics and whose creator, Fumito Ueda, was friends with Aonuma.

Before full development, the developers designed a playable 2D prototype similar to the original Zelda to experiment with physics-based puzzles. At the 2017 Game Developers Conference, director Hidemaro Fujibayashi, technical director Takuhiro Dohta, and art director Satoru Takizawa held a presentation titled "Change and Constant – Breaking Conventions with The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild", during which they demoed the prototype.

Aonuma called the physics engine in Breath of the Wild a major development for the Zelda series, saying that it "underpins everything in the world" and makes things operate in a "logical and realistic way", allowing players to approach puzzles and problems in different ways. As previous Zelda games increased the amount of actions Link could perform, the development team realized that having too many actions would complicate the controls, so instead of adding more actions, they increased the number of events the player could interact with in the world.

Breath of the Wild was built and demonstrated with touchscreen features for the Wii U, but the developers found that "looking back and forth between the GamePad and the screen" was a distraction. Kataoka and Wakai had previously worked on the Zelda games Spirit Tracks and The Wind Waker respectively.

The soundtrack was primarily written for and performed on piano, with a focus on ambient music and sounds rather than melodic and upbeat music as seen in previous Zelda games. In the same year, Nintendo let attendees play the Wii U version at the E3 convention held in June, where they also announced its subtitle, Breath of the Wild.

Breath of the Wild launched for both the Wii U and Switch on March 3, 2017. In October 2025, it was announced that the soundtrack to Breath of the Wild would release as a 16 side LP boxset, alongside a cut-down 4 side vinyl set.

An enhanced edition was released as a launch game for the Nintendo Switch 2 on June 5, 2025 alongside the sequel, The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom. The "Switch 2 Edition" features improved resolution, higher framerate, HDR support, and integration with the mobile Nintendo Switch Online App, enabling the "Zelda Notes" feature that includes a full overworld map, real-time voiced navigation for collectibles and secrets, and narration on Hyrule's characteristics by Zelda herself.

New items include the Korok Mask, which alerts the player when a Korok is nearby, and other themed cosmetics related to previous Zelda games. Breath of the Wild received "universal acclaim", according to the review aggregator website Metacritic.

Breath of the Wild broke sales records for a Nintendo launch game in multiple regions. In the United States, Breath of the Wild was the second-bestselling video game during its month of release behind Tom Clancy's Ghost Recon Wildlands.

Nintendo reported that Breath of the Wild sold more than one million copies in the US that month—925,000 of which were for Switch, outselling the Switch itself. Breath of the Wild had sold 34.06 million copies on the Switch by June 2026 and 1.70 million copies on the Wii U by December 2020.

Following its demonstration at E3 2016, Breath of the Wild received several accolades from the Game Critics Awards and won the award for Most Anticipated Game at the Game Awards 2016. Breath of the Wild won multiple awards at the Game Awards 2017, including Game of the Year, Best Game Direction, and Best Action/Adventure Game, and was nominated for Best Art Direction, Best Score/Music, and Best Audio Design.

GameSpot, Polygon, Entertainment Weekly, Eurogamer, Electronic Gaming Monthly, GamesRadar+, and Game Informer ranked Breath of the Wild the best game of 2017. According to Metacritic, Breath of the Wild topped 18 lists of the decade's best games, more than any other game of the 2010s.

In a 2021 conducted by TV Asahi of more than 50,000 Japanese people, Breath of the Wild was voted the best console game, above Dragon Quest V. A 2023 poll conducted by GQ which surveyed a team of journalists across the industry ranked Breath of the Wild as the best video game.

Shortly after Breath of the Wild s release, journalists and video game industry figures discussed how it would influence the Zelda series, and future open-world games. Damien Monnier, designer of The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, said Breath of the Wild managed to bring classic open world mechanics together while not relying on them to guide the player through its world, encouraging players to explore.

Multiple games and developers have cited Breath of the Wild as an inspiration, including Genshin Impact, Ghost of Tsushima, Eternal Strands, Immortals Fenyx Rising, Telling Lies, Halo Infinite, Elden Ring, Forspoken, Final Fantasy VII Rebirth, and Clair Obscur: Expedition 33. Similarities have also been noted between Breath of the Wild and other open-world games since its launch, including Pokémon Legends: Arceus, Sonic Frontiers and Horizon Forbidden West.

According to Digital Trends, Breath of the Wild has become a popular point of comparison among open world games. Hyrule Warriors: Age of Calamity, a hack and slash game in the style of Dynasty Warriors series and the successor to 2014's Hyrule Warriors, is a spin-off of Breath of the Wild, with the story set 100 years before.

A sequel, The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom, was released on May 12, 2023. The game was conceived during planning for Breath of the Wild s DLC; the team came up with too many ideas, some of which could not be implemented due to technical constraints, and they were used for a new game.
