Sam Fisher carries out secret infiltration operations. Dynamic light and visibility were the technical and mechanical center of the stealth.

Sam Fisher, voiced by Michael Ironside, breaks into sites where light gives him away sooner than the noise of a shot. The visibility meter ties detection to the shadow you stand in and to how fast you move. Darkness is ammunition, not scenery.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/311-screenshot-1.jpg)

Ubisoft Montreal built it in 2002 on Unreal Engine 2 and released it first on the Xbox. The Tom Clancy license holds a thriller frame, but the rules belong to the lights. Guards talk to each other and you eavesdrop with an optic cable or from shadow. Information is a reward for not shooting.

Three degrees of suspicion give the player time to retreat. Failure need not be an immediate restart if you manage to leave the cone of light. In 2002, console stealth games had their models in Metal Gear and Tenchu. Splinter Cell moved the center of gravity from the radar to the bulb above you.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/311-screenshot-2.jpg)

Some rooms are a trial-and-error test and the camera in narrow corridors has aged. The relationship of light and silhouette has not, and Chaos Theory will later extend it with noise. Night and thermal vision are not just filters. They change which information you get about a guard at all, and therefore the plan of a route.

For stealth students it is a case where the technology of the dynamic shadow is at the same time the rule of the game. Fisher's body is slow and heavy on purpose. An action hero would break the contract the game made with the dark.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/311-cover-3.jpg)

Later entries push the series toward a faster pace. The first is still the one where you learn that a glint gives you away. When you play it today, count on strictness. That strictness is what made light the main character of 2002.
