Dungeon Keeper is a strategy video game developed by Bullfrog Productions and released by Electronic Arts on 27 June 1997 for MS-DOS and Windows 95. In Dungeon Keeper, the player builds and manages a dungeon, protecting it from invading "hero" characters intent on stealing its accumulated treasures, killing monsters and ultimately the player's demise.

The ultimate goal is to conquer the world by destroying the heroic forces and rival dungeon keepers in each realm. Dungeon Keeper uses Creative Technology's SoundFont technology to enhance its atmosphere.

Dungeon Keeper took over two years to develop, and an expansion pack, a Direct3D version, and a level editor were released. Dungeon Keeper was re-released on GOG.com in 2011, Origin in 2016, and Steam in 2024.

Dungeon Keeper was followed by a sequel, Dungeon Keeper 2, in 1999, and influenced games such as Lego Rock Raiders and Ghost Master. The player constructs and manages a dungeon, recruiting and catering for minions to run it and defend it from enemy invaders.

The primary method of control is the hand, used to pick up creatures and objects in the dungeon, carry them around, and drop them. Throughout the game, a "mentor" will advise the player as to various happenings and problems within the dungeon, as a sinister voiceover.

The dungeon has a fleshed-out ecology: certain creatures are natural enemies. Creatures will frequently enter combat with heroes or creatures belonging to another keeper.

Each creature has a star of the colour of the keeper it belongs to above it, displaying its experience level. The player has the ability to possess a creature, seeing the dungeon from its first-person perspective and using its attacks and abilities.

The dungeon can be protected from being breached by having the imps fortify the walls. Programmers Simon and Dene Carter also said Dungeon Keeper is a parody.

Dungeon Keeper uses lighting algorithms used in Magic Carpet, which provided effects such as fireballs lighting corridors. According to Molyneux, in May 1995 publisher Electronic Arts told him he had to ship Dungeon Keeper in six and a half weeks.

Dungeon Keeper was shown at the European Computer Trade Show in September 1996, Sega Saturn and Sony PlayStation versions were in development and due for release in 1997, but cancelled. A sequel, Dungeon Keeper 2 was released in 1999, and the series was rebooted by Mythic Entertainment with the release of a remake (also titled Dungeon Keeper) for iOS and Android on 30 January 2014.
