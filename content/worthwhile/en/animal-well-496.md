A nameless character explores a compact animal labyrinth full of hidden layers. Items have many non-explicit uses, and the puzzles continue far beyond an ordinary "completion"; community knowledge becomes another layer of the map.

In Animal Well you walk a dense animal labyrinth, and the objects you pick up are not keys to one door. A disc, a bubble, a yo-yo, or a firecracker have physical behavior, and the player discovers their second and third meaning long after the game looks finished. The map is small and deep. No combat is the center; the center is the suspicion that a wall you passed was a mechanism.

Billy Basso under the name Shared Memory made the game for years essentially alone, and Bigmode released it in May 2024. It is a metroidvania that counts on the community after the credits to keep drawing layers one person is not meant to find all of. The decision not to write item uses into a tutorial means a secret is structure, not a reward for one hundred percent. The consequence is a game whose outer layer lives on forums as a shared detective story, while the first playthrough still makes sense on its own.

Pixel density hides information in the decoration. The studio bets on a player who will stop, which is the opposite contract from games that keep pointing at the next objective. The animals are both inhabitants and puzzles. You are not in a kingdom with lore dialogue; you are in a machine that looks like a night forest.

In 2024 this is an argument that a metroidvania need not grow to the length of Hollow Knight if it grows into the depth of one square meter. Beside the big maps it is compact and maliciously quiet. Completion in the ordinary sense is only a threshold. Anyone who leaves at the credits has seen the levels; anyone who stays sees that the levels were a surface.

Only the fact that some outer secrets are already publicly solved has aged. The inner playthrough and the behavior of the items still belong to you, if you do not have them explained beforehand. Respect for the player who notices holds. It is still the best contemporary example of how to design an object with several truths and not announce the second of them with an arrow.

The solo production shows in the density. There is no filler zone for length; there is a place someone drew until every tile could bear something. In class, split it into the playthrough and the layer after the credits. The first is a game about movement, the second is a game about collective attention, and both are designed.

Animal Well is a 2024 Metroidvania puzzle-platformer video game developed by Billy Basso as Shared Memory and published by Bigmode. The player controls an unnamed blob creature and explores an underground animal-filled labyrinth which incorporates nonlinear platforming and puzzle solving.

Billy Basso developed Animal Well, his first solo work, over the course of seven years. The design aesthetics were inspired by gardens and urban areas around Chicago, as well as his own artwork.

Basso developed the entire game himself, including the underlying engine; he partnered with Dan Adelman after four years of development to handle marketing, and partnered with Bigmode a year later as their first published game, after YouTuber Jason Gastrow (videogamedunkey) saw Animal Well at a festival. Animal Well was released for Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 5, and Windows in May 2024, and for Xbox Series X/S in October.
