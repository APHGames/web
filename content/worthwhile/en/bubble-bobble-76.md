Two little dragons catch enemies in bubbles on single-screen levels. Cooperation, the chaining of bonuses, and surprisingly deep rules of secrets make it a model of the systemic arcade.

Two little dragons, Bub and Bob, jump around a single screen, trap enemies in bubbles, and then ram them so that fruit falls out. Two players are not just double the shooting. One player's bubble carries the other, a chain of hits multiplies the bonus, and cooperation is mathematically more profitable than solo play.

Fukio Mitsuji designed it for Taito, and the machine was released in 1986. The hundred screens are small, but the rules of the fruit, of the enemies' anger, and of the secret endings are deeper than the cabinet promises at first glance. One room saves hardware and at the same time forces you to read the whole screen like a board game. There is nowhere to scroll away; all the danger is present.

The decision that a kill by bubble could start a chain had the consequence that greed is a strategy. Whoever pops them one by one survives; whoever waits gets better food and a higher score. If you hesitate, the enemies turn red and speed up. Time is not just a level limit, it is a mood of the system that punishes you for wavering.

In 1986 Out Run is selling speed and luxury beside it. Bubble Bobble sells a shared room and a joke, and yet it stays in the arcade as a game people return to for the rules, not for a new car. The true ending does not arrive automatically at the desk after the last screen. The game hides the conditions, and thereby turns an arcade game into a secret the venue passes along.

What has aged is the part of the secrets that, without a hint from a magazine, feels like arbitrariness, and the simplicity of the later rounds once you know the chain. What has not aged is the bubble as a temporary prison: the enemy is briefly an object, and you decide when to turn it into points.

From Bub and Bob on, we measure how deep a game can be that fits into one unscrolled room. In a seminar, play with two people and count the chain. A solo death on the same screen is a worse account than a shared bonus.

The first entry in the series, Bubble Bobble, was released in 1986 as an arcade cabinet. This was followed by many sequels in both the main Bubble Bobble series, as well as the spin-off Rainbow Islands and Puzzle Bobble series.

The most recent release in the series is Bubble Bobble Sugar Dungeons in 2025. The first game in the Bubble Bobble series is the platform arcade game Bubble Bobble, originally released on June 16, 1986 in Japan.

Bubble Bobble was ported to many home video game consoles, including the Amstrad CPC, Atari ST, ZX Spectrum, Commodore 64, MS-DOS, Apple II, Amiga, Famicom Disk System, Nintendo Entertainment System, MSX2, and Master System. The Master System port featured 200 levels and was released as Final Bubble Bobble in Japan.

Ports to the Game Boy in 1991 and the Game Boy Color in 1996 were released as Classic Bubble Bobble. Bubble Bobble is considered by many sources to be among the greatest video games of all time.

Bubble Bobble Part 2 is a sequel to Bubble Bobble which had simultaneous development by different teams on the Nintendo Entertainment System and the Game Boy. The players can now charge the bubble, and if released when the dragon's horns or bow is glowing, he/she will breathe three bubbles at once, with the specific formation dependent on the character chosen.

Graphically this game is very similar to Bubble Symphony, with the addition of using photographs of animals and plants as the level backgrounds. It also includes a gameplay feature similar to Bubble Symphony's charged bubbles, in this game called Super Bubbles.

By holding down the bubble button, the player can produce a giant bubble that can trap multiple enemies or one large enemy. Bubble Bobble Evolution (known in Japan as Bubble Bobble: Magical Tower Daisakusen!!) was released to PlayStation Portable in 2006.

The gameplay returns to a style similar to the original Bubble Bobble, with only a few twists. Each dragon blows a bubble matching their color and bubbles of a certain color are required to capture and kill certain enemies.
