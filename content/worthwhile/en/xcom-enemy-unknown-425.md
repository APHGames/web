XCOM defends Earth in turn-based missions and manages a global base. It reduced the original complexity to two actions, a cover system, and readable probabilities, without losing permanent losses.

In XCOM: Enemy Unknown you run the resistance to an invasion from a geoscape map and then drop into a turn-based mission where each soldier has two actions and cover decides whether the percentage on the screen means a life. The player names the units, equips them, and watches the odds on a shot. Permanent death means a veteran you carried for a month can end on one bad piece of cover.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/425-cover-1.jpg)

Firaxis and designer Jake Solomon released the game in October 2012 through 2K as a new reading of Julian Gollop's X-COM from 1994. Unreal Engine 3 carries a readable diorama of a city, not the old freedom of an endless inventory. Two actions, cover, and a visible probability are a deliberate narrowing. The original game had more complex action points and economy; this one wants the stress to fit into a single turn that a console player can also understand.

The consequence is drama from a small number of choices. Moving or shooting is a sentence, and a percentage that fails at ninety-five becomes a story even though the math was honest. Country panic on the geoscape forces you to play the campaign as triage. You cannot save everyone, and the world map is the account of what you left burning.

In 2012 the tactical-combat genre is asleep in the big market. This game wakes it in a form that can be streamed and explained to a viewer during a single turn. The base as an ant-farm cross-section makes research and workshops a second game between missions. The interceptor and the laboratory are as tense as a firefight in the street.

Some of the maps have aged, and they repeat, as has the feeling that the enemy later only multiplies hit points. The probability interface also still tempts an argument with the generator, even though it is not lying. Permanent losses and cover as a language hold. Enemy Within in 2013 adds gene mods and a new enemy, but the contract of two actions remains.

Soldier classes are readable silhouettes of roles. A sniper who must stand still is a design sentence about power having a position. In class, use it for probability: the game shows you a number, lets you disobey it, and then takes your favorite soldier. That is a more honest lesson than a hidden die.

XCOM: Enemy Unknown is a 2012 turn-based tactics video game developed by Firaxis Games and published by 2K. It is a "reimagining" of the 1994 strategy game UFO: Enemy Unknown (also known as X-COM: UFO Defense).

XCOM: Enemy Unknown is set during an alien invasion of Earth in an alternative version of 2015; the player controls an elite, multinational, paramilitary organization called XCOM and commands troops in a series of turn-based tactical missions. Between missions, the player directs the research and development of technologies from recovered alien technology and captured prisoners, expands XCOM's base of operations, manages finances, and monitors and responds to alien activity.

Aliens from the original game return in Enemy Unknown, though Firaxis redesigned many of them. While the game uses procedural generation for enemy placement, all maps in the game were handcrafted by Firaxis.

XCOM: Enemy Unknown was announced in January 2012, and was released for Microsoft Windows, PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 in October 2012. Firaxis released an expansion pack called XCOM: Enemy Within in November 2013 and a sequel called XCOM 2 in 2016.

XCOM: Enemy Unknown was often credited for renewing interest in the turn-based-tactics genre in the 2010s. XCOM: Enemy Unknown casts the player as the commander of an elite military organization who directs soldiers in turn-based combat missions against invading alien enemies.

Some soldiers may have access to psychic powers, allowing them to boost their team's willpower, reduce the enemy's combat efficiency, and briefly take control of enemy units. XCOM's underground headquarters is presented in a view dubbed the "ant farm".

Some resources can be sold to other XCOM nations through the gray market. Which situations the player decides to respond to influences the panic level of XCOM's member nations: responding in an area decreases panic while ignoring an area causes a rise in panic and potential for the nation to pull out of XCOM.

XCOM: Enemy Unknown was developed independently of 2K Marin's XCOM, which was later rebranded as The Bureau: XCOM Declassified, and although the two games are set in different universes, the games' developers were in contact with one another. Enemy Unknown was the first title Firaxis Games developed that did not feature the name of Sid Meier, who was not directly involved in the game's development.

Being a fan of UFO: Enemy Unknown (1994), director Jake Solomon had repeatedly petitioned Firaxis' leaders to create a sequel since he joined the studio. In 2003, Solomon was given six months to lead a team and develop a prototype for an XCOM game.

In early prototypes, Solomon envisioned the game as a remake of the first game in the XCOM franchise. Despite restarting development, the team did not want to completely overhaul UFO: Enemy Unknown and instead intended to modernize the gameplay experience.

Solomon said XCOM is "an unfeeling collection of systems" that induces stress in players by forcing them to recognize the game did not care if they make the correct decision. To illustrate the game's strategic management, Solomon and Meier built an XCOM board game using pieces from Risk.

The developers, who were inspired by animals such as insects, spider monkeys and gorillas, wanted to modernize designs for some of the aliens from UFO: Enemy Unknown. The Sectopods were originally intended to be a huge, hulking enemy but were significantly shrunk to ensure they can spawn in several of the game's levels instead of existing only as a setpiece.

The design of XCOM's soldiers was inspired by action figures; the team strove for a stylized, bright, flat-textured look. Roland Rizzo, who has been working on the X-COM series since its inception, became the audio lead for XCOM: Enemy Unknown, and was tasked with re-imagining and updating John Broomhall's original music score.

Pre-ordering the game granted players access to an Elite Soldier Pack, which included the "Classic X-COM Soldier"—a haircut for the player's male soldier based on the model for troops in UFO: Enemy Unknown—and the option to customize the aesthetic design of soldiers' armor. XCOM: Enemy Unknown playable demo version was released in September 2012.

XCOM: Enemy Unknown was released for Windows PC, PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 in North America on October 9, 2012, and in Europe on October 12, 2012. XCOM: Enemy Unknown Plus, which includes the base game, Enemy Within and other DLC packs, was released for PlayStation Vita on March 22, 2016.

An expansion pack titled XCOM: Enemy Within, which was released worldwide on November 15, 2013. According to review aggregator website Metacritic, XCOM: Enemy Unknown received "generally favourable reviews" from critics.

Adam Biessener from Game Informer wrote that turn-based combat and real-time management presented "life-or-death conundrums to which there is no right answer", and concluded his review by writing XCOM: Enemy Unknown is a "singular achievement that every gamer deserves to experience". GameSpy, Kotaku, MTV, Giant Bomb, and GameTrailers selected XCOM: Enemy Unknown as their Game of the Year.

The game, which was released when interest in turn-based strategy games was low, PC Gamer s Fraser Brown, in a 2019 article, wrote that XCOM: Enemy Unknown "came along and lit a fire under the genre, arguably kicking off the decade of turn-based tactics games". The game takes place 20 years after the events of Enemy Unknown; its story is an alternative scenario in which XCOM failed to stop the alien invasion and humanity surrendered.

XCOM 2 was released for Windows in February 2016 and for consoles in September the same year. A board game based on Enemy Unknown, developed and published by Fantasy Flight Games, was released on January 28, 2015.

The mod was praised by players and members of the Firaxis development team, and 2K recruited the team to create several day-one mods for XCOM 2.
