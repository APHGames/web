Fox McCloud leads a team through polygonal space. The Super FX chip brought 3D polygonal action to the SNES, and branching routes increased replayability.

Fox McCloud flies on rails through a polygonal space and, with a team that advises you over the radio, chooses a harder or an easier branch at the junctions. The shooting is on rails, not in free space. The skill is in hitting and in not losing a teammate, not in flying anywhere you like.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/140-screenshot-1.jpg)

Nintendo EAD made the game in 1993 with the British Argonaut Software. The Super FX, a chip in the cartridge, computes polygons the SNES itself cannot do. Dylan Cuthbert and the people from Argonaut brought to Kyoto a kind of 3D that the console otherwise draws only as Mode 7. It is a collaboration of hardware and software, not just a new sprite.

The decision to branch the route has a consequence for replay. A short game gets longer because the second flight is a different map, not merely a harder version of the same corridor. In 1993 polygons on a home console are still a wonder. Star Fox is that wonder wrapped in a squad that talks, so the technology has a character.

Slippy, Peppy, and Falco are not just life icons. Their lines keep you oriented on a rail where otherwise there would be only a stream of enemies. What has aged are the low polygons and the impossibility of leaving the path. The freedom a player expects from 3D is not yet the contract here.

What has not aged is the idea of a team in your ears. Later Star Fox 64 will amplify it, but the foundation, that you fly with someone, is here. The chip in the cartridge is an economic decision by Nintendo. Instead of a new console they add a coprocessor to selected games, and Star Fox is the display case of that compromise.

Compare the rail with Panzer Dragoon two years later. Sega lets the rider turn the view; Nintendo in 1993 keeps the camera forward and leaves the branching to the map. In a seminar, distinguish what is the engine and what is the design. The Super FX made the image possible; the junctions and the voices turned it into a game that can be repeated.

The first entry in the Star Fox series, the story follows pilot Fox McCloud and his elite squadron of mercenaries known as the Star Fox team defending their homeworld of Corneria against the invading forces of the malevolent mad scientist Andross. Regarded as one of the greatest and most influential video games of all time, Star Fox pioneered 3D rail shooter gameplay on home consoles, popularized the use of the Super FX chip, and established the Star Fox series as a flagship Nintendo franchise.

A sequel, Star Fox 2, was developed but remained unreleased until 2017 as part of the Super NES Classic Edition. The next-released game in the series, Star Fox 64, is a 1997 reboot for the Nintendo 64 and further expanded the series' influence on 3D space combat and squad-based gameplay.

Miyamoto cited the X-Wings from Star Wars as inspiration for the Arwings. Miyamoto stated that he wanted the Star Fox series to star animal characters since he was not interested in making a series with conventional science fiction stories with humans, robots, monsters, and superheroes.

The game was released under the title Starwing in Europe due to the similarity of the title Star Fox to the name of the German company StarVox. On the weekend of April 30 to May 2, 1993, the "Super Star Fox Weekend Competition" took place at approximately 2,000 retail locations within the United States.

Competitors received a limited edition Star Fox pin, and those who accumulated a particularly high score received Star Fox T-shirts as prizes. According to Peter Main, Nintendo's vice president of marketing at the time, Star Fox had a marketing budget of $15 million.

Star Fox received generally favorable reception from critics, holding a rating of 88% based on seven reviews according to review aggregator platform GameRankings. Unlike most games of this genre, though, Star Fox shows some heart behind the hardware — rarely have such powerful spacecraft been piloted by so adorable an array of frogs, birds, and bunnies".

Star Fox was awarded Best Shooter of 1993 by Electronic Gaming Monthly. In 2018, Complex ranked Star Fox 24th in their "The Best Super Nintendo Games of All Time".

Star Fox has become an established Nintendo franchise, with six more video game instalments, an LCD game, and numerous appearances by its characters in other games and media such as the Super Smash Bros. During the game's release, Nintendo collaborated with Kellogg's and Nelsonic to distribute a promotional Star Fox LCD Game Watch to those who bought a box of corn flakes and sent the order form to Kellogg's.
