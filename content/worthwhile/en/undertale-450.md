A child can kill the underground monsters, or form a different relationship with them. Bullet-hell defense, the possibility of killing no one, and the long-term memory of save and reset make the player's treatment of RPG violence the theme of the game.

The child in Undertale descends underground, and in battle their heart dodges bullets while the menu offers fight, mercy, and speech. The player can kill no one, and the game remembers that even at the moment you delete the file and pretend to start over. Every battle is a small bullet hell and a conversation at the same time. Papyrus, Undyne, or Toriel are not only hit points on a bar; they are sentences that change according to whether you arrived with a knife.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/450-cover-1.jpg)

Toby Fox wrote the game, composed it, and in September 2015 released it almost on his own, with art help from Temmie Chang. It was made in GameMaker and carries the imprint of EarthBound and of Fox's music for Homestuck. SAVE and reset are the theme, not only a menu function. The technology of the JRPG, that is level, experience, and a new game, turns here against the player who is used to taking violence as the optimal path.

The decision to allow a pacifist, a neutral, and a genocide route means the same encounter has several legal regimes. The consequence is that the genre violence of the RPG becomes the content, not an unquestioned tool. The music changes a battle before the statistic does. Fox, as composer and designer, ties a leitmotif to whom you are willing to spare.

In 2015 this is an independent explosion that speaks to people who know JRPGs with a joke and with an indictment. The low price and the short campaign are part of how fast the game spreads. The meta elements around Flowey and the later endings count on the player having already seen the genre. Anyone who has never killed a slime for experience will miss part of the joke.

The internet tone of some jokes has aged, as has the weight of the fandom, which now hangs over a short game like a foreign history. The battles themselves are still precise. The joining of the dodge, mercy, and the memory of the file holds. It is still the basic text on how a reload is not a morally neutral key.

The genocide route is a worse game in the ordinary sense and a better argument. Boredom and disgust are part of the punishment, not content that should entertain you the way mercy does. In class, have students play it twice under a different rule of killing. The second playthrough is a commentary on the first, not new content in a shop.
