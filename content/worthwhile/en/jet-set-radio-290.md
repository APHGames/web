A gang of skaters tags Tokyo-to and escapes the police. Cel shading, movement on skates, and musical identity created one of the first truly distinctive styles of the polygonal era.

On skates you ride Tokyo-to, which is a manga in motion, and the verb is the spray can. The police and the rivals are an obstacle of time, not an army you are supposed to erase. Cel shading, an outline and a flat color on a 3D model, is still a statement in 2000. The Dreamcast refuses here to chase a photorealistic texture, and the style that other games will take up after it comes from that refusal.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/290-cover-1.jpg)

Smilebit, a Sega studio, released it in 2000. In North America it is called Jet Grind Radio because of a trademark, and Hideki Naganuma's music holds the city together as much as the grind rail does. The decision to make graffiti the goal and the ride an escape had a consequence for violence in a more open street. The game does not have to kill anyone in order to have a score, a chase, and a gang, and that is why it sounds different from the city games of the same period.

In 2000 polygonal realism is the prestige. Jet Set Radio is the counterargument in the shop: a console can also be shown off by looking like a magazine, not like metal. What has aged is the camera and the timer on a tag, which can be unfair. What has not aged is the look, the soundtrack, and the fantasy of a crew that has its own wall.

In seminar, hide the outline in discussion and let students say what remains of the picture. Little remains, and that names what was a technical decision and what was fashion. Against Tony Hawk there is less of a trick list here and more territory. Both ride, and each says differently whom the street belongs to.

The transfer to other regions under another name is an industrial detail that teaches that even a style runs into the legal owner of a word. The game itself does not change by that, and the box does. The police, Captain Onishima, are a drawn antagonist. The pursuit is the rhythm of the level and not a simulation of a city, which has to be said so the game is not judged as GTA.

On this game the Dreamcast receives a visual sentence that people keep quoting after the console's collapse. The style outlived the machine, and that is rarer than the reverse. Anyone who wants to teach that 3D does not have to be real should start here, not at an engine showcase. Here the unreality is the rule of movement.

In North America, it was released on October 31 as Jet Grind Radio due to trademark problems for "Jet Set" in the United States at the time. The Jet Set Radio soundtrack includes original and licensed tracks in genres including J-pop, hip hop, funk, electronic dance, rock, acid jazz, and trip hop.

In 2012, Naganuma said Jet Set Radio and its sequel were his favorite projects. A soundtrack CD, Jet Grind Radio Music Sampler, was given to those who pre-ordered the game in the US, featuring 10 tracks, four of which do not appear in any version of Jet Set Radio.

The soundtrack CD Jet Set Radio Original Soundtrack (UPCH-1048) featured 19 tracks including a data track from the game and was distributed by Polydor Records on December 20, 2000, in Japan. For the HD release, a new soundtrack CD, Jet Set Radio: Original Soundtrack with Bonus Tracks from JSRF, was distributed by Sumthing Else on September 18, 2012, for North America and Europe, containing 17 tracks.

A second soundtrack for the HD version, Jet Set Radio Sega Original Tracks, was distributed by Sega, containing 15 tracks and was released on iTunes on October 3, 2012, alongside Jet Set Radio Future Sega Original Tracks. Sega re-released Jet Set Radio in Japan under the name This version was released on October 18, 2001, in Japan via Dreamcast Direct (later renamed Sega Direct) and included a T-shirt featuring the protagonist Beat for those who pre-ordered.

It combines the North American, European, and Japanese versions' soundtracks and adds bonus tracks from Jet Set Radio Future, but it omits the PAL version's tracks "Yappie Feet" and "Many Styles". The Xbox 360 version of Jet Set Radio was delisted off the Xbox Live Arcade store in February 2023, but is still available for download for those who own it.

Jet Set Radio received universal acclaim for its gameplay, visual style, and music. GamePro called Jet Set Radio one of the best-sounding games of the year for its soundtrack, Accolades.

Jet Set Radio won the Best Console Game at the E3 Game Critics Awards in 2000 and was the runner up for Best in Show at the same event. Jet Set Radio received nominations for the "Game of the Year", "Console Game of the Year", "Console Innovation", "Art Direction", "Game Design", "Original Musical Composition", "Sound Design", and "Visual Engineering" categories at the 4th Annual Interactive Achievement Awards (the most nominated game at that ceremony).

Jet Set Radio also holds a Guinness World Record for the first video game to use cel-shading. Jet Set Radio is recognized as one of the first games to feature cel-shaded graphics, with exaggerated shapes, thick lines, and flat, bright colors.

Numerous indie developers have cited Jet Set Radio as a major influence on their games, the most notable being the successfully crowdfunded Hover: Revolt of Gamers, Lethal League, and Bomb Rush Cyberfunk, all of which draw heavy inspiration from Jet Set Radio s visuals and music and feature contributions from its composer, Hideki Naganuma. Other fan community-based Jet Set Radio projects include Jet Set Radio Live, a 24/7 browser-based radio station based on the fictional radio station in-game created in January 2016 and the albums Memories of Tokyo-To released on February 20, 2018, and Sounds of Tokyo-To Future by American musician 2 Mello on August 24, 2021.

Jet Set Radio and Jet Set Radio Future are highly requested games to be ported to the Xbox One, the Xbox Series X/S, In 2021, Sega announced Beat as a playable character for Super Monkey Ball Banana Mania.
