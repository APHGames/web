Races can be won by the track, by destroying opponents, or by a violent sandbox. Destruction, physics, and looser rules turned the race into an anarchic systemic space.

You can win a race by finishing the track, by destroying the opponents, or by running people down. The track is one of three contracts, not an obligation. The cars flip and an impact has weight. The physics is not decoration behind the racing line; it is the reason it is worth leaving the ideal line at all.

Stainless Games built it in Britain for SCi and released it in 1997 on DOS and Windows. The inspiration is film demolition in the manner of Death Race 2000, and the studio turns that into a system, not a cutscene. The custom 3D engine of the period can crumple metal on machines that can barely draw a textured street. The technical bet is on chaos that has to be calculated, not on a pretty sunset.

The decision to give three victory conditions had a consequence that turned the race into a sandbox. If you do not want to drive the lap, you can use the city as a toy and still get a result. The violence against pedestrians produced censored versions: in some releases the blood disappears or the people are replaced with zombies. The game is also a document of what 1997 was willing to put in a shop.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/231-cover-2.jpg)

Beside Gran Turismo, which teaches you to brake, Carmageddon is the other pole of the automotive year. One game is a driving school, the other is permission not to keep the rules. What has aged is the graphics and the sensitivity you now have to handle pedagogically, not merely play through. What has not aged is the permission to ignore the race and take the car as a physics joke.

In seminar, separate the three victories on the board. Students will see that a genre is held together by a goal, and when you loosen the goal, a different game appears in the same car. Against later open-world demolitions this one is more honest in that it puts the anarchy into the victory conditions, not only into a side mode.

The handling is arcade-like and mean. Treat that as a property of the toy, not as an unfinished simulation that Polyphony would recognize. The game belongs in teaching also as a dispute about content. The system is interesting and the subject is vulnerable; both belong in one sentence, not in an apology.

In 2011, Stainless Games obtained the rights to Carmageddon from Square Enix Europe (now Eidos Interactive). THQ Nordic acquired the rights to the Carmageddon series from Stainless Games in December 2018.

The game that became Carmageddon started out as 3D Destruction Derby, a banger racing sim prototyped by Stainless Software. The Carmageddon Max Pack, released on February 17, 1998, bundled the original game and its expansion pack into one package.

As a bonus, it also included a strategy guide, mousepad, and a leather car key chain with Carmageddon s logo. Carmageddon and its expansion Splat Pack were released on GOG.com on September 27, 2012, for modern operating systems.

In July 2011, the City of Los Angeles launched a massive media campaign under the title "Carmageddon" to warn drivers about a major closure on the 405 Freeway during the weekend of July 15–17. Stainless Games capitalized on the coincidence to promote the upcoming Carmageddon releases by announcing on the official web site that "L.A.

The news that Carmageddon is back has been such a hit in California, that the authorities have decided to dedicate a whole weekend to the game!" A port for Android based devices was released on May 10, 2013. According to the co-founders of Stainless Games, about two million copies of the Carmageddon series were sold in total.

Carmageddon had two sequels, Carmageddon II: Carpocalypse Now (1998) and Carmageddon TDR 2000 (2000). In 2005, Swedish publisher Synergenix released two adaptations of the game for mobile devices entitled Carmageddon and Carmageddon 3D, developed by Impressionware and Kampo Interactive, respectively.

Released on July 4, 2005, Carmageddon features a top-down perspective for lower-end Java devices. Carmageddon 3D features three-dimensional graphics and was released on November 29, 2005, for Symbian and Mophun-based mobile devices.

A reboot of the series, Carmageddon: Reincarnation, was developed by Stainless Games, who re-acquired the rights to the Carmageddon name and released the game in May 2015. An updated version of the game, Carmageddon: Max Damage, was released the following year for Windows, PlayStation 4, and Xbox One.

In 2018, THQ Nordic bought the rights to the Carmageddon intellectual property. On August 3, 2021, THQ Nordic announced a Carmageddon-themed tournament in their Wreckfest racing game.

In December 2025, a spin-off of the Carmageddon franchise, titled Carmageddon: Rogue Shift, was announced by 34BigThings.
