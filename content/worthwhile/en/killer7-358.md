Harman Smith uses seven personas to fight the Heaven Smiles. Restricted movement, radical graphics, and politically fragmentary storytelling reject the ordinary conventions of 3D action.

In Killer7, seven personalities of Harman Smith move along nodes, and each shoots differently. The corridor between nodes is not exploration; it is a cut that lets you only where the graphics want. The Heaven Smile, enemies you see only in a certain mode, are both horror and a target. The game forces you to switch perception, not only to aim.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/358-screenshot-1.jpg)

Goichi Suda directed it at Grasshopper Manufacture, and Capcom published it in 2005 on the GameCube and the PlayStation 2, with Shinji Mikami among the producers. It is an auteur bet by a large publisher beside Resident Evil 4. The consequence of the restricted movement is that the action refuses the freedom of the camera that generation takes as progress. The restriction is a style and, at the same time, a political, fragmentary script that cannot be summarized into one mission.

The visual side, flat and sharp like a comic, has aged more slowly than the games that in 2005 chased a realistic face. The line was deliberately outside the race for texture. The controls are an acquired taste. Whoever expects an ordinary third person leaves. Whoever accepts the nodes sees that the fight is a gallery, not a simulation of walking.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/358-cover-2.jpg)

In 2005 it is one of the clearest art titles on a major console. Capcom thereby shows it can publish even a game that laughs at ordinary 3D. The story about Japan, the United States, and terror is fragmentary on purpose. The consequence is that the player assembles meaning like a dossier, not like a campaign with a clear commander.

For auteur students Suda51 is here even before No More Heroes. Killer7 is harder in form: the later games allow him more movement and less visual dictatorship. The music and the editing hold a pace the controls deliberately slow down. The conflict between an image that flies and a body that stands on a node is the main feeling of the game.

The seven personalities are seven weapons and seven narrators. Switching is not only a tactical choice; it is an exchange of who is speaking at all. When you play it today, do not correct its camera in your head. The game is not a broken third person. It is a different contract about where you are allowed to step.
