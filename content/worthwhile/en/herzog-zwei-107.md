A transforming aircraft buys units and conquers bases in real time. A significant console predecessor of the RTS: an economy, production, bases, and simultaneous combat between two players.

In Herzog Zwei you pilot a craft that transforms between a fighter and a walker, buy units, and occupy bases before your opponent does. Money flows from the bases you hold. If you lose a base, you lose income, so combat and economy share one screen.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/107-cover-1.jpg)

Technosoft, a studio better known for the Thunder Force shooters, released the game in 1989 on the Mega Drive. This is not a PC strategy game with a mouse. The Mega Drive hardware computes fast scrolling and the simultaneous movement of two players. The image is a platformer and an overview at once, not the isometric base of later years.

The decision to give the commander a body has a hard consequence. When you fly off for money or to rescue a unit, nobody stops the opponent's landing for you. In 1989 the Dune standard of harvesting and barracks does not exist yet. Herzog Zwei looks for real-time strategy in the language of the console: falls, shots, a map on a gamepad.

Units are ordered from a menu and walk to the target on their own. You are not their cursor; you are the strongest piece on the field and the only wallet. What has aged is the learning. Without a manual the game hides what each icon builds, and two players at one television need to know the rules already.

What has not aged is the commander's vulnerability. The tension comes from money management and the craft's survival being one problem. Two players share the same air. There is no turn that waits while you think about a purchase.

When you draw the family tree of real-time strategy, put this title on the console branch. Beside it, not beneath it, Dune II belongs only three years later. Technosoft is testing whether an arcade reflex can carry an economy. It can, but only for a player who can stand a defeat after thirty seconds of inattention.

It is the sequel to Herzog, which was available on the Japanese MSX and PC-8801 personal computers. Herzog Zwei combines the arcade-style play of Technosoft's own Thunder Force series with a simple, easy-to-grasp level of strategy.

Herzog Zwei supports both single-player mode against the AI, as well as a two-player multiplayer mode. Herzog Zwei was not a huge commercial success, due to its lack of marketing, relatively early release on the Genesis platform, The game was particularly well received in Germany, where it was known as Herzog 2; Power Play magazine gave it an 80% score in its April 1990 issue, Retrospective coverage.

Command & Conquer (1995), StarCraft (1998), War of the Ring (2003), Herzog Zwei is considered a predecessor to the MOBA subgenre, also known as action RTS, though a key difference is that Herzog Zwei has a fully customizable command unit with role-playing video game elements that the player has full control over, while commanding an army to go into battle with rather than mindless drones that respawn at set intervals. Herzog Zwei was re-released on Nintendo Switch as the final entry in the Sega Ages line of rereleases in Japan on August 27, 2020.

Herzog Zwei was re-released for the Sega Genesis Mini 2 on October 27, 2022.
