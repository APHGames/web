Billy Blaze travels planets in a fast PC platformer. Carmack's adaptive tile-scrolling solution showed that a DOS PC could handle smooth console scrolling.

Billy Blaze in a helmet runs across planets, jumps over enemies, and collects objects in a platformer that finally scrolls smoothly on DOS. The first episode is shareware. The player gets it free and buys the rest, which in 1990 bypasses the shelf of boxes.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/115-screenshot-1.jpg)

Carmack's adaptive tile refresh redraws only the piece of the image that changed. Thanks to that, an EGA computer can manage the console scrolling that DOS had limped through until then. The decision to split the game into episodes has a commercial consequence. A prototype that would have gotten lost in a shop spreads on a floppy and lives off the extra payment.

In 1990 the PC is still considered a bad platformer. Keen is an argument in code, not in advertising. The level design teaches running and pogo-stick shooting. It is not Mario; it is proof that the genre can be moved, and only then polished.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/115-screenshot-2.jpg)

What has aged is some cruelty of placement and the short lives. The map editor is not yet the one id will give players with Doom. What has not aged is the technical lesson. A smooth image on a weak machine is design, because without it the game is not a platformer but a slideshow.

Romero and Hall give the child hero his pace; Carmack gives the machine a reason to carry him. That division of labor in the studio will stay. When you tell the story of id Software, do not start with Doom. Start with the boy who had to run on a PC the way he ran on a Nintendo.

Apogee is not just a distributor here. It is the model by which Wolfenstein will be carried two years later, and Doom after that. Commander Keen is a series of side-scrolling platform video games developed primarily by id Software.

The series consists of six main episodes, a "lost" episode, and a final game; all but the final game were released for MS-DOS in 1990 and 1991, while the 2001 Commander Keen was released for the Game Boy Color. The series follows the eponymous Commander Keen, the secret identity of the eight-year-old genius Billy Blaze, as he defends the Earth and the galaxy from alien threats with his homemade spaceship, rayguns, and pogo stick.

Their obligations to Softdisk, where they had worked during development of the game, led to the creation of Keen Dreams as a prototype for the second trilogy of episodes. The final Keen game ten years later had oversight but little development work from id.
