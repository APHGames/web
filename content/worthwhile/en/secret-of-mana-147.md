A trio of heroes restores the power of Mana in real-time battles. Ring Command and local multiplayer showed an alternative to menu-driven turn-based JRPGs.

Three heroes restore the power of Mana in battles that do not wait for a turn. You charge a weapon by holding a button and you pick a spell from a ring without leaving for another screen. A second and a third player can join on the same couch. The Japanese RPG, which is otherwise solitude at a menu, has local cooperation here.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/147-screenshot-1.jpg)

Square released the game in 1993 on the SNES. Hiroki Kikuta wrote the music, and it is why people remember even the places they missed in a fight. The engine is two-dimensional and real-time on 16 bits. This is not an action game with a light story; it is a quest that refused to wait for an ATB bar to fill.

The decision to replace the menu with a ring has a consequence. Choosing a spell is a gesture in the middle of movement, so a partner can save you, or get in your way, while you are choosing. In 1993, beside Final Fantasy VI, which is still being prepared for the next year, Secret of Mana shows Square's second path: not a deeper turn, but a shared real time.

Charging a strike is a risk. Whoever holds an attack longer is briefly defenseless, so power is not just a weapon's number. What has aged is the company when you play alone. The allies step into your hits, and real time with artificial intelligence on the SNES can be chaos.

What has not aged is the music and the feeling that the world is one place you walk through together. A charged strike in the forest still belongs among the most legible pleasures of the series. The story of Mana is a simple frame. The design bets that three people at one television are the content, not an extra.

Compare the ring with the menu of Final Fantasy IV. There, time punishes hesitation in a bar; here, hesitation punishes the one who is standing in the fight. In a seminar, try the game with two players if you have the controllers. Alone you will see only half the design, because the other half is the other player's elbow.
