Pit ascends through the underworld, collects upgrades, and passes through fortresses. An interesting hybrid of the platformer, RPG progression, and vertical scrolling.

Pit ascends from the underworld through fortresses and gardens, collects hearts, improves the bow, and spends money in shops, while poverty in a fortress can turn you into an eggplant that can barely defend itself. The world is sometimes vertical, sometimes horizontal. The same character has to manage a fall, a chamber, and a maze, and the game is not ashamed of being a collage.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/078-cover-1.jpg)

Nintendo R&D1 released it in 1986 on the Famicom Disk System, in the same workshop and the same year as Metroid, under Gunpei Yokoi's supervision. It is the same team's second road: instead of a quiet return into corridors, there is a fairy tale that keeps climbing upward. The disk can save statistics that a cartridge without a battery would have held less well. The character's progress is part of the platforming, not a separate RPG in a menu.

The decision to mix the jump and the numbers had the consequence that death is not only a loss of place. It is also a question of whether you were strong enough before the fortress, or merely brave. The eggplant for a debt is a punishment that is comic and systemic at once. The game is saying that the economy is not a joke at the edge, but the state of your body.

In 1986 the Famicom Disk System is full of great names: Zelda, Metroid, Castlevania. Kid Icarus looks among them like an experiment that keeps an uneven rhythm, because it is more interested in the mix than in purity. Pit is not Mario. He does not have a perfect jump; he has a path that wants you to endure even an ugly screen for the sake of a number you will carry onward.

What has aged is exactly that unevenness of difficulty, and screens that are a test of patience rather than an idea. What has not aged is the willingness to let a platformer have a pocket, a shop, and a statistic, even if that spoils the elegance.

The series will return only much later. In 1986 it is proof that R&D1 can, beside Metroid, also make a game that shouts. In a seminar, compare one vertical shaft with one fortress. The students should name where the game stops being a platformer and starts being a test of preparation.
