A dragon rider fights through branching levels with an evolving dragon. Routes and the development of the dragon gave the linear rail shooter surprising replayability.

You sit on a dragon that flies for you, and you spin the aim around a circle, shoot, and in the bends of a level choose which branch of the sky you keep. The dragon is not a mount with one appearance. It changes according to the route and according to what you kill, so a repeated run is not the same machine.

Team Andromeda and Yukio Futatsugi released it on the Saturn in 1996 as a continuation of Panzer Dragoon from the previous year. The rail shooter, a game on rails, gains a memory here. Technically it is a Saturn 3D corridor with the dragon model in the center of the shot. The hardware cannot carry an open world, so the authorship sits in the route, the branching, and the creature's transformation.

The decision to let the dragon grow out of the style of play has a consequence for repetition. Failure and success are written into the partner's body, not only into a score that disappears when you switch off. The level branches are short detours, not a sandbox. Even so, in a genre where you usually ride once forward, they feel like surprising replayability.

In 1996 the rail shooter is treated as an arcade format on the way out, because the camera takes your freedom. Zwei shows that freedom can be returned in the rules between rides, not in walking. The world is told in fragments: riders, an empire, the dragon's biology. It is not built as an encyclopedia, and that is why it feels larger than the maps you actually visit.

What has aged is the feeling that the rail is carrying you and that the enemy is a wave in a tunnel. Anyone expecting a camera as free as in the later Saga will be restless here. What has not aged is the dragon as a changing partner and the choice of route as a reason to return to a level you have already survived.

In seminar, put it before Panzer Dragoon Saga. You will see how the aiming circle of a rail shooter later becomes the battle system of an RPG. Futatsugi's team defends the genre here by giving it consequence. A shot that misses can change the dragon you will ride next time.
