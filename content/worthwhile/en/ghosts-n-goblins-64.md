Knight Arthur fights a demonic world, and hits destroy his armor. Extremely precise timing and two-stage vulnerability are an archetype of difficult arcade action.

The knight Arthur runs through a graveyard and caverns, jumps on a predetermined arc, and loses his armor when hit; the second hit, now in his underwear, is death. A weapon on the ground takes the one you are holding. There is no inventory in a pocket, only a choice of what you can carry right now, and a bad choice cannot be set aside in a bag.

Tokuro Fujiwara directed it for Capcom, and the machine was released in 1985. It is a peak of the strict arcade action of that season, beside Commando by the same author, but with a completely different time: here you wait, there you advance. The picture is large, gothic, and readable from a distance. The red demon, the zombie rising from the ground, and the princess at the end are signs, not statistics.

The decision that the jump should have a fixed arc had the consequence that the player commits in advance. You cannot change your mind in midair, and so every pit is a promise, not a reaction. The game has to be completed twice before the true ending opens. The first victory is false, and the reward for endurance is more endurance, which is both a coin business model and an authorial gesture.

In 1985 Capcom is aggressively present in the arcade. Ghosts 'n Goblins gives it a face that is not a shooter: a slow knight, a cruel space, no bar as in Gradius. The difficulty is famous, and fair only to the person who learns a room like a score. Chance has little say here.

What has aged is exactly that cruelty of the second loop and the feeling that the game is selling time you already know how to play. What has not aged is the knight who, after the first hit, is both comic and vulnerable, and the jump that cannot be taken back.

A long line of sequels runs from Arthur, but the first game still says most cleanly: a life is two states, and then nothing. In a seminar, let the students die in their underwear and name whether that is a joke or a system. It is both, and that is exactly why Capcom left it in the game.

The Ghosts 'n Goblins franchise was created by Tokuro Fujiwara (also known for producing games in the Mega Man franchise) and is produced by Capcom.

The original score for the initial arcade release of Ghosts 'n Goblins was composed by Ayako Mori. Tamayo Kawamoto composed the score for Ghouls 'n Ghosts, the next entry in the series, followed by Mari Yamaguchi for Super Ghouls 'n Ghosts (with Tomohiro Masuda and Nobuhiko Isa handling the GBA re-release), and Masaya Tsunemoto and Kazuhiro Kotani for Ultimate Ghosts 'n Goblins.

This 7 disc boxed set featured over 350 tracks and was a complete soundtrack for the series to date, featuring the complete scores of not only every entry of the Ghosts 'n Goblins and Gargoyle's Quest series, but also of every PC and home console port of each game therein. Capcom released a soundtrack for Ultimate Ghosts 'n Goblins on September 30, 2006.

The Ghosts 'n Goblins franchise stands as Capcom's 13th best-selling game franchise, having sold over 4.7 million units. The main series went on a minor hiatus after the release of Super Ghouls 'n Ghosts in 1991.

Capcom general producer Noritaka Funamizu explained that the Ghosts 'n Goblins games take a long time to develop and were not popular in Japan; as a result, Capcom had taken a net loss on previous installments and were wary of producing a new entry. With the exception of the largely overlooked Makaimura for WonderSwan which was released only in Japan, and a re-release of Super Ghouls 'n Ghosts on the Game Boy Advance, the main Ghosts 'n Goblins series did not see a major release until Ultimate Ghosts 'n Goblins on the PlayStation Portable in 2006.

Ultimate Ghosts 'n Goblins was also the first entry in the series to employ 3D graphics, a fact praised by most reviewers: "The 3D graphics enhance the 2D gameplay by giving the world a sense of depth without interfering with the gameplay, and the whole package simply glows with vibrant color and lighting effects."
