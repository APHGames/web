Adam Jensen investigates an attack on a company concerned with augmentations. Approach paths, hacking, stealth, and social augmentation successfully renewed the Deus Ex tradition for the modern console era.

Adam Jensen in Deus Ex: Human Revolution investigates an attack on the company that gave him his augmentations, and he can open every building through a vent, a hack, a conversation, or gunfire. The player spends praxis points on a chip that changes whether he sees through a wall, carries a turret, or convinces a guard. Energy for abilities is a budget, not the infinite cooldown of a cinematic action game.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/422-cover-1.jpg)

Eidos Montréal, a studio founded in 2007, released the game under director Jean-François Dugas in August 2011 through Square Enix. It continues Deus Ex from 2000 without Warren Spector in the director's chair, and it translates the immersive sim into the console generation. The gold image filter and the dense cities of Detroit and Hengsha are a visual technology as much as the engine. The readability of paths, ventilation shafts, and terminals has to work on a gamepad and on a mouse.

The decision to outsource the bosses to Grip Entertainment is still visible in the game's reception. Those bosses are fights you cannot bypass with speech or a vent, and they break the contract the rest of the game keeps. The consequence is an instructive contrast: the Sarif or Hengsha levels teach choice, while a boss teaches that someone in production wanted a mandatory action scene. Players recognized that seam immediately.

![Artwork](/img/worthwhile-games/media/422-artwork-2.jpg)

In 2011 this is a successful return of a genre that people were calling dead for a big budget. Beside Skyrim it offers tighter, authored cities instead of a province. The social augmentation and the dialogue battles give speech its own statistic. Persuasion is not always only the correct menu choice, but a prepared character.

The gold grading has aged, as have some of the cover shooting and those bosses in particular. The endings, which meet in a fairly sudden choice, also fail to match the richness of the paths before them. The structure of a level with several legal solutions holds. The Missing Link add-on, set on a ship, keeps that contract more cleanly than the main bosses do.

Hacking as a network minigame is a period feature that slows stealth, but it gives the hacker a pace of their own outside shooting. In class, use it for the seam in production: you can recognize a great immersive sim also by the place where a production compromise stopped listening to it.
