A trio of heroes across three epochs stops a mutant tentacle. Puzzles based on cause and effect between time periods are a demonstration of extraordinarily elegant systemic comedy.

Bernard, Hoagie, and Laverne are trapped in three epochs, and an object you use in one changes the puzzle in another. The tentacle is a purple threat that ties centuries together. The comedy stands on cause and effect having decades between them.

![Artwork](/img/worthwhile-games/media/142-artwork-1.jpg)

Tim Schafer and Dave Grossman directed the game in 1993 at LucasArts. SCUMM still offers verbs, but the joke is already in the temporal logic, not only in the line. The CD version adds voices, so the timing of a sentence is acted. The engine itself is still a two-dimensional adventure with an inventory.

The decision that an action in one era is a condition in another has a consequence. The player has to hold three worlds in their head, and the game turns that into a gag, not just a crossword. In 1993 this is a demonstration of how far systemic comedy can go before the interface starts to slim down. There are still many verbs, and each one can be a joke.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/142-screenshot-2.jpg)

The family house as a node between epochs keeps the map legible. You are not in an abstract multiverse; you are in one place that gets rebuilt. What has aged is some of the combinatorics in which the right object is only the second joke. You understand the first joke, and you have to try the second.

What has not aged is the writing, or the nerve that an apocalypse can be ridiculous without ceasing to be the stake. Maniac Mansion returns in one epoch as a game inside the game. It is the studio's authorial memory, not a mandatory reference for a player who does not know the original.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/142-cover-3.jpg)

Compare time with Chrono Trigger two years later. There, time is a quest with combat; here, time is the puzzle itself. In a seminar, draw three columns and one arrow between them. If the arrow is a joke, you understand the design better than from a retelling of the plot.
