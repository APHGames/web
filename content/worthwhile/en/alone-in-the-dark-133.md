A hero survives a haunted mansion from cinematic fixed cameras. Polygonal characters in pre-rendered scenes and limited resources created the basic model of 3D survival horror.

In the mansion of Derceto you move through rooms from fixed cameras, conserve bullets, and open drawers, because the inventory is small and the house does not want you. The character is a polygon in a pre-rendered scene. It looks like a film, but the step is still a game: the wrong door, and something you cannot see in the shot comes after you.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/133-cover-1.jpg)

Frédérick Raynal directed the game in 1992 at Infogrames on DOS. The Lovecraftian house is a European answer to the idea that 3D need not be a fast corridor. The engine pastes a live model onto a painted background. The camera is chosen by an author, so the tension is in the crop, not in your having chosen the angle yourself.

The decision to limit resources and to give the camera cinematic power has a consequence. The player is fragile and often blind, which is the basic contract of survival horror even before Resident Evil. In 1992 the CD-ROM is on the way, but Alone in the Dark can do fear even on floppies. The spectacle is in the composition, not in minutes of video.

The puzzles are adventure puzzles and the combat is awkward on purpose. When fighting wins over flight, the game starts to break, which is a lesson the genre will repeat. What has aged are the tank-like step and some obscure locks. A death that comes from misunderstanding a shot can feel like an interface error.

What has not aged is the grammar of the fixed camera. A few years later Capcom will take it and add doors that open like a cut. After this game Raynal will leave the studio and, at Adeline, make Little Big Adventure. Both games share an appetite for a space that is not only an arena.

Compare the fear with Doom, which arrives the next year. id arms you; Infogrames disarms you and leaves you standing at the wrong angle. In a seminar, pause a shot and ask what the camera is hiding. What is outside the frame is a rule, not an unfinished spot.

Alone in the Dark is a survival horror video game series originally developed by Infogrames. In September 2018, Atari SA sold the Alone in the Dark franchise to THQ Nordic.

A small game made during the second game's production, Jack in the Dark, was used as a promotional game distributed during Christmas of 1993, just before the second installment's release. During Halloween, she enters a small toy store after dark and gets locked inside.

Jack in the Dark is an adventure game that focuses purely on puzzles and has no combat. Later CD versions of both Alone in the Dark and Alone in the Dark 2 came packaged with this game.

Developed by Infogrames and released for PC in 1993, Alone in the Dark 2 was a drastic departure from the original game, being more action-oriented than its predecessor, with a much greater emphasis on firearms and shootouts, and resulting in more difficult combat. An enhanced port under the name of Alone in the Dark: Jack is Back (Alone in the Dark: One-Eyed Jack's Revenge in the United States) was released in 1996 for the PlayStation and Sega Saturn.

Alone in the Dark 3 was the last game to utilize the same engine and characters as the original game. A version of the game for Windows 95 was released as Alone in the Dark: Ghosts in Town in 1996.

The next generation game of the Alone in the Dark series was created by Darkworks as a console survival horror title, and shows significant influence from the Resident Evil series. The control scheme and gameplay are much closer to that of Resident Evil than the original Alone in the Dark games.

Internally known as Alone in the Dark 4, the game was released in 2001, developed by Darkworks for the PlayStation and Dreamcast, and by Spiral Studios for PC and PlayStation 2, also with an alternative portable Game Boy Color version developed by Pocket Studios. Developed by Eden Games for PC, PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360, and by Hydravision Entertainment for the PlayStation 2 and Wii, Alone in the Dark changed the focus of the series back to survival horror.

Initially known as Alone in the Dark: Near Death Investigation, the game was re-branded as Alone in the Dark despite having few connections with the original game. The PlayStation 3 version was released several months after the other versions as Alone in the Dark: Inferno.

Developed by Pieces Interactive, Alone in the Dark is a reimagining of the original 1992 video game in a similar style to the Resident Evil remakes. When Alone in the Dark: The New Nightmare was made, the setting was moved from the 1920s to 2001, and a background legend was made up in order to explain that Edward Carnby belonged to a lineage of Shadow Hunters who were born the 29th of February of every 40 years and raised as orphan children in St George's Orphanage, all of them going by the name of Edward Carnby.

The Edward Carnby that plays the main role in Alone in the Dark: The New Nightmare was born in 1968. The 2008 Alone in the Dark reinterpreted once again the series storyline.

It follows the canon of the first three games, and ignores the events of Alone in the Dark: The New Nightmare. Alone in the Dark: Illumination takes place in the mining town of Lorwich, Virginia and harkens back to the series' Lovecraftian background.

Ted is actually implied to be Edward Carnby himself living under an assumed identity, still alive in the modern era due to the events of the 2008 Alone in the Dark game. The game engine developed for Alone in the Dark was reused in its first two sequels (Alone in the Dark 2 and Alone in the Dark 3) without substantial changes.

In 2005, an Alone in the Dark film was released starring Christian Slater as Edward Carnby. In 2008, a low-budget sequel titled Alone in the Dark II was released, starring Rick Yune as Edward Carnby and featuring an entirely new cast and a story centered on witch hunting.

An Alone in the Dark one-shot comic book entitled Life is a Hideous Thing was published by Semic Comics in 2001 in France and translated by Image Comics in the U.S. Written by Jean-Marc Lofficier and drawn by Matt Haley and Aleksi Briclot, it takes place just before Alone in the Dark: The New Nightmare.
