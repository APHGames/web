Mario and Luigi clear the sewers of creatures by striking platforms. Single-screen cooperative-competitive design cemented Mario's identity before Super Mario Bros.

Mario and Luigi in the sewers jump up into platforms from below and thereby flip turtles and crabs; only an enemy you have already knocked over may be finished off. The POW block shakes the whole screen and flips everyone who is currently standing on the ground. It is a shared weapon and also a way to complicate your partner's life.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/041-screenshot-1.jpg)

Shigeru Miyamoto designed it for Nintendo, and the machine was released in 1983. It is the last great arcade Mario game before the character moves to the Famicom and to a run along the side. One screen, pipes, and fixed platforms save hardware and at the same time force two players to share a tight space. Cooperation and competition fit nowhere except the same room.

The decision that an enemy dies only on the second touch had the consequence that the first jump is preparation, not a reward. A dangerous thing you still have to finish stays on the screen. Luigi here is not a color swap in the later sense, but a second player with a name. The brotherly pair is born from a cabinet for two, not from a story.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/041-screenshot-2.jpg)

In 1983 the arcade market in America is collapsing, and Nintendo, by contrast, is assembling a mascot. Mario Bros. is a small game in a year when the industry is arguing about whether video games will survive at all. Compared with Donkey Kong there is no longer a damsel on a girder. The goal is to clear the room. Mario's identity shifts from rescue to the craft of the jump.

What has aged is the severity of a single screen and the punishment for a partner standing under you at the wrong time. What has not aged is the rhythm of preparation and the finishing blow: flip, then kick, and meanwhile watch that the enemy does not get back up.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/041-screenshot-3.png)

Super Mario Bros. does not take the scroll from this game, but it does take the certainty that Mario is a person who deals with the world by a jump from below as well as from above. In a seminar, play it with two people. A solo demonstration makes it a poorer game than the one Nintendo was building in 1983.
