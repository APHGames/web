Civilians try to survive a besieged city. A war game shifts the perspective from the soldier to hunger, illness, theft, and the moral cost of civilian survival.

In This War of Mine you sleep, eat, and tend the sick in a besieged city by day, and at night one of you goes out to steal, because the game refuses to give you a soldier's rifle as the solution. The player assembles beds, cooks, watches depression, and decides whether to rob the defenseless. In the morning you read what the night expedition cost in body and in conscience.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/445-cover-1.jpg)

11 bit studios from Warsaw released the game in November 2014. It publicly relates the game to the siege of Sarajevo and to the civilian experience of war, not to the front-line strategy the studio and players know from elsewhere. Hunger, illness, noise, and fatigue are the entire technology. This is not a big engine world; it is a house and a few locations you visit at night with a flashlight and a pack.

The decision to move a war game from the soldier to the civilian means killing is often a moral bill, not a tactical gain. The consequence is tension from a refrigerator and from someone else's apartment, not from a map of the front. Characters have states that argue with your plans. A depressed person cannot carry another bad night only because the player needs parts.

In 2014, beside shooters and strategy games about armies, this is a sharp counterpoint. The Polish studio thereby enters an international argument about what the subject of a game about war is allowed to be. Random events can be cruel and sometimes outright a lottery. The studio bets that helplessness is the theme, and some players feel in it a punishment without a decision.

The places where randomness beats the preparation of the house have aged, as has the repetition of night locations in a longer run. The text events feel after a while like cards you have already leafed through. The perspective holds. It is still the basic text for a class on how changing the hero changes the genre, even if a shortage of food and the risk of violence remain on the table.

Day and night are two different controls. By day you manage a household, at night you play a fragile infiltration, and both halves are billed in the morning. In class, let students finish it without advice on whom to rob. The hesitation in the doorway itself is a lecture the system can give better than a slide.

This War of Mine is a 2014 war survival video game developed and published by Polish developer 11 Bit Studios. On November 14, 2019, 11 bit studios released a free content update called This War of Mine: Final Cut.

The iOS version of This War of Mine received "universal acclaim", while the PC and console versions received "generally favorable reviews" according to the review aggregation website Metacritic. In 2020, the Polish Chancellery of the Prime Minister announced that This War of Mine would be added to the recommended reading list in Polish high schools during the academic year of 2020&ndash;2021.
