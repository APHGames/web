Hundreds of tasks lasting a few seconds require immediate understanding of a verb. A radical compression of both the tutorial and the game: the player must grasp the rule within seconds.

In WarioWare you are given a verb for a few seconds and you must carry it out before the screen speeds up. The tutorial is the game itself, because the rule is explained by your playing it immediately. The microgames last so briefly that failure does not have time to hurt. The next verb is already running, and the tempo is both punishment and joke.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/328-screenshot-1.png)

Nintendo R&D1 published it in 2003 on the Game Boy Advance. Hirofumi Matsuoka directed it and Takehiro Izushi produced it. The idea continues from a mode in Mario Artist and it grew aside from the studio's ordinary assignment. The consequence of the compression is that the game teaches the grammar of games as such. Cut, dodge, time it: each verb is a foreign genre shrunk into one second.

Wario as the boss of a game company is a frame that justifies the mismatch. Without him this would be a bundle of minigames. With him it is a parody of the industry that manufactures those minigames. In 2003, on a handheld, it is a formal experiment that pretends to be a joke. The seriousness is in how little time a rule needs in order to be playable.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/328-screenshot-2.jpg)

Some of the pop-culture quotations have aged. The five-second form itself has not, and the series carried it to later consoles without changing the promise. Acceleration is a difficulty that needs no new enemies. An old game becomes harder because it will not give you time to think.

For tutorial students it is an extreme. If you fail to understand the verb, the game does not persuade you; it only throws you out and starts the next one. The bosses and the longer pieces are a reward for having learned to read instantly. The exception confirms that the norm is brevity.

Multiplayer and the later party versions show that the form is social. Laughter in the room is part of the feedback that the GBA alone only suggests. When you play it today, do not look for depth in a single microgame. The depth is in how many rules in a row you manage to read before your attention runs out.

WarioWare, Inc.: Mega Microgames!, known as WarioWare, Inc.: Minigame Mania in PAL regions, is a 2003 action game developed and published by Nintendo for the Game Boy Advance. The debut title in the WarioWare series, the game is about rapid completion of "microgames", short minigames given to the player consecutively and with increasing speed per each game complete.

The game went on to receive a multiplayer-focused remake called WarioWare, Inc.: Mega Party Games! The game went on to spawn the WarioWare series of video games, which all have the same formula of gameplay as the debut title, with the exception of Game & Wario.
