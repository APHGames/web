A D&D adventure is accompanied by tools for creating one's own campaigns. The Aurora Toolset and the Dungeon Master client turned the game into a platform for user-made RPG modules.

In BioWare's Neverwinter Nights you play an adventure under the third edition of Dungeons & Dragons, and you also receive the tools to build a campaign yourself. The Aurora Toolset is in the box. The official campaign is thereby only one module among those the community wrote after it.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/313-cover-1.jpg)

The studio of Ray Muzyka and Greg Zeschuk published it in 2002 through Infogrames. For BioWare it is a passage from its own stories to a platform on which others can write. The dungeon master client lets a live narrator into someone else's session. The game returns to the table the D&D rules came from, only over a network.

The third edition is visible in the rolls, the feats, and the small party. The goal here is not yet console comfort but fidelity to the table. In 2002, beside Morrowind, this is an RPG as a workshop. The value is not in one ending but in how many endings someone else can manufacture.

The camera and the small-party combat have aged into awkwardness. The toolset and the idea that an RPG is a module have not. The official campaign is honest and at the same time weaker than the best user modules. That is the price of deciding to ship the editor as the main content.

The persistent worlds on those tools lived longer than the game's marketing. The platform beat the campaign here, and that is a historical result. The second game later moved to Obsidian. The first remains the one that showed BioWare could lend its engine to other narrators.

Beside Warcraft III's editor it is the other great tool of the year. What grows here is not a strategy map but a module for a table with dice. When you launch it today, start with the toolset. Otherwise you will see only an average adventure and miss the reason the game is still discussed.

Neverwinter Nights is a series of video games developed by BioWare and Obsidian Entertainment, based on the Forgotten Realms campaign setting of the Dungeons & Dragons role-playing game. Aside from also being set around the city Neverwinter, it is unrelated to both the 1991 Neverwinter Nights online game and the 2013 online game called Neverwinter.

The Neverwinter Nights series take place primarily in the city of Neverwinter, located in the Sword Coast region of the fictional land of Faerûn. Also commonly referred to as the City of Skilled Hands, Neverwinter grew from a multi-racial settlement named Eigersstor founded several hundred years prior to the start of Neverwinter Nights.

At the time the first game takes place, the city of Neverwinter was beset by a magical plague named the Wailing Death, whereas in the second game the city is threatened by an invasion from its ancient enemy named the King of Shadows. Both Neverwinter Nights and Neverwinter Nights 2 contain three game modes: a default campaign, a multiplayer mode, and custom contents.

In addition to the base campaign and the multiplayer mode, both Neverwinter Nights and Neverwinter Nights 2 also included sets of software development tools which allow the players to create custom campaigns which they can then share with other players. The first installment in the series, Neverwinter Nights, was generally well received according to review aggregator Metacritic, which gave it a score of 91/100 based on 34 critic reviews and a score of 8.1/10 based on 608 user reviews.

Allgame reviewer Mark Hoogland commended the Aurora toolset shipped with Neverwinter Nights, calling the breadth of level, story, environment, and module creation options "impressive". The Neverwinter Nights series has been adopted by a number of educators for use in the classroom.
