Fast top-down football is complemented by an enormous database and a career. Joining immediate arcade controllability with management created a uniquely integral football model.

Tiny figures play football from a bird's-eye view, and behind them stands a database of clubs with which you can play a career, not just one match. The controls are fast and almost a single button. Readability at that size is a craft: you can tell a pass even when a player is a few pixels.

Jon Hare and Sensible Software released the game in 1994 through Renegade on the Amiga and DOS. It is their football after Sensible Soccer, extended by a world that outgrows the match. The engine holds hundreds of teams in data and an arcade match in real time. The simulation of the rules is deliberately thin; the simulation of the calendar is thick.

The decision to join an immediate match to a managerial season has a consequence. A player who does not want a table still kicks, and a player who does not want only to kick still runs a club. In 1994 the Amiga is in retreat, and this game is one of its late peaks. The database freezes in time, but the model of the match does not freeze with it.

Aftertouch and the direction of a shot are a skill that decides on a small pitch. There is no today's ball physics; there is an agreement that an arrow and a time are enough. What has aged is precisely that frozen database once you compare it with the yearbooks that come out every season. The career is a historical snapshot, not a living league.

What has not aged is the immediacy. Two players still know who passed badly, because the image is honestly small and honestly fast. After Cannon Fodder, Sensible again shows that a jokey miniature can carry a serious system. There is no grave here, but there is a transfer and a table.

Compare it with NBA Jam. Midway turns sport into the theater of the dunk; Sensible turns sport into a season that fits on a floppy. In a seminar, play only a half without the career, and then open a transfer. You will see that the core is still that half, and the rest is a reason to come back tomorrow.

Sensible World of Soccer is a 1994 football video game designed and developed by Sensible Software as the sequel to their 1992 game Sensible Soccer. The game includes contemporary season data of professional football from around the world, with a total number of 1,500 teams and 27,000 players.

In 2004, the Amiga version of Sensible World of Soccer was inducted into GameSpot's list of the greatest games of all time. Sensible World of Soccer s inclusion in this list is notable on three accounts: it is the only game in the list developed in Europe, it is the only sports game in the list, and it is the most recent game in the list.

The career game mode in Sensible World of Soccer enables players to manage a club through 20 seasons. "Goalscoringsuperstarhero" by Jon Hare (born 1966) and Richard Joseph (1953–2007), with vocals by Jackie Reed, was composed for Sensible World of Soccer.

The original song published in 1994 only had one verse; for the version of the games published in 2006, Hare wrote two more verses and he and Joseph re-recorded the song with original vocalist Jackie Reed who also appears with the Sensible team in the intro video to the game on some formats. The CD versions of the 2006 version of the game, entitled Sensible Soccer 2006, also include the 2006 studio recording as an audio track.

During the 1998 World Cup in France they made a special update, which was based on 97/'98 and contained also some new graphics. This "World Cup 98 update" was officially supported by Sensible Software and released on the Cover CD number 24 of the magazine "CU Amiga".

One of the best known is "Sensible World of Moon Soccer" which allowed users to try in-game options. Any attempts since 1998 to bring back Sensible Soccer as a 3D game were not successful.

It became quiet about Sensible Soccer after Codemasters' takeover of Sensible Software in 1999. After a 2 Player Plug 'n' Play version, a small mini console with two controllers and TV-out (containing Mega Lo Mania and Cannon Fodder besides Sensible Soccer), Codemasters decided to release Sensible Soccer 2006 right in time for the World Cup in Germany.

About the same time they released Sensible Soccer Skillz, another game for mobile phones which contained just a few mini games such as penalties or corner kicks. The graphics are enhanced (HD mode) and features the music from Sensible Soccer 2006.

Events in the past were the World SWOS Tournaments (WST) of the "SWOS Witnesses" in Serbia and the tournaments of the ISSA (International Sensible Soccer Association) in Copenhagen, Denmark, both played on the PC DOS version of the game. Since 2004 SensibleSoccer.de offers Sensible World of Soccer online play, and national and international tournaments and leagues are being organized and played on a monthly basis.

SensibleSoccer.de is host of the "Sensible Days", an annual meeting of Sensible World of Soccer fans with international championships on PC and Amiga in the manner of a LAN party. Since 2008, the "Sensible Days" are acknowledged by Codemasters as the official SWOS World Cups.
