The immigrant Niko Bellic seeks a new life in Liberty City. Euphoria animation and a physically dense city moved open-world interactions toward a greater feeling of weight and consequence.

In Grand Theft Auto IV, Niko Bellic, voiced by Michael Hollick, is an immigrant in Liberty City, and the city is smaller than the state of San Andreas and heavier. A fall, a crash, and a brawl have weight because Euphoria, from NaturalMotion, computes the animation. The phone rings and cousin Roman wants bowling. When you do not show up, the relationship decays. The open world here has a social price, not only an icon on the map.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/385-cover-1.jpg)

Rockstar North published it in 2008 on the PlayStation 3 and the Xbox 360. The RAGE engine, which had appeared earlier in a smaller game, carries the city here, and Euphoria carries a body that grabs a railing when it falls. The consequence of procedural animation is the feeling that the street is not a script of an impact. A character catches himself the way a specific edge struck him, and therefore the city is discussed as mass, not as scenery.

The tone is darker than in San Andreas. The immigrant story and the joke that hurts are a conscious turn away from the excess of the previous game, and some players in 2008 did not want that turn. The gray-green palette and some of the closed missions have aged. The weight of the body and the density of the city have not, and GTA V will later return from them toward a wider spectacle.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/385-screenshot-2.jpg)

The driving is heavier than in Vice City. A car has inertia, and therefore a chase is not an arcade; it is a consequence of the same physics that governs falls. For animation students Euphoria is a class about what happens when you stop drawing every fall by hand. You gain surprise. You lose part of the authorial control over how a fall looks in the shot.

The episodes The Lost and Damned and The Ballad of Gay Tony in 2009 read the city again. The base game is still Niko and his phone, not a whole trilogy of tones. Multiplayer exists, but the historical center of gravity is in the campaign. At the start of the generation Rockstar builds a prestige single-player game here, not a service.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/385-screenshot-3.jpg)

The bridge, the swing, and other physical toys became folklore because the system let players find a sentence the mission did not write. When you play it today, let Niko fall from a small height and watch the hands. In those hands 2008 is still more convincing than on the map.
