The player searches fragments of police interrogations by their own keywords. Database search is at once the interface and the detective method; the order of the story arises from the player's hypotheses.

In Her Story you type words into a search box and receive fragments of police interviews from the nineties. The player is not a detective with a badge in a 3D city; they are a user of a database whose hypothesis decides which tape they will even see. Each clip is short and ends before you want it to. The next word is a bet: a name, a date, or something the woman in the shot said only once.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/451-screenshot-1.jpg)

Sam Barlow, an author with a past on Silent Hill: Shattered Memories, released the game in June 2015. Viva Seifert plays the woman in the interview, and the whole production stands on FMV, not on a character model. The database is both the engine and the method. Full text rewards you only with the clips that contain the word, so a dead end is an empty list and success is a new cut, not an unlocked cutscene.

The decision to give no linear order means two players have a different film. The consequence is a detective story whose plot arises from what you are willing to type into the line. The old computer interface is not extra nostalgia. It limits you: the number of clips seen for one word, and a duration in which you have to work with memory, not with a map.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/451-screenshot-2.jpg)

In 2015 this is a short game that makes its way among the big RPGs as proof that search can be a verb. Barlow will later build Telling Lies and Immortality on it, but this is the first sentence. The argument about when you should stop searching is part of the design. The game does not give you a clear stamp that you already know the truth; it gives you another clip if you have another word.

The CRT has aged as a fashion, not as a constraint. The method itself still works, because curiosity and vocabulary do not fall behind resolution. The feeling that you figured something out yourself holds. That is rare in games that otherwise highlight the next objective.

One actress and many cuts keep the production at a scale an indie game can carry. The limit of the cast is a condition of the form, not a saving that would betray it. In class, let students play it without shared keywords. The different order of clips in the room is proof that the query assembles the story here, not the author's timeline.

Her Story is an interactive film video game written and directed by Sam Barlow. Her Story was acclaimed by many reviewers, with praise particularly directed at the narrative, unconventional gameplay mechanics, and Seifert's performance.

Her Story is an interactive movie game, focusing on a series of seven fictional police interviews from 1994. It is unclear if Eve's story of being an identical twin is true, an intentional fabrication meant to confuse the police, or a case of dissociative identity disorder, with pieces of evidence lending credence to each theory.

As the player uncovers enough of the story, a chat window appears asking if they are finished. Kimberley Wallace of Game Informer wrote that the "fragmented" delivery of the story "works to its benefit".
