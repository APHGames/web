The Dragonborn freely wanders a northern province struck by war and the return of dragons. An extremely accessible sandbox and an extensive modding culture made the open RPG a global mainstream.

The Dragonborn in Skyrim can, after the opening at the execution, go almost anywhere in a province that is fighting a civil war and the return of dragons at the same time. The player collects shouts, brews potions, takes quests in towns, and often turns aside because there is a tower on the horizon. Combat in first and third person is accessible: block, strike, spell, and a dragon you pull out of the sky with a shout. The deeper game is in how you spread a character across thief, mage, and warrior without a hard class ceiling.

Bethesda under Todd Howard released the game on November 11, 2011. The Creation Engine continues from Gamebryo and is built to carry an open landscape, object physics, and above all mods. Radiant quests can manufacture a contract from a random target. The consequence is a world that feels inexhaustible, and at the same time a sense that some tasks are only an arrow to a bandit in a cave.

The decision to let the player go to any mountain they see is this entry's famous principle. Orientation by sight replaces some quest markers and turns the province into a promise, not a corridor. The modding community and the Creation Kit turned that promise into a platform. Years later the game is played in forms Bethesda did not ship, from fixes to entire new lands.

At the end of 2011 this is the moment when the open RPG becomes a global mainstream even for people who never finished a dialogue in Fallout. The accessibility is deliberate. The civil war, the guilds, and the main dragon line run side by side. The strongest parts are often the small stories in inns, not the throne in the main branch.

Face animations have aged, as have the repeated voices and the radiant contracts, which after dozens of hours reveal their template. The main dragon line is also thinner than the world around it. The sense of place holds, as do Jeremy Soule's music and the freedom to go the wrong way. That is why the game keeps returning in new editions.

Shouts are spells that change movement: speed, a push, a breath. The studio thereby gives a fantasy ability that is also a means of transport. In class, set it beside Dark Souls from the same year. The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim is a 2011 action role-playing game developed by Bethesda Game Studios and published by Bethesda Softworks. It is the fifth main installment in The Elder Scrolls series, following The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion (2006), and was released worldwide for Windows, PlayStation 3, and Xbox 360 on November 11, 2011.

The game is set 201 years after the events of Oblivion and takes place in Skyrim, the northernmost province of Tamriel, a continent on the planet Nirn. A remastered version subtitled Special Edition, containing all three DLC add-ons along with a graphical upgrade, was released in October 2016; a port for the Nintendo Switch was released in November 2017 alongside a separate virtual reality-only version titled Skyrim VR.

The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim is a fantasy action role-playing game, playable from a first- or third-person perspective. The player may freely roam over the land of Skyrim, an open world environment based on Scandinavia consisting of wilderness expanses, dungeons, caves, cities, towns, fortresses, and villages.

Skyrim is set 201 years after the events of The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion, and it is not a direct sequel. Full development began following the release of Fallout 3 in 2008; the developers considered Skyrim a spiritual successor to both Fallout 3 and previous Elder Scrolls games.

Howard expressed the team's desire to re-encapsulate the "wonder of discovery" of Morrowind s game world in Skyrim, as the return to the classic fantasy of Arena and The Elder Scrolls II: Daggerfall in Oblivion meant sacrificing a world with a unique culture. Skyrim features 244 quests, over 300 points of interest with a map marker and numerous unmarked locations.

Skyrim is powered by Bethesda's Creation Engine, forked from the Fallout 3 codebase specifically for Skyrim. After Fallout 3 s release, the team devised numerous design objectives to meet for Skyrim, and according to Howard, the team "got all those done and kept going".

Had the team not been able to meet their design goals with seventh generation of video game consoles, they would have waited for the next generation and released Skyrim then, but Howard felt that the technology of the time did not hold the team back at all. In Skyrim, NPCs can move around and make body gestures while conversing with the player.

Skyrim uses the Radiant AI artificial intelligence system that was created for Oblivion, and it has been updated to allow NPCs to "do what they want under extra parameters". The team employed Jeremy Soule to compose the music for Skyrim, who previously worked on Morrowind and Oblivion.

Creative director Todd Howard envisioned the theme for Skyrim as The Elder Scrolls theme sung by a choir of barbarians. "Day One" pre-orders from Amazon.de also included a five-track promotional Skyrim soundtrack sampler.

By November 1, 2011, a copy of the Xbox 360 version had been leaked and made available through the Internet, allowing those with a hacked Xbox 360 to play Skyrim 10 days before its release. On January 11, 2014, Bethesda partnered with 2K Games to bundle Skyrim with BioShock Infinite on the Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3.

At the 2011 QuakeCon conference, the team unveiled Skyrim s "Collector's Edition" package. Bundled with a copy of the game is a map of the game world, a 12-inch figurine of Alduin, as well as a 200-page concept art book and a DVD feature about the making of Skyrim.

On June 4, 2016, Bethesda announced The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim – Special Edition, a remaster for the PlayStation 4, Windows, and Xbox One. On December 9, 2025, Bethesda also announced the release of Skyrim Anniversary Edition on Switch 2.

At E3 2017, Bethesda announced a PlayStation VR version of Skyrim, titled The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim VR, for release in November 2017. In October 2016, the reveal trailer for Nintendo Switch featured gameplay of Skyrim on the console.

A parody of Skyrim s numerous ports and re-releases, titled Skyrim: Very Special Edition, was released by Bethesda at their E3 2018 press conference for Amazon Alexa, stating it would be also released for the Etch A Sketch, Motorola pagers and Samsung smart refrigerators. The Alexa version was found to be a legitimate version of the game developed by Bethesda, playing off the joke that the studios had seemingly ported Skyrim to all possible platforms following its E3 2017 announcements.

While Skyrim was being ported to next-generation systems, Zen Studios developed a virtual pinball adaptation of the game as part of the Bethesda Pinball collection, which became available as part of Zen Pinball 2, Pinball FX 2 and Pinball FX 3, as well as a standalone free-to-play game for iOS and Android devices. A Skyrim-themed expansion for the digital collectible card game The Elder Scrolls: Legends, titled Heroes of Skyrim, was released on June 29, 2017.

Dawnguard was the first downloadable add-on for Skyrim, revolving around a battle between the Dawnguard and Clan Volkihar. It adds two new areas to explore outside of the mainland of Skyrim: the Soul Cairn (a plane of the realm of Oblivion) and the Forgotten Vale (a secluded glacial valley).

Hearthfire was Skyrim s second add-on, which allows players to build houses and adopt children. The add-on takes place on Solstheim, an island northeast of Skyrim, belonging to the province of Morrowind, which is presented as an additional open world.

In April 2013, Bethesda announced via their blog that they were "moving on" from Skyrim and preparing to work on other projects. The 74 Creations were also separately packaged together on Steam and GOG.com as The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim Anniversary Upgrade, which was sold as a single DLC pack for the Special Edition in 2021.

A wide variety of official and fan-created mods, short for modifications, are available for Skyrim, which are generally made using the packaged Creation Kit. The Space Core acts as a non-player character voiced by Nolan North which follows the player around the world of Skyrim and dispenses space-related comments.

Enderal: The Shards of Order is a total conversion mod of Skyrim developed by SureAI as a sequel to Nehrim: At Fate's Edge which was developed for Oblivion. Several Skyrim modders were ultimately hired by Bethesda, or other games studios to work on future projects.

According to Skyrim s director Todd Howard, the notion that bugs were caused by "restrictive RAM" is incorrect. Another list of issues and bugs were addressed in this patch, and Skyrim launcher support for Skyrim Workshop (PC) was provided.

On April 12, 2012, Bethesda announced that Kinect support would be coming for the Xbox 360 version of Skyrim, with over 200 voice commands. According to Todd Howard, Skyrim had sold over 30 million units by November 2016, and over 60 million units by June 2023.

During the first day of release, Steam showed over 230,000 people playing Skyrim concurrently. Shortly after its release, Skyrim was the most-played game on Steam by a huge margin, with double the number of players as Team Fortress 2, the second-placed game.

Regarding sales on the PC, Todd Howard stated in an interview with Rock, Paper, Shotgun that, "Skyrim did better than we've ever done on PC by a large, large number. Skyrim has been credited by developers as an influence on later games, such as Valheim, The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild, and Avowed.

Skyrim spawned a number of noteworthy Internet memes that circulated as image macros or video parodies and achieved viral popularity. These include the dragon shout "Fus Ro Dah", and variations or adaptations of the phrase "I used to be an adventurer like you, then I took an arrow in the knee." During an interview in 2018, Todd Howard attributed Bethesda's frequent re-releases of the game to the "millions" of people still playing Skyrim.
