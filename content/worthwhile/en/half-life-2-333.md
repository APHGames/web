Gordon Freeman helps the resistance against the Combine in City 17. Havok/Source physics is not only spectacle but a puzzle and combat tool; the game also belonged among the essential titles of early Steam.

In Half-Life 2 you are Gordon Freeman in City 17 and gravity is a verb. The gravity gun lifts a saw, a barrel, even an enemy explosion, and makes physics a tool of both puzzle and combat. The Combine, Breen, and Alyx hold the line of the resistance. The story is not told by a journal but by what the city is doing in front of your eyes.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/333-cover-1.jpg)

Valve published it in 2004 on the Source engine with Havok physics. Viktor Antonov led the art direction of City 17, and the game also became the reason a broad PC world had to accept Steam. The obligation to launch Steam was a dispute in the year of release. The consequence that interests history more is that an update client began to grow into a platform without which PC play later hardly moves.

In 2003 an unfinished version leaked. When the finished game came out a year later, one could see how much still had to be written in Source so that the physics would not be merely a demonstration. The boat and buggy rides have in places aged into an obligatory insert. The gravity gun, the facial acting, and the environmental storytelling have not.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/333-screenshot-2.jpg)

Episodes One and Two continue, and a third episode never arrived. The history of the series is thereby also the history of an unfinished promise that players waited on for years. For students of physics in games it is a break. An object in the scene stops being decoration and starts being a sentence you can rearrange.

The linear corridor is honest. The game does not promise a sandbox; it promises that each next space will teach one use of physics and then turn it against you. The Combine soldiers and the headcrabs are readable even years later, because silhouette and sound were designed before the detail of a texture.

The year 2004 also has San Andreas and Halo 2. Among them Half-Life 2 defends a single-player game that does not need an open map in order to feel like a world. When you play it today, solve the puzzle with the gravity gun before you reach for a weapon. Valve's whole instruction is still in that order.
