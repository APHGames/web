Plants in rows defend a house against zombies with various traits. Extreme visual readability and the gradual dosing of new combinations are a textbook example of accessible systemic design.

You plant rows of the lawn with plants that shoot, slow, or block, and zombies with readable silhouettes come in from the right. You collect sun as the only resource, and each level adds only a few new combinations. The player does not pilot a unit. They build the economy of a row: when to plant a sunflower, when a wall, and when to spend everything on shooters because the wave is already halfway across the garden.

The decision to dose out plants in small lessons means complexity grows by combination, not by a manual. The consequence is a game that a person who has never played tower defense can open.
