A transport company connects industry and cities by trains, ships, and planes. Complex network logistics and emergent economic growth created one of the most durable management simulations.

You build tracks, roads, ports, and airports, and you watch whether a factory starts producing because you connected it to a city that wants the resource. Signals and junctions are a traffic puzzle. A train that is standing is not an animation; it is a hole in your network.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/167-cover-1.jpg)

Chris Sawyer programmed the game in 1994 largely on his own, in assembly, and MicroProse published it. It is an auteur simulation in the year when tycoon is becoming a fashionable word. The engine holds a long span of game time and a crowd of units on DOS. Assembly is not a romantic note; it is the reason a map can carry decades of traffic.

The decision that industry reacts to transport has a consequence. The growth of a city is emergent, so two players on the same map will not produce the same world. In 1994, beside Theme Park, Transport Tycoon is the stricter cousin. Less color, more of the question whether two trains fit on one track.

The four kinds of transport are not spice. A ship, a plane, a truck, and a train have different economies of distance, so the choice of machine is a choice of scale. What has aged is the original interface and the simplicity of the signals. Players later sharpened the traffic rules in follow-up versions and in the open OpenTTD.

What has not aged is the fantasy of a network that earns money even when you only look at it for an hour. OpenTTD is proof that the rule outlived the box. From the same discipline Sawyer will build RollerCoaster Tycoon a few years later. Transport Tycoon comes first: before the roller coaster arrives, there is freight that has to get there.

Compare it with The Settlers. Wertich lets carriers walk the paths; Sawyer lets the economy run on a graph that is already almost iron infrastructure. In a seminar, build one line and one jam. The students will understand the game sooner from a waiting train than from a catalog of vehicles.
