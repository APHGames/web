A dragon rider fights in a melancholic post-apocalyptic world. 360° aiming around the flying character used 3D space differently from ordinary forward rail shooters.

On a dragon you fly a rail, and the aiming is not only forward. You turn the view around the rider, and the shots go to the side and behind, so the space is a sphere, not a tunnel. The world is melancholic and post-apocalyptic. The landscape is not hostile only because there are targets in it; it is hostile in how empty and beautiful it is.

Yukio Futatsugi and Team Andromeda made the game in 1995 for the Saturn. In a difficult console launch, Sega needs a signature that is not just a technical demonstration from the arcade. The engine is a polygonal rail shooter. The rail holds the tempo, but the free turn of the camera gives level design a reason to build threats outside the center of the shot.

The decision to let the aiming go to 360 degrees has a consequence. The player stops reading only the horizon and starts reading the radar, which an ordinary forward shooter does not need. In 1995, beside Wipeout and Tekken 2, Panzer Dragoon is the Saturn's quiet argument. Less of a party, more of an authorial world that does not fit into one catchphrase.

The episodes are short. The game bets on repetition and on your knowing, on the second flight, where the thing that shot the dragon from behind last time will come from. What has aged are the length and the impossibility of leaving the rail. The freedom 3D promises is still directed here by the author of the shot.

What has not aged is the art direction and the grammar of the lock-on. Later entries, including the RPG Saga, still stand on the dragon and the rider being one weapon system. The music and the color hold a seriousness that a rail shooter usually trades for a score. Here the score serves the place.

Compare the rail with Star Fox. Nintendo branches the route and talks over the radio; Andromeda leaves the silence and lets you turn your head. Panzer Dragoon is a video game series developed and published by Sega. The first three games — Panzer Dragoon (1995), Panzer Dragoon II Zwei (1996), and Panzer Dragoon Saga (1998) — were produced by Team Andromeda for the Sega Saturn.

The fourth, Panzer Dragoon Orta (2002), was developed by Smilebit for the Xbox. A spin-off, Panzer Dragoon Mini (1996), was released for the handheld Game Gear in Japan.

The Panzer Dragoon games are rail shooters, except for the role-playing game Saga. The Panzer Dragoon games received positive reviews for their art design, visuals, sound design, and atmospheric settings.

The Panzer Dragoon series takes place in a post-apocalyptic world in which humans have begun to recover technologies from the Ancient Age, a world-spanning, hyper-advanced civilization destroyed thousands of years before the events of the games.
