Ashley Riot explores the cursed city of Leá Monde. A web of weapon affinities, body-part targeting, and an almost theatrical polygonal direction pushed the PS1 to the edge of its possibilities.

Ashley Riot in Leá Monde attacks body parts, and the weapon you hold builds affinities over the course of a fight. Combat is a workshop, not a choice from a party menu, because the party is almost absent. A chain of hits changes the risk and the type of damage. Whoever keeps striking the same way teaches the enemy to resist him, and that is why a statistic is dramaturgy, not only a reward for a level.

Yasumi Matsuno directed it at Square in 2000, with art by Akihiko Yoshida and music by Hitoshi Sakimoto. After Final Fantasy Tactics this is the second serious medieval game by the same author, and on the PlayStation it plays like a theater of polygons at the end of the console's life. The decision to leave you alone in a cursed city had a consequence for the tone. There is nowhere to hide a weak build inside a companion, and the camera, almost theatrical, turns that solitary figure into an actor, not a tourist of a dungeon.

In 2000, as the PS2 is already knocking, Vagrant Story is a game that wrings the picture out of the last year of the old console. The ambition is visual and systemic, and both are dense enough to be missed. What has aged is exactly that density, if you do not come to it with patience for the rules, and also the returns through corridors. What has not aged is the logic of the body part and the city as a gothic stage, not as a hub with a shop.

In seminar, take one enemy and force students to name which weapon and which limb they choose and why. If they cannot, the game has not let them in yet, and that is part of the design. Against Parasite Eve there is less of the city above and more of one labyrinth. Square is still testing how to join a statistic to space, and Matsuno tests it without the compromise of a party.

No large series came out of it. For a syllabus that is a plus: you see a finished tool that did not become a template, and you can ask what the template lacked, the market or the intelligibility. The writing is literary and in places closed. Treat that as a choice of tone, not as a translation error, even though the translation of the period does not make the work easier.

The game is proof that the PS1 at the end can do more than at the beginning. In genre terms it is not a leap in camera action; it is a leap in how much system fits into the combat of one body. Anyone expecting Final Fantasy will leave without a party. Anyone who stays gets a craft that resembles the preparation of a weapon more than a hero's journey.
