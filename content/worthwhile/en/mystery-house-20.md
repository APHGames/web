The player solves a murder mystery in a house using text commands and pictures. Roberta Williams joined the parser with illustrated rooms and opened the way to graphic adventures.

The player types text commands and at the same time sees a line drawing of the room in which they are solving a murder in a house full of suspects. The picture does not animate a character across the scene; it is a static illustration that says where you are, while the parser still decides what you are allowed to do.

Roberta Williams designed the game and Ken Williams programmed it on the Apple II; in 1980 they sold it as On-Line Systems, still a home business, not the later large Sierra. The drawings are Apple hi-res lines, designed by hand and transferred into the computer. Memory is not enough for a painting, it is enough for an outline, and the outline carries the genre.

The decision to add a picture of the room to the text had the consequence that the player reads the space with their eyes, not only from a list of exits, and the adventure stops being purely literary. The plot is a detective story in a closed house: a limited company, objects, and alibis. A genre from paper meets the parser from Colossal Cave.

In 1980 Akalabeth is coming into being on the Apple II beside it, and Pac-Man in the arcades. Mystery House is proof that a home machine can carry a story with a picture, even if the picture cannot yet walk. The Williamses build Sierra from that business. One game is also a company's founding charter.

What has aged are the unmoving lines and a parser that still punishes the wrong verb even at the moment you can already see the door handle. What has not aged is the contract of the graphic adventure: a place has a face, and the puzzle is about what you overlook in that face.

King's Quest, four years later, sets a character walking inside such a picture; without Mystery House that step would have had nothing to step from. Mystery House is an adventure game released by On-Line Systems on May 5, 1980. Mystery House is the first graphical adventure game and the first game produced by On-Line Systems, the company which would evolve into Sierra On-Line.

The player is soon locked inside the house with no other option than to explore. Initially, the player has to search the house in order to find a hidden cache of jewels.

Soon, dead bodies (of the other people) begin appearing and it is obvious there is a murderer on the loose in the house. One day, he took a teletype terminal to his house to work on the development of an accounting program.

Roberta decided that she could write her own, and conceived of the plot for Mystery House, taking inspiration from Agatha Christie's novel And Then There Were None. Mark Marlow reviewed Mission: Asteroid, Mystery House, and The Wizard and the Princess for Computer Gaming World, and stated that "Mystery House is considerably more difficult and provides many traps for the unwary in a wonderfully Victorian setting".

GamePro named Mystery House the 51st most important game of all time in 2007, for introducing a visual component to adventure games and for featuring graphics at a time when most computer games did not. Mystery House s success led the Williams to create the Hi-Res Adventures series, and note the game as Hi-Res Adventure #1.

In Japan, several different adventure games under the title Mystery House were released. In 1982, MicroCabin released Mystery House, which was unrelated to (but inspired by) the On-Line Systems game of the same name.

The following year, the Japanese company Starcraft released an enhanced remake of On-Line Systems' Mystery House with more realistic art work and depiction of blood, for the NEC PC-6001 and PC-8801, while Mystery House II for the MSX was released as a sequel to MicroCabin's Mystery House. The Japanese versions of Mystery House had sales of 50,000 units, including 30,000 copies on the MSX and 20,000 copies on the PC-6001, PC-8001, PC-8801, PC-9801, FM-7, and X1 computers.

One location from that game is a spooky house, where the player is told, "He's killed Ken!" and must seek absolution for murder. Mystery House is considered one of first such games where exploration of a strange house, where things are not what they may be seem, is a central element to the game.
