A writer investigates voodoo murders in New Orleans. More adult, research-oriented storytelling and strong characterization represent a peak of Sierra's literary adventure.

The writer Gabriel Knight in New Orleans assembles voodoo murders from conversations, books, and places, not from an inventory that would be enough to rub against a wall. The hero is vain and imperfect. The game lets you be him, so the investigation has a character, not just a list of clues.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/150-screenshot-1.jpg)

Jane Jensen wrote and designed the game in 1993 at Sierra for DOS and the Mac. In the CD-ROM version Tim Curry speaks Gabriel, and beside him sits a cast that treats the adventure like a radio play. The engine is Sierra's point-and-click. The verbs serve a text that wants to sound like a novel, not like a hint for a puzzle.

The decision that progress is research has a consequence. The player reads and asks, and clicking is a way to turn the page, not the main skill. In 1993, beside its fairy-tale series, Sierra lets out a more adult tone. Gabriel Knight is the peak of that ambition: a city, guilt, and a family inheritance.

New Orleans is not postcard scenery. Places have opening hours and people who know only a piece, so the detective story is in the gaps. What has aged is some of the treatment of voodoo as exotic color. The period text is strong in character and fragile where it borrows a religion as atmosphere.

What has not aged is Jensen's appetite for writing a hero who is not likable for free. The adventure here has a literary ego, not just a funny sentence on a click. The Schattenjäger, a family line of hunters, gives a series to a single case. Jensen builds a character who can carry a sequel, not a one-off case.

Compare the tone with Day of the Tentacle from the same year. LucasArts laughs at a system; Sierra here wants you to take the night city seriously. In a seminar, separate the puzzle from the sentence. If the sentence stands even without an object on the right pixel, it is a literary adventure, not merely a difficult one.
