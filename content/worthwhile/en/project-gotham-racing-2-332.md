The player combines speed with "Kudos" for stylish driving. It scores not only the result but the manner of driving; Xbox Live online integration was extraordinarily strong on consoles.

In Project Gotham Racing 2 you do not collect only a finishing position but kudos for how you drive: a drift, a flight, and a clean line have a price even when you are not first. The cities are real circuits pulled into a game track. The race is at once a tour of a place and a test of whether you can be fast and conspicuous at the same time.

Bizarre Creations of Liverpool published it in 2003 through Microsoft on the Xbox. The studio had already made Metropolis Street Racer on the Dreamcast, and from there it carries the idea of style as a score. The consequence of kudos is that the etiquette of the clean line stops being the only one. The game pays for an exaggerated passage, and therefore the track is driven differently from a simulation that pays only for time.

Xbox Live, launched a year earlier, is not an extra here. Ghosts, leaderboards, and network challenges are the reason a console in 2003 needs a racer like this at all. In 2003 Forza does not yet exist and Gran Turismo belongs to another console. PGR2 is Microsoft's answer, and it does not bet on an encyclopedia of the garage but on urban style.

The models of cities and cars are of their time. The idea that the manner of driving is judged, not only the result, has not aged, and it later echoes in other open racers. Kudos can be overcooked into empty theater. When you drive only for effect and lose the lap, the system punishes you because style without speed is not enough.

For students of scoring it is a class about a second meter. One number at the finish would make this an ordinary race. The second number makes it a performance. Bizarre later moves on to Geometry Wars and then the studio ends. PGR remains their purest sentence about what a console city race should look like.

The difficulty of the medals forces you to repeat the same street. The iteration is hidden here in the license and in the city, not in an abstract loop as in TrackMania. When you play it today, watch when you drive worse because you want more kudos. The whole design is in that conflict.

Project Gotham Racing 2 is a 2003 arcade-style racing video game developed by Bizarre Creations and published by Microsoft Game Studios. It was released exclusively for the Xbox in 2003 as the successor to 2001's Project Gotham Racing.

Project Gotham Racing 2 was critically acclaimed and is often considered one of the Xbox's greatest exclusive titles. There are four main parts to the single player game: Kudos World Series, Arcade Racing and Time Attack.

In Arcade Racing there are 60 medals available, with 20 medals each for street racing, timed runs and cone challenges. In both styles circuits and cars may only be chosen if they have previously been unlocked in kudos world series or arcade racing.

Project Gotham Racing 2 is now playable online again on the replacement Xbox Live servers called Insignia. Unlike many other Xbox Live enabled games, Project Gotham Racing 2 s online multiplayer ranking system is solely based on the kudos earned online.

Microsoft released two downloadable content packages for Project Gotham Racing 2: the Paris Booster Pack (released in May 2004), featuring eight new cars and seven tracks in Paris, France, and the Long Beach Booster Pack (released in June 2004) with eight new cars and eight tracks based around Long Beach, California, United States.
