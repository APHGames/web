Joanna Dark in the campaign uncovers a war of corporations and aliens, and in multiplayer you set simulants against yourself, bots whose skill you set, instead of waiting for three friends. Counter-operative lets a second player into the campaign as the enemy. Cooperation and a fight share the same missions, and the console shooter stretches further here than GoldenEye managed.

Rare released it in 2000 on the Nintendo 64 as the successor to GoldenEye. The main campaign needs the Expansion Pak; without the extra memory the machine cannot carry most of the game, and a peripheral becomes a condition, not an image upgrade. The weapons are gadgets, a laptop that unfolds, or a rifle that sees through a wall. The design continues the Bond toy and exaggerates it, because the James Bond license is no longer here and the studio invents its own.

The decision to give multiplayer bots and configurable modes had a consequence for being alone at the console. Practice stops being a privilege of the PC arena, and the N64, at the end of its life, receives a sandbox that is technically bold. In 2000 people are already talking about what will come after the cartridge. Perfect Dark is that cartridge's swan song: large, dark, demanding on memory, and still tied to a controller without a second stick.

What has aged is the frame rate and the readability in the dark. What has not aged is the idea of an opponent inside the campaign and the pleasure of a weapon being a joke, not only a higher damage number. In seminar, play one mission in two, the second time as counter-op. Students will see that the same level is two games if you are allowed to occupy the second role.

Against GoldenEye there is less of a film here and more of a system. Rare takes what it learned on split screen and adds solitude with bots, because four people are not always in the room. The story of the Carrington Institute is pulp, and that is enough for it. The historical work is in the tools, not in lore that would have to compete with Half-Life.

The Expansion Pak is a didactic object. Bring it to class if you have one, because a sentence about memory without the object sounds like trivia, and with the object it sounds like a design constraint. The game shows the ceiling of the N64. The ceiling is part of the history: the next year the console FPS will be moved by Microsoft onto another controller and another contract about life.

Perfect Dark is a 2000 action-adventure stealth game developed and published by Rare for the Game Boy Color. As a direct prequel to its Nintendo 64 counterpart, the game follows agent Joanna Dark as she completes her training at the Carrington Institute research centre and uncovers information against rival corporation dataDyne.

Perfect Dark was developed simultaneously with the Game Boy Color version of Donkey Kong Country.
