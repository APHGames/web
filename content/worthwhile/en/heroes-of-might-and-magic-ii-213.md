Heroes of Might and Magic II: The Succession Wars is a turn-based strategy video game developed by Jon Van Caneghem through New World Computing and published in 1996 by the 3DO Company. The game is the second installment of the Heroes of Might and Magic series and is typically credited as the breakout game for the series.

Heroes II was voted the sixth-best game for IBM PC compatibles of all time by PC Gamer in May 1997. Later, 3DO bundled Heroes II, its expansion pack and 30 community maps in one box, releasing it as Heroes of Might and Magic II Gold in 1998.

For the most part, Heroes of Might and Magic II is largely a graphical overhaul to the first game. Aside from the updated look, Heroes II added many new features that would become staples in the Heroes of Might and Magic franchise.

Heroes II added the Necromancer and Wizard factions, joining the original four factions (Knight, Barbarian, Sorceress, and Warlock) from the first game. Each hero still retains the 'primary skill' system from Heroes I but now can also learn secondary skills, giving each hero the ability to become more distinctive as they gain experience.

In Heroes I, heroes had a single, fixed special ability according to their class. Heroes I had used a memorization system in which each spell could be cast a certain number of times before being exhausted, where the player had to return to a guild to relearn the spell.

Heroes II uses a magic point system that allows the player to apportion spell use as needed, while the varying point cost of different spells maintains balance. Another major feature introduced in Heroes II is the ability to upgrade certain army units, granting them improved statistics and, in some cases, important abilities.

In order to play, players guide around their heroes on the global map and have them fight creatures, other heroes and castles on a instanced per-battle battlefield map. Lastly, Heroes II added more scenarios to the game and a proper campaign, where the player chooses the good or evil side and plays a series of scenarios using the three factions of that side.

The canonical ending of Heroes I results in Lord Morglin Ironfist's victory. This event is referenced later in Might and Magic VI: The Mandate of Heaven, when Archibald is freed of the spell.

He goes on to be a significant NPC in Might and Magic VII: For Blood and Honor. Much of the core programming of Heroes II was carried over from the original Heroes, thus the game actually took less time to develop than the original Heroes.

In December 1996, PC Gamer reported that Heroes of Might and Magic II was "doing particularly well in retail, with every 3DO retailer reordering the product." Combined sales of the Heroes of Might and Magic series, including Heroes II, surpassed 500,000 copies by October 1997. On the other hand, if you enjoyed the original this sequel will prove even more challenging and fun to play, and if you have never encountered Heroes of Might & Magic, there's a real treat in store for you in this game." The editors of PC Gamer US named Heroes of Might and Magic II 1996's "Best Turn-Based Strategy Game", and called it "clearly a cut above" the rest of its genre that year.

The Price of Loyalty was the expansion pack for Heroes II, released in 1997. The expansion also added a new structure for the Necromancer faction - a shrine that enhanced the heroes' ability to raise the dead (Necromancy Skills).

In addition to combining the base game with its expansion, Heroes of Might and Magic II Gold also included 30 maps created by community members.
