Players drive short tracks and build their own. Instant restart, ghost opponents, and an editor turn racing into an iterative time-trial puzzle system.

In TrackMania you drive a short track again and again, and between attempts there is no loading to knock you out of rhythm. The restart is part of the driving, not a punishment in a menu. Ghosts of other runs drive beside you. The opponent is not a body you crash into but a trace you try to undercut by a tenth.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/331-cover-1.jpg)

Nadeo published it in France in 2003. The car is not a simulation of rubber but a tool for loops, flips, and for a track you build yourself in the editor. The consequence of the instant restart is that the race becomes a puzzle about time. You do not solve who pushed you in a corner; you solve where to lead the same meter this time.

The editor moves the content ceiling from the Nadeo team to the players. The best tracks were often not written by the studio, and that is a success of the system, not its failure. In 2003, beside the big licensed racers, it is a small tool on PC. Historically a discipline grows out of it that will later run as an esport as well.

The simple graphics have aged into a style, not into an embarrassment. The loop of try, restart, compare with the ghost has not aged at all. Later TrackMania Nations and further games open it to a larger audience and add stadiums. The first is still the pure statement: a race is an iteration.

For time-trial students it is a model of how to remove friction between attempts. Every second of loading would kill the reason a track lasts half a minute. The physics forgives reality and punishes indecision. The car holds in a loop, but it will not hold a badly timed jump.

Sharing tracks makes the game a network before that is called a platform. A track file is content the studio did not have to paint. When you play it today, do not drive the campaign as a story. Drive one track twenty times and only then judge whether it is a game or a tool.

TrackMania is a racing video game developed by Nadeo and published by Focus Home Interactive. TrackMania includes some pre-constructed tracks that players can race on to unlock "coppers", the in-game currency.

TrackMania has three styles of cars, rally super minis (similar to a Renault 5 Turbo), alpine 4x4s (similar to a Suzuki Jimny), and speed muscle cars (similar to a Ford Escort). TrackMania Original is an October 12, 2005 re-release of TrackMania that runs on the TrackMania Sunrise graphics engine.

It was released free of charge for owners of TrackMania, in the form of a patch/expansion pack, and made available in store as a cheap collector's edition containing bonus features.
