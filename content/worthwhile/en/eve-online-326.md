Players build an economy, alliances, and wars in a persistent galaxy. The market, politics, and territorial conflict are largely player-driven; social structures become the main content.

In EVE Online you do not found a character in an instance but in one galaxy, where the market, the corporations, and the wars are driven mainly by players. Most ships you lose are gone for real. You fly, mine, manufacture, and in nullsec you hold territory that is fought over for weeks. The content is not a chain of quests but the politics of people who own the stations.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/326-screenshot-1.jpg)

CCP has run it from Reykjavík since 2003 as a world with a subscription. A server architecture around Stackless Python carries one shared galaxy, not a copy for every group. The consequence of the player market is that the price of a ship is a social event. When a large alliance breaks another, the economy registers it even for a player who was not in the battle.

In 2003, just before the arrival of World of Warcraft, it is deliberately the harsher sandbox. An MMO here does not have to be an amusement park with a clear next task. The interface and the newcomer's entry have aged into a reputation for impermeability. The political history the players wrote themselves has not aged as proof that a system can give birth to history.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/326-screenshot-2.jpg)

Corporations and alliances are the main content. A ship without people is an expensive tool, not a game. For economics students it is a rare case where most goods come into being in players' hands. The designers govern the rules, not the price list.

Full looting in dangerous sectors makes risk into a social contract. Without it, territory would be only a map with flags. The game has run so long that 2003 is only the first layer. Whoever judges it by the opening hours misses the reason chronicles are written about it.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/326-cover-3.jpg)

The newcomer's friction is an honest price for a world that does not turn around you. The studio has softened it for years and at the same time knows that without friction the politics would disappear too. When you talk about it in a seminar, talk about an alliance, not about the tutorial. The tutorial is a vestibule; the game begins where someone else can take your ship.
