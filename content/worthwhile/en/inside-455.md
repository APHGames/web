A boy escapes a totalitarian world full of experiments. Almost without words, it uses animation, sound, and fluid puzzle set-pieces as its only directorial language.

In Inside a boy runs to the right through a world of laboratories, dogs, and water, and the player dies in traps that are often the point of the shot, not a failure of orientation. No dialogue explains why the world looks like this. Animation, sound, and when the camera lets you finish a run are the entire direction.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/455-cover-1.jpg)

Playdead, the Danish studio of Arnt Jensen after Limbo in 2010, released Inside in 2016 first on Xbox One and on computer. A small team again builds a side view, but leaves the fog silhouette for more concrete and crueler institutions. The physics of water, of a crowd, and of dragged bodies is the puzzle technology. The player does not open an inventory; they push, swim, and at one moment take over other bodies as a tool.

The decision to stay silent means the player assembles a political reading of control and experiment on their own. The consequence is a game that gets quoted for its shots, not for a line of dialogue. Death is cheap and often funny precisely in its precision. The studio lets you fall because the fall is a way to understand the next jump.

![Artwork](/img/worthwhile-games/media/455-artwork-2.jpg)

In 2016 this is the studio's second sentence after Limbo, more confident and more generous in its set pieces. Beside the open worlds it reminds you that a side camera can still lead attention better than a map. The final transformation of the player's body is a risk that changes the verb of the whole game. Anyone who expected only a longer escape gets a different kind of movement and a different kind of guilt.

Few of the systems have aged. Some puzzles are known years later as famous shots, so the surprise fades, but the craft of timing remains. The silence and the fluidity hold. It is still the reference game for how to carry the theme of institutions without a single caption.

Light and sound lead before an arrow does. When the game wants you to be afraid of the water, it does not say so with an icon, but with the surface. In class, play it a minute at a time and stop. Ask what in the shot is a rule and what is only a mood; in Inside those often turn out to be the same thing.

The developers created a temporal anti-aliasing filter for the engine, entitled "temporal reprojection", to create a signature look for Inside. Martin Stig Andersen, with SØS Gunver Ryberg, composed and designed Inside s soundtrack, returning from Limbo.

Instead, he created the music by routing sound through a human skull and recording the result, a "bone-conducting sound" that created a "sombre, chill quality" that often complements Inside s visuals. Inside has a close integration of the game-play and audio, with some puzzles set directly to visual-aural cues.

505 Games published Inside and Limbo as a dual-game retail package for Xbox One and PlayStation 4, which was released in September 2017. Inside received universal acclaim, according to video game review aggregator website Metacritic.

Jaz Rignall of USgamer previewed Inside and wrote that it was one of the best puzzle platformers he has played, even better than its predecessor. In 2022, PC Gamer listed Inside as one of the best PC games, with editor Rich Stanton stating the game was "one of the very best experiences I've had in gaming", and contributing writers stating the game was "the perfect narrative sidescroller", and "had one of the best endings of any game".
