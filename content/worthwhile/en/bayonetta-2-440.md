Bayonetta fights through angelic and demonic worlds to save Jeanne. It tuned Witch Time, weapon switching, and the spectacle of the first game into an extraordinarily pure action system.

Bayonetta in the second game cuts down angels and demons again, and the player holds a long combo through dodges, weapon changes, and Witch Time, which here becomes even more a reward for insolence. Umbran Climax and switching weapon sets on the hands and the feet extend the sentence from the first game. It is still about not losing the rhythm when the scene around you is deliberately absurdly large.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/440-cover-1.jpg)

PlatinumGames' game was directed by Yusuke Hashimoto, Hideki Kamiya supervised it, and in 2014 Nintendo published it as a Wii U exclusive, because the sequel did not find its original publisher in Sega. The combat system is still the handwork of windows and animations. The Wii U as a platform can carry the game technically, but the small installed base means the pure action reaches a smaller audience than it deserves until the Switch port arrives in 2018.

The decision to tune the first game instead of looking for a new genre means the spectacle grows and the rules get cleaner. The consequence is an action game that series players often take as the peak of control, not as a bigger story. The journey for Jeanne, and between heaven and the inferno, is a pretext for set pieces. The studio knows the reason to play is the weapon in the hand, not a political map.

In 2014 the Wii U exclusivity is a strange historical rescue: Nintendo buys a sequel to someone else's very un-Nintendo game and thereby allows it to exist at all. The tone stays overshot and deliberately comic. Anyone waiting for a more serious Bayonetta is waiting wrong; anyone who wants cleaner combo sentences gets them.

What has aged above all is the hardware the game was originally stuck on. On the later port that debt is less visible, and the combat remains. Witch Time, weapon switching, and the courage to be a short dense campaign all hold. Beside the open worlds of the same year this is almost arcade purity.

Difficulty and rankings still invite you to repeat a chapter. The reward is execution, not new loot in an inventory. In class, set it immediately after the first game and ask what happens when a sequel is not allowed to change the verb, only to play it more cleanly and on an unexpected console.
