Players live various professions in a persistent Star Wars world. The original skill-based professions, crafting, and player cities represented an extraordinarily sandbox-like MMO model.

In Star Wars Galaxies you live in a persistent Star Wars world as someone who does not have to be a Jedi. The original professions mix in a skill tree, crafting depends on other players, and the inhabitants build the cities themselves. The cantina, where an entertainer buffs you, is a system, not a joke. A social role has a mechanical price, because without it a fighter is weaker.

Sony Online Entertainment published it in 2003 with LucasArts, and Raph Koster led the creative direction. It is a sandbox MMO at the moment when the genre is about to narrow into clear quests. The consequence of player cities and interdependent crafting is that the economy needs other bodies. A loner here is not a hero; he is a hole in the chain.

In 2005 the New Game Enhancements arrived and the game was rewritten toward simpler classes and toward Jedi as the main power. Many players of the original model took that as an exchange of a sandbox for an amusement park. The servers were shut down in 2011. Historically one studies mainly the version before that rewrite, and emulators keep it alive for that reason.

The original combat and interface have aged. The idea that a profession is a network and not three class buttons has not aged, as a warning and as a model. For MMO students it is a pair with EVE and a counterpoint to what World of Warcraft will do a year later with accessibility.

The Star Wars license pulled an expectation that you would be a knight with a sword. The design of the first years deliberately went elsewhere, and that tension defines the game. When a system is rewritten under living players, it is not an ordinary patch. It is an exchange of the social contract, and Galaxies is a textbook of what that exchange costs.

The player cities show that architecture can be content. A street someone else built is stronger lore than another expansion. When you talk about it, separate 2003 from 2005. Otherwise you mix two games that share a name and do not agree on whom you are allowed to be in them.
