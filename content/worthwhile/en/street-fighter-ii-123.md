Eight fighters with distinct styles compete in one-on-one matches. The combination of differing characters, special inputs, and combo potential defined the modern competitive fighting game.

You choose one of eight fighters and in one match you read distance, timing, and a special motion the opponent must not read in advance. Six buttons divide the strength of punches and kicks. A character is not a skin; it is a whole sentence of feet and hands that you learn by heart.

Capcom sent the game to arcades in 1991 on the CPS board. Yoshiki Okamoto was the producer, and Akira Nishitani and Akira Yasuda sat on the design. The hardware holds large sprites, mirrors, and bonus barrels. It is not 3D; it is a plane on which every pixel of a strike has been agreed.

The decision to give each fighter their own inputs has a consequence from which the competitive genre grows. A match is knowledge of the opponent's sentence, not just a faster fist. Combos became the language of the game because players began to read them out of cancelled animations. Capcom then took that language seriously in later revisions.

In 1991 Street Fighter II takes over the arcades. The beat 'em up for two players side by side gives way to a match in which you stand against each other. Four bosses behind the world warriors keep the single-player mode as an exam. The real life of the game, though, is in two coins and a rematch.

What has aged is the balance of the first versions. Some characters and some charge motions are, in the early revisions, harsher than the design intended as a fair game. What has not aged is the neutrality of distance. You still learn when you are in range and when a special is a legible punishment.

The mirror and the bonus rounds are a rest, but also an advertisement. Capcom knows the player has to receive, between matches, a share of skill that is not just a life bar in the corner of the screen. It is the second installment in the Street Fighter series and the sequel to 1987's Street Fighter. Street Fighter II vastly improved many of the concepts introduced in the first game, including the use of special command-based moves, a combo system, a six-button configuration, and a wider selection of playable characters, each with a unique fighting style.

Street Fighter II is regarded as one of the most influential video games of all time, and the most important fighting game in particular.

The release of Street Fighter II in 1991 is often considered a revolutionary moment in the fighting game genre. Whereas previous games allow players to combat a variety of computer-controlled fighters, Street Fighter II allows human combat.

The popularity of Street Fighter II surprised the gaming industry, as arcade owners bought more machines to keep up with demand. Street Fighter II also influenced the development of the combat mechanics of beat 'em up game Streets of Rage 2.

However, Street Fighter II also received criticism for its depiction of street violence, and for having inspired numerous other violent games in the industry.
