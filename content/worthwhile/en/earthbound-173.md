Shigesato Itoi finished the game in 1994 with Ape and HAL Laboratory for Nintendo on the SNES. In Japan it is Mother 2; in America it comes out a year later as EarthBound. The engine is a classic Japanese RPG with a map and a battle. What is unusual is the tone and that meter, not the way experience is calculated.

The decision to set an apocalypse into everyday life has a consequence. A joke from an advertisement and the horror of Giygas sit in one playthrough, because the world is not a castle. In 1994, beside Final Fantasy VI, this is a conscious alternative. Square builds an opera and an empire; Itoi builds a town, a playground, and an ending that experiments with form.

EarthBound, originally released in Japan as is a 1994 role-playing video game developed by Ape Inc. Its returning staff from EarthBound Beginnings (1989) included writer/director Shigesato Itoi and lead programmer Satoru Iwata, as well as composers Keiichi Suzuki and Hirokazu Tanaka, who incorporated a diverse range of styles into the soundtrack, including salsa, reggae, and dub.

Most of the other staff members had not worked on the original EarthBound Beginnings, and the game came under repeated threats of cancellation until Iwata joined the team. Like its predecessor, EarthBound is themed around an idiosyncratic portrayal of Americana and Western culture, subverting popular role-playing game traditions by featuring a modern setting while parodying numerous staples of the genre.

Since the original Mother had not yet been released outside Japan on the Nintendo Entertainment System before 2015, Mother 2 was called EarthBound to avoid confusion about what it was a sequel to. Although it was positively received by Japanese audiences, EarthBound sold quite poorly in the United States.

EarthBound was later made available worldwide on the Wii U Virtual Console in 2013, 3DS Virtual Console in 2016, the SNES Classic in 2017, and the Nintendo Classics service in February 2022. EarthBound features many traditional role-playing game elements: the player controls a party of 4 characters who travel through the game's two-dimensional world composed of villages, cities, caves, and dungeons.

Reviewing the game years after its release, writers described the game as "original" or "unique", Jeremy Parish of USgamer called EarthBound "the all-time champion" of self-aware games that "warp ... The few role-playing games set in real-world settings, PC Gamer has written, are often and accurately described as having been influenced by EarthBound.

Japanese writer Hiromi Kawakami told Itoi that she had played EarthBound "about 80 times". Other fan efforts include EarthBound, USA, a full-length documentary on Starmen.net and the fan community, and Mother 4, a fan-produced sequel to the Mother series that went into production when Itoi definitively "declared" that he was done with the series.

Books that have been written about EarthBound include Ken Baumann's Earthbound, by Boss Fight Books, and Legends of Localization Book 2: Earthbound, by Clyde Mandelin. In Europe, which did not see an EarthBound release, Ness was better known for his role in the fighting game than for his original role in the role-playing game.

Ness returned in the first sequel, Melee, alongside an EarthBound-themed item and battle arena. In 1996, Nintendo announced a sequel to EarthBound for the Nintendo 64: Mother 3 (EarthBound 64 in North America).

IGN described the series as neglected by Nintendo in North America, as EarthBound Beginnings, Mother 1+2 and Mother 3 were not released outside Japan. When Nintendo launched its digital distribution platform, Virtual Console, for the Wii in 2006, IGN expected EarthBound to be among Nintendo's highest priorities for rerelease, given the "religious" dedication of its fanbase.

As part of the anniversary celebrations for the Nintendo Entertainment System and Mother 2 in March 2013, Nintendo rereleased EarthBound for Japan on the Wii's successor, the Wii U Virtual Console. EarthBound producer Satoru Iwata soon announced a wider rerelease, citing fan interest on Nintendo's Miiverse social platform.

The game was a top-seller on the Wii U Virtual Console, and both Kotaku users and first-time EarthBound players had an "overwhelmingly positive" response to the game. In September 2017, Nintendo released the Super NES Classic Edition, which included EarthBound among its games.
