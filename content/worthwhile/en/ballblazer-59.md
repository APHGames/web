Two futuristic gliders compete for a ball in a first-person arena. Fast perspective 3D presentation showed the potential of computer sport beyond imitating real disciplines.

Two gliders in first person chase a ball across a field that runs to the horizon, and shoot it into the goal at a speed that leaves no time for a sporting ritual. The view is from the cockpit. You do not see the opponent as a piece on a plan, you see them as an object that rushes out of the depth and steals your angle.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/059-cover-1.jpg)

Lucasfilm Games developed the game alongside Rescue on Fractalus!, and Epyx published it in 1984 on the Atari 8-bit after the original deal with Atari fell with the market crash. The perspective is calculated in real time on an eight-bit machine. The field is a grid racing under you, and the ball is an object in the same space, not an icon stuck onto a flat surface.

The decision to invent a sport that does not exist had the consequence that the game does not have to imitate football or hockey. The rule is speed and direction, and a spectator understands it without a league table. The music is composed as it goes, not as a loop from a tape. The sound too is a system, which belongs to Lucasfilm's ambition to look like a laboratory, not like a transcription of the arcade.

In 1984 computer sport is mostly a transcription of tennis or racing. Ballblazer says that a computer can have a discipline of its own, one that would make no sense on grass. Two players splitting attention are still the goal, even if the home machine often ends up with one person against the program.

What has aged is the severity of the speed, which will not let a newcomer into the tactics, and a picture that, without the astonishment of the time, looks like a poor grid. What has not aged is first person as a sports camera: you play an angle, not an overview.

A thread runs from this field to games that invent a sport instead of licensing an existing league. In a seminar, let two students play for a minute with no explanation of the rules. If they start shouting about the goal, the design has explained itself.
