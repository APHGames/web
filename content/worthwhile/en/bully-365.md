Jimmy Hopkins survives a school year at Bullworth Academy. A class schedule and a small-town school show that a GTA-like structure can work without an automotive metropolis.

In Bully, Jimmy Hopkins is at Bullworth Academy and the day has a schedule. A class in English, chemistry, or gym is a minigame that gives you an ability, and the break is an open yard with cliques. Nerds, jocks, bullies, preppies, and the town beyond the wall are factions. Respect with one group spoils respect with another, and the school thereby has politics without a single metropolitan car.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/365-cover-1.jpg)

Rockstar Vancouver published it in 2006 on the PlayStation 2. In parts of Europe it came out as Canis Canem Edit, because the word Bully looked like a problem before anyone had launched the game. The consequence of the schedule is that a GTA structure works without an automotive metropolis. A small town and a campus are enough if they have a time regime and a social map.

The controversy around violence at school arrived before release and concerned the title and the place, not some specific scene the game was hiding. The context is part of the reception of 2006. The camera and some of the repeated tasks have aged. The schedule as a world and the small social map have not, and they still show that a sandbox does not have to be a state.

The bike, the slingshot, and chemistry are a child's tools, not an arsenal. The consequence is that conflict has the scale of a locker room and therefore feels more personal than a gang war over a district. For open-world students it is proof that the Rockstar tool can be carried elsewhere. When you remove the city and leave the systems of respect, the day, and factions, the game still stands.

The school year is a chapter, not an endless day. The structure holds the campaign in a time the player knows from their own life, and therefore it is satirical even without a large lore. The Scholarship Edition later carries the game to other platforms. The original PlayStation 2 version is still the one that in 2006 sits beside San Andreas as a smaller sibling.

The English in class and the dialogue in the yard are two registers. The game can be a school joke and, at the same time, a small drama about who is allowed to sit at which table. When you play it today, miss a class on purpose and watch what the school does. The whole argument that time is a map is in that punishment.

Bully (released in the PAL region as ; Latin for 'dog eat dog') is a 2006 action-adventure video game developed by Rockstar Vancouver and published by Rockstar Games. Bully s expected violence and sexual content were initially controversial.

Bully was re-released for the PlayStation 4, available via the PlayStation Network, on 22 March 2016. Bully is an action-adventure game set in an open world environment and played from a third-person perspective.
