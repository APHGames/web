D&D adventurers share a graphical online world of the Forgotten Realms. One of the important intermediate steps between text MUDs and graphical MMORPGs.

Through America Online you share a graphical world of the Forgotten Realms with strangers. The character remains even after you close the client. The rules are still D&D in the spirit of Gold Box: a portrait, combat, a dungeon. What is new is that someone is standing in the corridor whom a script does not control.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/129-cover-1.jpg)

Don Daglow's Stormfront Studios launched the game in 1991 with SSI on the AOL network. It ran until 1997, across the whole period when graphical online worlds were only just being born. The engine is tile-based and derived from the single-player games on the license, not a free three-dimensional world. The technology serves persistence, not spectacle.

The decision to close the world inside a commercial service that bills for time has a consequence. A community arises, but only among those who are inside that service at all. In 1991 text MUDs are already settled, and Ultima Online is yet to come. Neverwinter Nights is an intermediate step: an image, a license, and a shared map.

Do not confuse this title with BioWare's Neverwinter Nights from 2002. The name is the same; the contract with the player is different: here there is no module editor for one person, here there is a service for many. What has aged is the Gold Box graphics and the dependence on a single provider. When the service ended, the world ended with it.

What has not aged is the desire to meet a stranger in a licensed world, not just a monster from a table. The dungeon and the town keep a social role. Someone sells, someone invites you into a party, someone merely stands in the square, because persistence does not yet know today's assembly of activities.

When you draw the path to the MMORPG, put this AOL map between the MUD and Ultima Online. Without it, it looks as if the graphical world fell from the sky in 1997. Daglow's studio is running a service here, not just a box. The lesson is that an online game is a service that has to be fed even after release.

Neverwinter Nights is a graphical massively multiplayer online role-playing game developed by Beyond Software and published by Strategic Simulations. Neverwinter Nights was developed with gameplay similar to previous games in the Gold Box series.

Neverwinter Nights was a co-development of AOL, Beyond Software, SSI, and TSR. Daglow chose Neverwinter as the game's location because of its magical features (a river of warm water that flowed from a snowy forest into a northern sea), and its location near a wide variety of terrain types.

In 2008 Neverwinter Nights was honored (along with EverQuest and World of Warcraft) at the 59th Annual Technology & Engineering Emmy Awards for advancing the art form of MMORPG games. It is this committed fan base that BioWare sought when they licensed the rights to Neverwinter Nights from AOL and TSR as the basis for the later Neverwinter Nights game.

In 1998, development work began on a fanmade clone of Neverwinter Nights called Forgotten World, which opened for play in 2003. After the release of BioWare's non-MMO Neverwinter Nights game in 2002, a group of former Neverwinter Nights players used the Aurora toolset included with the new game to reconstruct the content of the original Neverwinter Nights and host it online as a multiplayer game, albeit with limited player capacity.

Neverwinter Nights: Resurrection was modestly successful early on in drawing former Neverwinter Nights players, but player numbers dwindled over the years as online gaming options expanded and the underlying game technology aged. A post on IGN from the game's host revealed that Neverwinter Nights: Resurrection shut down its servers on July 31, 2012.

In 2012 a single-player conversion of Neverwinter Nights was released for Unlimited Adventures after two years of development. A number of games set in and around the city of Neverwinter were later released: Neverwinter Nights (2002), Neverwinter Nights 2 (2006), and Neverwinter (2013).
