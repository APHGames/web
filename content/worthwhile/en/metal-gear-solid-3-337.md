Naked Snake survives the jungle during the Cold War. The camouflage index, treatment of wounds, and survival systems give stealth an ecological dimension.

In Metal Gear Solid 3, Naked Snake is in the Soviet jungle of 1964 and camouflage has percentages. The percentage on the indicator says whether the bush, the mud, and your movement are about to give you away. Healing is a menu: you pull out a bullet, treat a burn, eat what you have hunted. Stealth receives an ecology, not only the cone of a guard's sight.

Hideo Kojima directed it at Konami in 2004, Yoji Shinkawa led the art, and Harry Gregson-Williams wrote the music. Snake's English voice is David Hayter. The consequence of the camouflage index is that terrain is equipment. A disguise is not a costume from a crate; it is a relationship between your skin and what you are kneeling on.

The camera can do the old top-down view and a freer third person. The series thereby leaves the fixed frame of the earlier games, and the jungle needs that change, because the vertical of the trees would disappear in a pure overhead view. In 2004, beside Splinter Cell, this is the other great stealth game. That one solves the light bulb; this one solves food, wounds, and loyalty in a story set before the rest of the series.

The long radio calls and some of the jokes have aged into a Kojima ritual. The camouflage index, the food, and the treatment of a wound have not. The Boss is a tragedy, not a boss in the ordinary sense. The ending forces you to reconsider whom you have been obeying the whole time, and that is a narrative construction, not merely a fight in a meadow.

For stealth students the jungle is a lesson that the enemy can be the environment. A guard is only one of the predators. CQC, close combat, is a third branch beside the pistol and the silence. The game thereby admits that pure stealth sometimes fails and that failure must have a craft of its own.

The later Subsistence release adds a freer camera. The original release is still the one that in 2004 taught players to look at the percentages in the grass. Written, produced and directed by Hideo Kojima, the game is the fifth mainline installment in the Metal Gear series and the first chronologically, serving as a prequel to the entire series. Set in 1964, 31 years before the events of the original Metal Gear, the story centers on the FOX operative codenamed Naked Snake as he attempts to rescue Russian rocket scientist Nikolai Stepanovich Sokolov, sabotage an experimental superweapon, and assassinate his defected former mentor.

While previous games were set in a primarily urban environment, Snake Eater adopts a 1960s Soviet jungle setting, with the high-tech, near-future trappings of previous Metal Gear Solid games replaced with wilderness.
