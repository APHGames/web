Dante fights through a gothic castle with a combination of sword and firearms. A style rating turned efficient combat into an expressive performance and founded the modern character-action school.

In a gothic castle Dante cuts with a sword and fires in the gaps between blows. The weapons are not switched like tools; they are two halves of one sentence. After a fight you receive a style grade. Surviving is not enough: the game watches whether you vary attacks or repeat one safe hit.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/303-cover-1.jpg)

Hideki Kamiya of Capcom directed it and Shinji Mikami produced it. The project began as an attempt at Resident Evil 4 and only later became its own game. On the PlayStation 2 in 2001 there was not yet a settled grammar of three-dimensional action. The studio had to build target lock, the jump, and Devil Trigger itself.

The style grade separated efficient killing from expressive killing. That difference outlived the castle and founded the school later called character action. The chambers, the camera, and some of the jumps look clumsy today. The style meter, the alternation of sword and guns, and the appetite for showing off have not aged.

In the same year the big games were showing what three-dimensional space could do with a map. This one shows what it can do with a body that must stay readable in motion. The difficulty does not hide a mistake behind a statistic. When an enemy knocks you out of the air, you can see which blow you let finish.

From here Kamiya carried the line through Viewtiful Joe to the later PlatinumGames. The public beginning of that school is in this castle, not only in its smoother sequels. The locks and the talk between fights are seasoning of the period. What is still copied is the feeling that a fight has an audience in the form of a style rank.

The player learns to cancel their own animations and keep an enemy in the air. Combat becomes a craft, not merely a test of supplies. After the modern sequels you will see a rougher camera in it and, at the same time, the whole alphabet that those sequels only multiplied.

Devil May Cry was conceived by Capcom developers as a possible concept for Resident Evil 4. Devil May Cry received prominent coverage in the video game media due to the impact it had in the action-adventure genre, its high difficulty, and the high overall scores given to it by professional reviewers.

Devil May Cry would go on to spawn a franchise, consisting of four sequels and a reboot, and two animated series. Devil May Cry consists of levels called "missions", where players must fight numerous enemies, perform platforming tasks, and occasionally solve puzzles to progress through the story.

Devil May Cry started out as the earliest incarnation of Resident Evil 4. Devil May Cry was developed by Team Little Devils, a group of staff members within Capcom Production Studio 4.

According to the director, Devil May Cry was designed from the ground up around Dante's acrobatics and combat abilities. Devil May Cry s difficulty was intentional, according to Kamiya, who called it his "challenge to those who played light, casual games".

According to Eurogamer, an earlier Capcom arcade video game, Strider (1989), was a vital influence on Devil May Cry. According to Retro Gamer, the over-the-top action of Devil May Cry draws from Strider.

Devil May Cry received a "Gold" sales award from the Entertainment and Leisure Software Publishers Association (ELSPA), indicating sales of at least 200,000 copies in the United Kingdom. By July 2006, Devil May Cry had sold 1.1 million copies and earned $38 million in the United States alone.

Combined sales of the Devil May Cry series reached 2 million units in the United States by July 2006. Devil May Cry received critical acclaim, with reviews typically praising its gameplay innovations, action, visuals, camera control, and gothic ambience.

Devil May Cry also frequents several Top Video Games of All Time lists. Gamefury, for instance, listed Devil May Cry at #31 in their Top 40 Console Games of All Time feature.

On October 15, 2004, three years after its release, a soundtrack containing its music was released alongside the soundtrack to Devil May Cry 2. Plans for a PlayStation Portable installment, tentatively titled Devil May Cry Series, and a live action film adaptation have been announced, although it was later confirmed in 2009 that the PSP adaptation of Devil May Cry was officially cancelled.

A reboot titled DmC: Devil May Cry was released in 2013 by Ninja Theory and Capcom. Kamiya considers his 2009 video game Bayonetta to have evolved from Devil May Cry although he played the sequel Devil May Cry 4 when developing it.

In a 2017 interview with Dengeki PlayStation, Kamiya expressed interest in making a remake of Devil May Cry. Devil May Cry was ported to the Nintendo Switch on June 25, 2019, worldwide and on June 27, 2019, in Japan.

Devil May Cry has been cited as the beginning of a subgenre of arcade-style hack and slash melee focused action-adventure games called "character action" or "Extreme Combat", which focus on powerful heroes fighting hordes of foes with a focus on stylish action.
