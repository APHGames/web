Robert Foster uncovers the dystopian secret of Union City. An intelligent sci-fi script and a clearer interface represent a European alternative to the schools of LucasArts and Sierra.

Robert Foster falls into Union City and assembles a dystopia from conversations and objects, while the opening comic was enough to tell you who raised him and why the city lies. The interface is cleaner than a forest of verbs. Revolution wants you to click the world, not to hunt for the right word in a bar.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/168-cover-1.jpg)

Charles Cecil and Revolution Software released the game in 1994 through Virgin. The drawings of Dave Gibbons, an author students know from comics, hold the visual identity from the first page. The Virtual Theatre engine, which the studio had used before, holds characters in a space that is not just a static screen with a hotspot. It is still an adventure, not an action game.

The decision to join a comics prologue to a sparer interface has a consequence. The European adventure stands here beside LucasArts and Sierra as a third tone: a drier joke, a clearer click. In 1994 the genre is at its peak and at the same time before a slimming down. Beneath a Steel Sky tries the slimming without throwing out the puzzle.

Union City is a British dystopia, not the American noir of Gabriel Knight. Industry, class, and sarcasm are local, even if the science-fiction scenery is general. What has aged are some period jokes and puzzles that want a particular object in a particular pocket. The craft is solid; the seam sometimes creaks.

What has not aged is the joining of drawing and city. Gibbons holds the world together even where a sentence ages. Revolution later released the game for free. The long life is not a marketing story; it is proof that someone will still start the interface.

Compare the density of the text with Monkey Island 2. Gilbert's joke is in the character; Cecil's is in the system of a city that pretends to be friendly and is not. In a seminar, start with the comic, not with the first click. You will see that the game assumes a reader, not only a player of reflexes.

Beneath a Steel Sky is a 1994 point-and-click adventure game developed by British developer Revolution Software and published by Virgin Interactive Entertainment for MS-DOS and Amiga home computers. A remastered edition was released for iOS in 2009 as Beneath a Steel Sky Remastered, which also received a positive reception from the gaming press.

Entitled Beyond a Steel Sky, it was released on Apple Arcade in June 2020, on Steam in July 2020, and on GOG.com in March 2021. Beneath a Steel Sky is a 2D adventure game played from a third-person perspective.

The production values became much higher for Beneath a Steel Sky than for Lure of the Temptress, resulting in a game six times larger, and by the end of 1993, the team working on the game had grown to eleven. According to Cecil, the original version of the engine seemed less applicable in Beneath a Steel Sky, as the ability to issue commands conflicted with the gameplay they intended to create.

Lure of the Temptress had one story that was moved forward by a key event, whereas Beneath a Steel Sky had multiple threads. Beneath a Steel Sky was published in March 1994 by Virgin Interactive on floppy disks and CD-ROM.

In July 2009, Revolution announced that a remastered edition of Beneath a Steel Sky would be released on iOS later that year. Slide To Play Keith Andrew said that "Beneath a Steel Sky somehow feels bigger and bolder than its rivals, raising the bar and highlighting what others have so far failed to achieve.

Beneath a Steel Sky: Remastered has been placed on numerous top lists as well, including Edge s Top 50 iPhone Games at 26th in 2009, as well as Pocket Gamer s Top 10 point-and-click adventure games on iPhone and iPad and Mashable's 10 Classic PC Games That Found New Life on the iPhone, both in 2010. Beneath a Steel Sky is often referenced in Revolution's Broken Sword games, including 1997's Broken Sword II: The Smoking Mirror, in 2009's Broken Sword: The Shadow of the Templars – Director's Cut and 2013/2014's Broken Sword 5: The Serpent's Curse, in the form of an easter egg.

In 2004, Cecil commented "Beneath a Steel Sky 2 is a project Revolution has been considering for a while, and has started to move forward on, but we are unable to comment beyond this." In an interview with Eurogamer in 2006, Cecil spoke of his admiration for the work done by ScummVM and the resulting interest in a sequel. He also stated that if he were to make the game he "would dearly love to work with Dave Gibbons again." In a February 20, 2009 interview with IGN UK about the Wii and DS versions of Broken Sword: The Director's Cut, Cecil and Gibbons re-iterated their interest in a sequel to Beneath a Steel Sky.

On September 25, 2012, Revolution announced that Beneath a Steel Sky 2 would be greenlit if their Broken Sword 5: The Serpent's Curse Kickstarter reached $1 million. Despite Broken Sword: The Serpent's Curse failing to meet the $1 million 'stretch goal', Revolution announced that the success of its crowdfunding campaign had inspired them to greenlight Beneath a Steel Sky 2.

However, Revolution later clarified that Beneath a Steel Sky 2 will not necessarily be the studio's first project following Broken Sword 5 and that they would "think" about what their next game will be after finishing the Broken Sword game. In February 2014 it was reported that Beneath a Steel Sky 2 had never entered production, despite previous reports to the contrary.

On March 25, 2019, Revolution announced that Beyond a Steel Sky would be released on Apple Arcade (iOS, macOS, tvOS) later in 2019.
