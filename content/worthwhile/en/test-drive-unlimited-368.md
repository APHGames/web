The player drives across a vast Hawaiian island with other online drivers. It naturally linked open-world driving with a persistent online presence — an early "massively open online racing."

In Test Drive Unlimited you drive across the island of Oahu, and on the same roads you meet other players' cars. The race is not a separated track; it is an appointment in a persistent world. Houses, clubs, and the showrooms of brands are progress. The career is social: a car in the garage makes sense because someone on the road will see it.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/368-cover-1.jpg)

Eden Games of Lyon published it in 2006 through Atari on the Xbox 360. The island is a reduced network of real roads, and that geography in particular holds the feeling that you are driving somewhere, not in a circle. The consequence of the persistent presence of others is a race that behaves like an MMO. The studio called it massively open online driving, and historically it is a predecessor of later games that join a city and a network more naturally.

In 2006, beside Forza and Gran Turismo, which still think in tracks and in an offline career, this is a different sentence. A car is an avatar in a landscape, not only a machine for a lap. The physics is a cruise and has aged beside stricter simulations. The idea of an island on which you are not alone has not, and Test Drive Unlimited 2 repeats it in 2011.

The police and points for driving style give the open road a rule. Without them the world would be only a pretty map between race icons. For online-world students it is a class about massiveness not having to mean a war. It is enough that a stranger's car drives on your road for the city to come alive.

The brand shops are identity. The choice of a car is a social signal more than in a pure simulation, where a car is judged only by a stopwatch. The map is large enough that the road between races is content. Whoever teleports misses the reason Eden built the island at all.

The console of that generation is only finding out what to do with a persistent connection. This game is one of the first answers in a genre that used to be offline. When you play it today, drive for a while without a race and watch whether you meet a stranger's car. If you do, you see the contract of 2006. If you do not, a landscape remains.

Test Drive Unlimited is a 2006 racing video game developed by Eden Games for Xbox 360 and Windows, with Atari Melbourne House developed the PlayStation 2 and PlayStation Portable versions, and published by Atari. Being the eighteenth entry in the Test Drive series, Unlimited serves as a reboot of the franchise, discarding the continuity of the previous games.

A third game and soft reboot, Test Drive Unlimited Solar Crown, was released in 2024. In the online world of Test Drive Unlimited, players are able to drive both on-road and off-road in free-roam mode, challenging other players in real-time anywhere on the drivable map.

It is also possible to drive on small islands outside of the main island. Following the third free update, Test Drive Unlimited supports force feedback wheel controllers such as Microsoft's Xbox 360 Wireless Racing Wheel.

There were 1500 total beta testers in Atari's closed beta for Test Drive Unlimited for PlayStation 2. At the end of the testing period, Atari awarded 30 of the most active testers with "immortality" in the PlayStation 2 version of Test Drive Unlimited.

Test Drive Unlimited received "generally favorable" reviews, according to review aggregator website Metacritic.
