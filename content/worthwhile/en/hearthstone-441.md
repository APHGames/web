Players build decks from Warcraft cards and compete in fast duels. The digital medium allows random and dynamic effects that are hard to achieve with physical cards; the UX dramatically lowered the barrier of the CCG.

In Hearthstone you build a deck from Warcraft cards and, in a fast duel, play creatures onto a board where mana grows by one crystal each turn. The player does not count hit points with a pencil; the game itself resolves effects, random summons, and destroyed neighbors. A turn has a timer and an animation. Even a simple attack is a small piece of theater, which is why a match can be watched without a rules judge.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/441-screenshot-1.jpg)

Blizzard released the game in March 2014, and Ben Brode became the public face of the design. It is free, with heroes from a world the studio has been teaching players for twenty years, and from the start it also aims at the iPad. The digital medium allows effects a paper card could not keep track of: a random target, a summon from the whole set, a transformation of the board. The drag-a-card interface lowers the threshold that Magic and similar games still have in the rules.

The decision to hide the rulebook inside animation means a newcomer plays before understanding every interaction. The consequence is an enormous entrance into the CCG, and also arguments about how much randomness may decide a match. Decks and expansions change the meta across seasons. The game is not a finished tournament list; it is a service that keeps printing new cards onto the same board.

In 2014 this is Blizzard's answer to the fact that its players want a short competition beside raids and ladders. A match fits into a break, not into an evening like Dota. Warcraft characters are readable silhouettes of abilities. Even without lore you can tell who heals and who hits, which is the same lesson as their hero shooter two years later.

The economy of decks has aged, as have expansion power creep and the parts of the meta where randomness beats preparation. The original client also feels today like an older layer of Blizzard. The readability of the board holds, and so does the sense that a card does exactly what it shows. That is why Hearthstone is still used to explain how a digital card game should teach.

Tavern Brawl and later modes show that the rules of the duel can also bear a temporary joke. The base ladder nevertheless remains the more serious contract. In class, set it beside a physical CCG and ask which rules sentence paper cannot handle, and which one digital instead hides so thoroughly that the player stops understanding why they lost.

Hearthstone is a 2014 free-to-play digital collectible card video game produced by Blizzard Entertainment. Originally subtitled Heroes of Warcraft, Hearthstone builds upon the existing lore of the Warcraft series by using the same elements, characters, and relics.

It has been a critical and commercial success, with Blizzard reporting more than 100 million Hearthstone players as of November 2018, and the game has become popular as an esport, with cash prize tournaments hosted by Blizzard and other organizers. In contrast to other games developed by Blizzard, Hearthstone was an experimental game developed by a smaller team based on the appreciation of collectible card games at the company.

Set within the Warcraft universe, Hearthstone is a digital-only, turn-based collectible card game which pits two opponents against each other. Unlike other card games such as Magic: The Gathering, Hearthstone was designed to speed up play by eliminating any manual reactions from the opposing player during a player's turn, and setting a timer for each player's turn.

Starting in 2020 and ongoing, Blizzard started to view Hearthstone as a platform for multiple game modes rather than fixed around the main one-on-one game. Hearthstone was first announced with the subtitle Heroes of Warcraft at Penny Arcade Expo in March 2013 for Windows, Mac, and iPad, with an expected release date in the same year.

To mark the release of Hearthstone, Blizzard released the Hearthsteed mount for World of Warcraft players that is obtained by winning three games in Arena or Play mode. Widely advertised on various World of Warcraft websites, this promotion encourages players to try Hearthstone, and marked the first significant crossover implemented between Blizzard games.

An alternate hero for Shaman, Morgl the Oracle, is available through Hearthstone "Recruit A Friend" program after the recruited friend reaches level 20. Thanks to the designers' focus on accessibility and fast-paced gameplay, Hearthstone has been the focus of a number of tournaments.

Hearthstone was the focus of a number of other tournaments during its closed beta, including those hosted by Major League Gaming and ESL. In March 2014, The esports organization Tespa announced the Collegiate Hearthstone Open, a free-to-enter tournament open to all North American college students, featuring $5,000 in scholarships.

Major League Gaming, ESL and the ZOTAC Cup all continue to regularly host minor Hearthstone leagues in the North American and European territories with small or no prize pools aimed at everyday players. The Hearthstone World Championship 2014 featured a total prize pool of $250,000, and the American winner, James "Firebat" Kostesich, received $100,000.

The second Hearthstone World Championship was held at BlizzCon 2015 on November 7 with players selected in a similar way in the previous year and it was played in the best-of-five conquest format; the Swede winner, Sebastian "Ostkaka" Engwall, received $100,000. The fifth Hearthstone World Championship took place in April 2019 and was held in Taipei; the winner was Norwegian Casper "Hunterace" Notto that received $250,000.

The first Hearthstone Grandmasters Global Finals was held at BlizzCon 2019; the Chinese winner was Xiaomeng "VKLiooon" Li, who received $200,000; VKLiooon was the first woman to win the Hearthstone World Championship and to also win any BlizzCon tournament. The seventh Hearthstone World Championship winner was Japanese Kenta "Glory" Sato who received $200,000.

Hearthstone has also been a part of a number of esport demonstration events at international competitions, such as the 2017 Asian Indoor and Martial Arts Games and 2018 Asian Games. Hearthstone received "universal acclaim" on iOS and "generally favorable" reviews for PC, according to review aggregator Metacritic.

A class-action lawsuit was brought against Blizzard in May 2022, asserting that Hearthstone encourages minors to spend non-refundable money on card packs as to try to get the rarer cards, which violates the California Family Code.
