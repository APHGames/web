Large teams fight on land, at sea, and in the air. Infantry and directly controllable vehicles on vast conquest maps established Battlefield's character.

On a large map you run as infantry, climb into a tank, a plane, or a ship, and fight over points whose loss eats your team's tickets. The classes are simple and the vehicles are objects in the same world, not a reward from a menu. Infantry and machine meet on one conquest field.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/309-cover-1.jpg)

Swedish DICE made it and Electronic Arts published it in 2002. It runs on the Refractor engine, which had to carry dozens of players and vehicles at once. Conquest with tickets means the map is not played for a single life but for the team's economy. Losing a point is an event for the whole side, not merely a firefight.

In 2002, large multiplayer on PC was mature mainly in smaller arenas. This game shifted the scale so that a plane could decide an infantry point. The Desert Combat mod later showed that the same skeleton could carry a modern war. DICE grew from that lesson into later games in the series.

The models and the vehicle physics are of their time. The loop of taking a point, bringing a tank, and covering infantry has not aged, and it still defines the Battlefield name. Beaches, desert airfields, and harbors teach that terrain is both a promise and a trap for a vehicle. Map design matters more here than the design of a single weapon.

A solo game with bots exists, but the point is the human count. Without occupied seats in the tank and the plane, the system shows only half of itself. For multiplayer students it is a break. The shooter stops being a duel of two bodies and starts being the management of a machine in the middle of a battle.

Team agreement here does not yet have the later commander with a map. Already, though, you can see that without it the tickets merely fade away. Today the directness is surprising. Everything the series later boasted of is here in a rough and still readable version.

Battlefield 1942 is a 2002 first-person shooter game developed by Digital Illusions CE and published by Electronic Arts for Microsoft Windows and Mac OS X. The maps in Battlefield 1942 are based on real battles and are somewhat realistically portrayed.

Upon release, Battlefield 1942 received generally favorable reviews, with particular praise directed towards the innovative gameplay, multiplayer, and World War II theme. Since its release, the game has spawned numerous sequels and spin-offs, which ultimately turned into the Battlefield series.

Battlefield 1942 is retrospectively considered to be one of the best video games of all time. The gameplay of Battlefield 1942 generally has a more co-operative focus than previous games of this nature, as it is not only important to kill the opposition but to also hold certain "control points" around the map.

Battlefield 1942 was one of the first mainstream FPS games to actively encourage teamwork and coordination over individual "lone wolf" style gameplay. In 2000, DICE acquired Refraction Games (developers of Codename Eagle) and inherited the in-development Battlefield 1942.

Battlefield 1942 was built on the formula of the less well-known and successful Codename Eagle video game, set in an alternate history World War I. The earlier Refractor 1 engine had more arcade-style physics and a less realistic focus than its successor, Refractor 2, which was used in Battlefield 2.

A Macintosh-compatible version of Battlefield 1942 was made and released by Aspyr Media in mid-2004. Two expansion packs would be released for Battlefield 1942, Battlefield 1942: The Road to Rome (adding the Italian Front) and Battlefield 1942: Secret Weapons of WWII, both adding various new gameplay modes, maps, and game concepts.

Battlefield 1942 Deluxe Edition includes the original game and Battlefield 1942: The Road To Rome, and the Battlefield 1942: World War II Anthology added Battlefield 1942: Secret Weapons of WWII expansion pack. Battlefield 1942: The Complete Collection later added Battlefield Vietnam and Battlefield Vietnam WWII Mod.

In the United States, Battlefield 1942 sold 680,000 copies and earned $27.1 million by August 2006. Combined sales of all Battlefield computer games, including Battlefield 1942, had reached 2.7 million units in the United States by August 2006.

Battlefield 1942 was the first in the Battlefield series and would go on to spawn many sequels and spin-offs set in different eras of war. Like Half-Life and some other popular FPS games, Battlefield 1942 spawned a number of mods.

Early modifications of Battlefield 1942 were produced without a software development kit. Later a "Mod Development Kit", Battlefield Mod Development Toolkit, was produced by EA to help the development of mods.

With the release of the Battlefield 1942 sequel Battlefield Vietnam and Battlefield 2, some mods have released a new version or have continued development with that game. Battlefield Vietnam uses an updated version of the Refractor 2 game engine.

Some mods have switched to the computer games Söldner: Secret Wars, Half-Life 2 while others were releasing a standalone game after completed mod development for Battlefield 1942 (Eve of Destruction - REDUX and FinnWars ).
