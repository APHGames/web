A space marine fights demons on Mars and in hell. Fast pseudo-3D action, networked deathmatch, WAD modification, and shareware distribution fundamentally shaped PC games; the original release was on DOS in December 1993.

On Mars and in hell you run with a shotgun, circle a demon, and collect keys in maps that are both a maze and an arena. The view is pseudo-3D. Rooms sit on a floor plan, the enemy is a sprite, and the original game does not let you look freely up and down.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/143-cover-1.jpg)

A WAD is a data pack the player can replace. The decision to separate data from the engine has a consequence: death in the game is not the end of the game, because on Sunday someone else's map comes out. Deathmatch on a network teaches that a shooter is a sport of two people, not only a quest. Client against client is still a technical feat in 1993.

The same year, Myst is the quiet opposite. Doom shouts, runs, and lends its tools; Myst hides the story in the image. Both, though, say that the PC is no longer an office. Shareware repeats the path of Keen and Wolfenstein. Mass reach is not a television advertisement; it is a floppy someone copies.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/143-screenshot-2.jpg)

What has aged is the absence of height and the demon sprites. The space that frightened people then is today legible as a theater of flats once you compare it with Quake. What has not aged is the tempo of the arena and people's appetite for making their own maps. The level-design language Doom set is still recognizable in later shooters.

Hell and the UAC base keep two palettes. The passage between them is a cut in tone, not just a different wall texture. Do not say that Doom invented the first person. Say that in 1993 it joined speed, networking, shareware, and modification into one contract with the player.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/143-screenshot-3.jpg)

In a seminar, open someone else's WAD if you have one at hand. You will see that the engine was a platform before that word became a business plan.
