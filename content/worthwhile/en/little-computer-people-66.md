The player observes and indirectly influences a virtual person living in a house. An early precedent of "dollhouse" simulations and The Sims: the character is not only the player's avatar, but an autonomous inhabitant of the system.

The player looks into a cross-section of a house where a little person lives, and influences them indirectly: brings food, pets the dog, types a message on the machine, or simply lets time run. The character is not an avatar. They have their own schedule, mood, and habit, and you are more a neighbor, or a god in the wings, than a hero who picks up a sword.

Activision published the game in 1985 on the Commodore 64. It belongs to a wave of home experiments that do not ask how to port the arcade, but whether a computer can carry someone who lives inside it. Each diskette has an inhabitant shifted in the details. The world is not one shared campaign, but a specific person you brought home in the box.

The decision not to give the player a victory had the consequence that the game is a relationship. Boredom, if you are expecting a score, is part of the contract; the reward is that the character comes to the door, or does not. The house is a cross-section like a doll's cabinet. You see the stairs, the gramophone, and the aquarium at once, because the design wants you to be spectators, not a first-person camera.

In 1985 Balance of Power is dealing with the Cold War beside it, and Ultima IV with the virtues. Little Computer People deals with something smaller and stranger: whether a stranger's day will interest you. It is not a city simulation and not a doll without rules. It is one life in one house, deliberately limited so that the relationship has an address.

What has aged is the slowness and the fragility with which the character reacts, if you expect every click to have a visible explosion of reward. What has not aged is the idea that a sim can be about someone who is not you, and that watching is a legitimate verb of a game.

The road to The Sims begins in houses like this, in which the furniture is a system and the person is not a cursor. In a seminar, let the game run for five minutes without intervention. Whoever starts talking about what the character wants has understood more than from a tutorial.
