Two brothers search for a cure for their sick father. Each analog stick controls a different brother; a change in this mechanic at the end gains a strong narrative meaning.

In Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons you hold both brothers at once, each on one stick, and lead them across the landscape for medicine for their father. The player is not a camera behind a hero, but a person who has two fates in their hands and sometimes must press them in the right rhythm. The puzzles are simple cooperation: one brother swims, the other pulls a lever, together they ride an animal. The story is told without words, through gestures and the environment.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/438-cover-1.jpg)

Starbreeze's game was directed in 2013 by Josef Fares and published by 505 Games. Fares will later found Hazelight and continue in cooperation between two players, but here those two players are your two hands. Two sticks are the entire control interface of the idea. Technically the game is not a grand engine manifesto; it is a storybook landscape in which it must be readable which brother can fall at that moment.

The decision to tie the controls to the brothers means the loss of one of them at the end is not only a cutscene. The consequence is that the sentence the hands knew how to perform changes, and the player carries out that change with the body. The younger and the older brother have different reach. The design keeps reminding you that they are not interchangeable silhouettes, even though one person holds them.

In 2013 this is a short console game that makes its way among the big action titles precisely because its idea can be explained in one sentence and finished in an afternoon. The landscape is an illustration of a journey from home to danger. The studio does not need an open world, because the theme is the relationship of two bodies side by side.

Some of the simple puzzles have aged, as have the storybook interludes, which feel shorter today. The power is concentrated in a few gestures, not in every minute equally. The final change of controls holds. It is still the cleanest example of how to give a mechanic grief without adding a sadness statistic to a menu.

One player's cooperation with themselves is a different problem from split screen. You cannot agree aloud with another person; you have to manage your own hands. In class, let students finish it. Describing the ending in words means giving away exactly the thing the hands are supposed to discover on their own.

Starbreeze sold the Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons intellectual property to 505 Games for $500,000 in January 2015. This version includes a special two-player co-operative mode where each player controls one of the two brothers in the game.

Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons received "generally favorable" reviews from critics, while the PC version received "universal acclaim", according to review aggregator website Metacritic. IGN said that Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons "might be the best downloadable title since Journey".

UK's GamesTM magazine gave Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons a score of 8/10 in its review in issue 137. Both IGN and PC Gamer stated Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons was one of the best games of 2013.

Brothers: A Tale of Two Sons had sold over 800,000 units by January 2015. Awards, the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences awarded Brothers with "Downloadable Game of the Year", along with a nomination for "Outstanding Achievement in Game Direction".
