The player hacks an abstract network synchronized with the music. Shooting, vibration, sound, and image are bound into a synesthetic feedback loop.

You fly through an abstract network and lock targets in time with the track. The shot, the controller's vibration, the bass, and the flash are meant to arrive as one event. The score is not the only language of success. Success is the feeling that your fire keeps up with the music and that the image breaks apart exactly on the beat.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/305-cover-1.jpg)

Tetsuya Mizuguchi and United Game Artists made it at Sega for the Dreamcast and soon after for the PlayStation 2. Mizuguchi brought target-locking from the Panzer Dragoon line. The game is poor in the skin of a character and rich in synesthesia. One rule, locking on, repeats across five areas, and the density and the music change.

Vibration is not a decoration on a hit. It is a third channel beside image and sound, so a break in the rhythm is something the player feels in the hand. In 2001, when consoles were showing off textures and stories, Rez builds the whole game from a wireframe model and from trance. It is a polemic against what was then taken as the next step in three-dimensional graphics.

Because it never bet on the photorealism of its day, it has barely aged visually. What aged was the hardware on which vibration and image could keep time. The later Rez Infinite and its virtual-reality area showed that the system was about perception from the start, not about one console.

The difficulty of the final stretches can break the trance and return the player to counting hits. Where the rhythm holds, it remains one of the purest examples of game synesthesia. For sound students it is a lesson that music can be the rule by which targets arrive, not a bed under someone else's rules.

The short length is part of the form. Trance cannot be stretched into a long campaign without turning into a shift. When you unplug the headphones, the game falls apart. That is not a defect but proof of where the author placed the center of gravity.
