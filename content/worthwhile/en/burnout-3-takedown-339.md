Players race aggressively and deliberately knock opponents out. Takedown turns contact from a mistake into a rewarded strategy, overturning traditional racing etiquette.

In Burnout 3: Takedown a hit on an opponent is a strategy for which you receive boost. The clean line that other racers reward is unnecessary politeness here. Aftertouch in a crash lets you steer the wreck for one more second. An accident is not the end of a lap; it is a weapon you can still direct into the target.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/339-cover-1.jpg)

Criterion Games, under Alex Ward and Fiona Sperry, published it in 2004 through Electronic Arts. The studio also maintains RenderWare, the engine on which San Andreas runs that same year. The consequence of the takedown is an inverted etiquette. Contact, which in a simulation means a penalty, is the main verb here, and the track is designed so you can crash on it to the player's benefit.

In 2004, beside Project Gotham, which pays for the style of a clean drive, Burnout is a conscious opposite. Both racing genres in one year say that position is not enough, and then they disagree on what should be paid for. The fictional cars have not aged into outdated licenses. The speed and the metal still work, because the game never bet on fidelity to a specific hood.

The crash modes from earlier games remain, but the takedown moves them into the middle of the race. You no longer have to drive a special map in order to be allowed to destroy. For reward students it is a class about what happens when you stop punishing a mistake and start buying it. The player begins to look for contact that elsewhere would count as failure.

The difficulty of the medals forces you to repeat the same collision. The iteration is bodily: you remember in which corner the opponent leaves a flank open. The later Paradise opens the city. The third game is still track-based, and therefore the argument about contact is more readable than in an open network of intersections.

Boost from a takedown ties aggression to pace. Whoever only crashes and does not drive wastes the boost. Violence is fuel, not the goal itself. When you play it today, do not swerve. The moment you swerve out of habit from another game, you stop playing Burnout.
