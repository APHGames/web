Link travels a map, but the battles take place from the side, with RPG statistics. A bold experiment with genre, experience, and precise swordplay shows the low rigidity of the early identity of the Zelda series.

Link walks a map of the kingdom, but the moment he enters a fight the picture flips to the side, and the sword has a height, a guard, and a downward thrust, while experience and magic grow as in an RPG. Towns are not just caves with a sage. They are places with people who teach you spells, and the palaces are tests that assume you grew stronger somewhere before.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/096-screenshot-1.png)

Nintendo released it in 1987 on the Famicom Disk System as a direct sequel to a game that had just defined the top-down view. Instead of certainty, the player receives an experiment, which in the second installment of a famous line is still a rare courage. Side-view combat uses a precision the first Zelda did not need. The shadow under you and the reach of the blade are information; random encounters on the map are the tax for the world also being a table of levels.

The decision to throw away the first game's camera had the consequence that the series does not yet have a fixed identity. Zelda can also be platform action with statistics, and the audience has to decide whether the name can bear it. Experience invites grinding. Whoever only runs toward the next palace hits a fight that is fair only after an hour the first game never asked of the player.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/096-screenshot-2.jpg)

In 1987 Final Fantasy and Mega Man are being released on the Famicom beside it. Zelda II takes from both: numbers from the RPG and a precise jump from action, and for a while it stops being a walk across Hyrule. The American version comes only later. In 1987 this is a Japanese wager on a disk, not the gold cartridge the West remembers as the first Zelda.

What has aged is the frequency of random battles and the part of the palaces that are a test of patience rather than a place to discover. What has not aged is the downward thrust as a readable verb and the willingness of a famous game to say that the next installment does not have to look like the previous one.

The series will return from this detour to the top-down view. The experiment remains as proof that Zelda's identity in 1987 was not yet a law. In a seminar, play a minute of the first Zelda and a minute of the second with no explanation. The silence that follows is more precise than a definition of genre.
