Each day Simon assembles the changing Mount Holly and searches for the mysterious Room 46. By choosing each new room, the player at once creates the dungeon, constrains future possibilities, and gains information for further attempts; Raw Fury confirmed a release of April 10, 2025.

In Blue Prince every morning you assemble the floor plan of Mount Holly again from rooms the game offers you like cards. The player chooses where to place the next room, and thereby at once builds the dungeon, closes off a future path, and collects information that will survive into tomorrow's otherwise rearranged house. The goal is Room 46. The day ends when the steps or the hope run out, and the next morning is a new draft of the same secret, not a new story from zero.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/499-cover-1.jpg)

Dogubomb released the game through Raw Fury on April 10, 2025. A small team builds a roguelike whose randomness is not a weapon drop, but architecture: a door leads only where you dared to place a chamber. The decision that the choice of a room is both movement and a constraint means a dead end is your own work. The consequence is the frustration of a domestic floor plan and a tension a deckbuilder knows from the shop, carried over into a wall and a corridor.

What persists are notes, passwords, and an understanding of how rooms relate logically. The geometry is erased, the knowledge is not, which is a contract close to Outer Wilds, only instead of an hour you have a house. The player almost necessarily keeps their own records outside the game. The studio counts on that: the interface is not an encyclopedia that would solve the secret for you.

In 2025 this is a distinct puzzle of the year, because it joins genres that usually only get cited. Beside the big RPGs it is quiet, repeated, and dependent on whether you enjoy drawing connections. Some days are generous and some close you into a pantry. The random draft is fair only across a long series of attempts, not in one afternoon, and that should be said to students beforehand.

It is early to talk about what will age. It is already visible that outside guides can replace your own notebook and thereby shorten the game by exactly the work it designs. The joining of architecture and a deck holds. It is still the cleanest contemporary example of how to turn roguelike progression into the drawing of a map, not the swapping of a sword.

Simon and the house have the tone of an inheritance mystery, not of a horror slaughter. The tension is whether today's offer of three rooms contains the one you already know how to use. In class, let students play it with paper on the table. Their notebook is part of the interface, even though the game did not configure it.
