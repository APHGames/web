An insect-like being carries entire worlds inside spheres and travels between them. A "world as an object" can be carried, inserted into another world, or used as an ability — remarkably pure recursive puzzle logic.

In Cocoon an insect-like character carries spheres that contain whole worlds, and the player sets a sphere down, enters it, or uses it as an ability in another world. There is no text to state the rule; there is the relationship between what you are carrying and what you are standing in. The puzzles are short and clean. One environment usually adds one consequence of the recursion and then lets you go before the idea becomes repetition.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/494-cover-1.jpg)

Geometric Interactive and Jeppe Carlsen, who designed the gameplay of Limbo and Inside, released the game through Annapurna in 2023. After years of set pieces in a side view, he builds a game whose only hero is the structure of space. The decision that a world should be an object you can insert into another world means recursion as a control. The consequence is that the player stops thinking in rooms and starts thinking about which sphere currently contains which.

Almost no combat and no inventory. The studio trusts that the cleanliness of the verb can carry a whole short game, which is a lesson from Playdead carried into another camera. Sound and animation lead without an arrow. When a sphere can open a path, you recognize it from how the world reacts to its presence, not from a tutorial window.

In 2023 this is a quiet counterpoint to the big worlds of the same year. Beside Zelda's building sets and the big RPGs it reminds you that a puzzle can be finished in an afternoon and still be precise. The surprise that you are carrying a map in your hand works most strongly the first time. The logic of the relationship of the spheres remains even after you know recursion is the point.

Only the one-time awe of the first insertion of a world into a world has aged. The solving itself still requires attention, not a memory of the trailer. The economy holds. It is still the cleanest contemporary example of a game that has few verbs and wastes none of them on explanation.

The ending does not extend the rules forever. It closes them, which is a courage longer puzzle games often lack because they want one more mechanic. In class, draw it as nested sets before you play it. Students will see that the design is topology, not the difficulty of a jump.

Cocoon is a 2023 puzzle adventure game developed by Geometric Interactive and published by Annapurna Interactive. Cocoon's sound design is entirely made up of synthetic noises rather than recordings.

Cocoon received "generally favorable" reviews, according to review aggregator Metacritic.
