Formations of aliens break away for independent attacks. Color graphics and individual attack trajectories moved the Space Invaders model toward livelier enemy patterns.

A formation of invaders holds its shape as in Space Invaders, but individual ships break out of it and attack along their own curve. The player still stands at the bottom edge and shoots upward; the novelty is not in the player's movement, but in the enemy ceasing to be one grid.

Namco released it in 1979 as a color arcade game. Compared with the overlay tint of older machines, the color is part of the picture, not a sticker on the glass. The Galaxian hardware becomes a base on which Namco and other companies build further games. The success here is also a board, not just one cartridge of behavior.

The decision to let enemies leave the formation had the consequence that the player must read two layers at once: the marching remainder, and a diving attack that no longer belongs to the rank. A bonus for shooting an attacker in flight teaches you to take risks. Anyone who shoots only at the safe grid earns slowly.

In 1979 Space Invaders is still the currency of the arcade. Galaxian is the first great color argument with its tempo, still before Galaga, which will finish the thought. Midway brings it to America, as it had brought Invaders before. A Japanese pattern becomes a world pattern again.

What has aged is the slow opening formation and an enemy whose personality is still only a trajectory. What has not aged is the break of an individual from the crowd as a way to turn a simple shooter into a reading of intention.

Galaga, and the family after it, takes this break from the rank and adds ship capture, waves, and finer patterns. In a seminar, do not treat it as a middle step to tick off. It is the moment when color and individual movement begin to carry the design, not merely the decoration.

Designed by company engineer Kazunori Sawano, Galaxian was Namco's answer to Space Invaders, a similar space shooter released the previous year by rival developer Taito. Galaxian is one of the first video games to feature RGB color graphics, and the first to use a tile-based hardware system, which was capable of animated multi-color sprites as well as scrolling; the latter was limited to the starfield background while the game itself remained a fixed shooter.

Galaxian has also been ported to many home systems and is included in numerous Namco compilations. The enemies appear in formation towards the top of the screen, with two escort ships, labeled the "Galaxian Flagship" or "Galboss", which take 1 shot to destroy.

Galaxian Flagships will make a dive bomb with two red escort ships; shooting all three of these will award the player bonus points, with extra points awarded to the destruction of the flagship. Galaxian was designed by Kazunori Sawano, who had previously worked on many of Namco's electro-mechanical shooting gallery arcade games, notably Shoot Away (1977).

Nichibutsu later found success in emulating Galaxian 's gameplay through Moon Cresta and its sequels. A Bally Astrocade version was published under the name Galaxian, but the name of the port was later changed to Galactic Invasion.

Coleco released a stand-alone Mini-Arcade tabletop version of Galaxian in 1981, which, along with Pac-Man, Donkey Kong, and Frogger, sold three million units combined. It is not a sequel, as Entex did not previously develop any port of Galaxian.

Galaxian had sold 40,000 arcade units in the United States by 1981, and 50,000 units in the US . Video magazine in 1982 reviewed the Astrocade version of Galaxian (named Galactic Invasion), noting that the graphics were inferior to the coin-op and PC versions, but praising the play-action as "magnificent" compared to other console versions.

Home Computing Weekly in 1983 gave the Spectrum version of Galaxian 3/5 stars describing it as a well-written version and praising the graphics as fast although flickery. The Galaxian arcade hardware had a significant influence on the hardware design of Nintendo's later arcade and console systems, including the arcade hardware for Radar Scope (1980) and Donkey Kong (1981) as well as the Famicom and Nintendo Entertainment System.

According to Nintendo R&D2 lead engineer Masayuki Uemura, Galaxian replaced the more intensive bitmap rendering system of Space Invaders with a hardware sprite rendering system that animated sprites over a scrolling background, allowing more detailed graphics, faster gameplay and a scrolling animated starfield background. This provided the basis for Nintendo's Radar Scope arcade hardware, which improved on Galaxian with technology such as high-speed emitter-coupled logic integrated circuit chips and memory on a 50&nbsp;MHz printed circuit board.

Super Impulse also released a stand-alone Tiny Arcade version of Galaxian. The Galaxian world record has been the focus of many competitive gamers since its release.

The most famous Galaxian rivalry has been between British player Gary Whelan and American Perry Rodgers, who faced off at Apollo Amusements in Pompano Beach, Florida, US, on April 6 to 9, 2006.
