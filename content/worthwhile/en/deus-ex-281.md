Agent JC Denton uncovers a global conspiracy in a world of biomodifications. Levels allow stealth, hacking, combat, and social solutions, and they react to the player's unexpected actions; it became the paradigm of the reactive immersive sim.

In one level JC Denton can go through a vent, hack a lock, shoot, or talk, and the game tries to have those surprises not kill the simulation but get written into it. Augmentations and skills are not only numbers on a weapon. They close and open paths, and the agent's body becomes a build that reads a level differently from a colleague's body.

Warren Spector directed it at Ion Storm Austin, and Harvey Smith was the lead designer; Eidos published it in 2000. Austin is not the Dallas office where Daikatana was made, but a second house under the same brand, and this game belongs to the one that wants a system. The engine is first person with dialogue, an inventory, and a level that has more than one correct corridor. Technically it is not the prettiest FPS of 2000, and historically it becomes the measure of the immersive sim.

The decision to build an obstacle as several systemic solutions had a consequence students recognize in their own stories. Two players do not tell the same level, and that is a success, not a contradiction in the canon. Some boss encounters step away from that contract and become hit-point fights. A seminar should show that crack, because even the model immersive sim can break its own rule.

In 2000, beside The Sims and Diablo II, Deus Ex is a game about action being able to be political without ceasing to be a toy with an inventory. The conspiracy is the theme and at the same time a pretext for levels that react. What has aged is the interface and part of the voice acting. What has not aged is the argument of the level, augmentation as an edit of the body, and a tone that takes an institution as a dungeon.

In seminar, give three students the same goal, forbid them to advise each other, and then compare the paths. If they are the same, the level failed at what it promises. Spector and Smith assemble a lesson from earlier immersive sims, from Looking Glass through System Shock 2. Deus Ex is the one that became the textbook, because a city and a conspiracy are more readable than a wrecked ship.

Against Thief there is more permitted violence here and more speech. Both want you to choose, and each punishes the loudest solution differently. The game is a paradigm not because it is without flaw. It is a paradigm because the same level can name both the flaw and the path.

Deus Ex is a series of action role-playing video games, originally developed by Ion Storm (2000-2003) and later Eidos-Montréal (2011-2017). The series as a whole has been praised for its gameplay design and setting, with some journalists citing the original Deus Ex as a prominent example of the "immersive sim", a category also attached to other series entries.

Beginning with Human Revolution, the series has seen multimedia expansions into comics and novels, many released as part of a project dubbed the "Deus Ex Universe".
