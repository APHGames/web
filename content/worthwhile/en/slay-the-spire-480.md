A hero advances through a random tower and assembles a card deck during the run. Joining a roguelike run with deckbuilding created one of the most imitated indie genre syntheses of the last decade.

In Slay the Spire you assemble a deck during a run up the tower, not before it. After a fight the player is offered a choice of cards, in the shop they delete a weak strike, and before the boss they discover that power is not in adding everything, but in having a deck that does one thing consistently. Relics and elites are bets on a map of nodes. The path upward is a draft of risk: a rest, an unknown event, or a fight that can both arm you and kill you.

Mega Crit, a small studio, kept the game in early access from 2017 and released version 1.0 in January 2019 with the publisher Humble. Ironclad, the Silent, and the Defect are the base grammars; the Watcher arrives later as the fourth. The decision to join a roguelike run with deckbuilding means you adapt to what the game offered, not to a meta you brought from home. The consequence is a genre that fills with imitations within a few years, because the sentence is so clear.

Removing cards is often stronger than buying them. The studio thereby teaches that a deck is the pace of a cycle, not a collection of pretty illustrations. Three acts and bosses that punish a specific laziness give the run its exams. There is no story about the tower; there is a structure that can be explained in two minutes and played for a hundred hours.

In 2019 this is a reference synthesis of two independent traditions. Beside Hearthstone, where you build the deck in advance, this game is a lesson in improvisation. Ascension later adds degrees of hardness for players who have already read the tower. The base game stays intelligible even without them.

Only the obviousness of the genre that appeared after it has aged. The readability of a choice of one card from three still serves as the measure of whether a new deckbuilder is even telling the truth about risk. The map and the shop hold. It is still the best example of how to turn randomness into a craft, if the player is allowed to refuse even an attractive card.

Enemy patterns are readable on purpose. You are meant to see that the next turn will hurt you, and to decide whether you have an answer in the deck now, or three cards later. In class, run it on one fight and one shop. Students should defend which card they will not take, because the refusal is the lesson.

Slay the Spire is a 2019 roguelike deck-building game developed by the indie studio Mega Crit. In Slay the Spire, the player attempts to ascend a spire of multiple floors created through procedural generation as one of four characters, battling enemies and bosses.

Slay the Spire has been very well received, becoming nominated for multiple awards in 2019 and 2020. A sequel, Slay the Spire II, was released in early access in March 2026.

The goal is to work through several floors of the spire, each level having a number of potential encounters distributed in a branching structure with a boss character at the end of the level. Slay the Spire is developed by Seattle-based studio Mega Crit, with Anthony Giovannetti and Casey Yano as lead developers.

Humble Bundle helped to provide publication support for Mega Crit for the non-PC releases, first for home consoles: Slay the Spire was released for the PlayStation 4 on May 21, 2019, for the Nintendo Switch on June 6, 2019, and for the Xbox One on August 14, 2019. Mega Crit announced that by the end of June, sales of Slay the Spire had exceeded 1 million, and reached 1.5 million by March 2019.

IGN named Slay the Spire as the Best Strategy Game of 2019, while PC Gamer gave it the Best Design for 2019. Mega Crit announced in January 2021 that they were creating a cooperative board game adaptation of Slay the Spire with Contention Games.

Mega Crit announced in April 2024 that a sequel, Slay the Spire II, was planned to be released in early access in 2025. As a result of the changes with the Unity engine licensing, Slay the Spire II is being developed in the Godot engine.
