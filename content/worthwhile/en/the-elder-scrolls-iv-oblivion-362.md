The player closes the gates of Oblivion in the province of Cyrodiil. Radiant AI and a fully voiced open world represented Bethesda's fundamental transition toward the modern mass sandbox.

In Oblivion you close the gates in Cyrodiil, and between them you walk a land that has a timetable. The smith, the guard, and the thief have a daily plan, and Radiant AI tries to play it even when you are not in a quest. The fully voiced world uses a small circle of voices over and over. The consequence is a liveliness that quickly gives itself away by repeating the same sentence in the street.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/362-cover-1.jpg)

Todd Howard published it at Bethesda in 2006 on Gamebryo, on PC and on the Xbox 360. After Morrowind it is a conscious step toward a more accessible, more readable fantasy that a console can carry too. Scaling enemies with your level keeps a sense of challenge and at the same time steals the sense that you have grown. A bandit in furs can later have more expensive armor than an Imperial soldier, and the world thereby stops having a fixed scale.

Patrick Stewart voiced Emperor Uriel Septim. The opening scene is a spectacle, after which the game lets you into a land where nobody guards that kind of direction anymore, and that contrast is the whole Bethesda model. In 2006 it is the breakthrough of the Bethesda sandbox to a wide audience. Morrowind was an alien province for the patient. Oblivion is an empire that wants to let you go on even without reading a journal.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/362-screenshot-2.jpg)

The faces, the repeated voices, and the Oblivion gates have aged into memes. The freedom of the guild lines, especially the Dark Brotherhood and the thieves, and the modding scene have not. Fast travel erases the road Morrowind forced you to walk. The consequence is comfort and, at the same time, a landscape you remember less because you let it be calculated.

Shivering Isles later adds an island stranger than the base Cyrodiil. The expansion thereby admits that the main map bet on intelligibility at the expense of strangeness. For AI students Radiant is a lesson in what a schedule does when a script does not watch it. An NPC steals because he has a need, and the game sometimes makes an anecdote out of that which the authors did not write.

Level scaling is still an argument in a seminar. Whoever defends it defends a constant challenge. Whoever refuses it defends a world that has places you are not yet allowed. When you play it today, turn the arrow off in your head and read at least one dialogue to the end. Otherwise you will see only the service 2006 added and miss the land under it.
