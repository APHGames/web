Tennis, bowling, and other sports are controlled by the movement of the Wii Remote. A gesture instead of an abstract button drastically lowered the barrier to entry and demonstrated the mass potential of motion controls.

In Wii Sports you play tennis, bowling, baseball, golf, and boxing by swinging the Wii Remote. The body in the living room is the controller, and the screen only confirms that the gesture arrived. The accelerometer reads the intention of a swing, not a precise model of the arm. The game trusts your timing and direction, but it does not see whether you actually extended the elbow as on a court.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/359-cover-1.jpg)

Nintendo EAD packed it with the console in most of the world in 2006 and sold it separately in Japan. It is the first Wii game most households launched at all, and at the same time the display case of the controller. The consequence of a gesture instead of an abstract button is a sharply lower threshold. Whoever cannot use a DualShock can swing at bowling, and the industry turns that into a strategy for a new audience for a few years.

The Mii is your body in the game. Personalization is not a skin in a menu; it is a face that laughs when you hit a strike, and therefore someone who is not playing is also watching. In 2006, after a generation that chased the adult shooter, Nintendo turns the console back into a shared room. Wii Sports is the argument of that turn, not its companion.

The ambiguity of the gesture has aged. The game still cannot tell a beautiful swing from a careless one, and that is the price of reading intention. Bowling and tennis as a household ritual lasted for years in the meantime. Competitors later add more precise cameras and sensors and often miss the social reason a wave was enough. Precision is not the same thing as a threshold.

For control students the difference between a gesture and motion capture is a required class. Wii Sports is the first extreme, not an imperfect version of later motion capture. The individual sports are short on purpose. The game counts on the controller passing around the room, not on one player spending the evening in a career.

Satoru Iwata at the time leads Nintendo toward a wider audience, and this bundle is the purest product of that policy. It is not a side minigame; it is the reason to buy the console. When you play it today, play it in pairs and notice when the game praises you for a bad swing. The whole deal with a new audience is in that praise.

It was included as a pack-in game with the console in all territories except Japan and Korea, making it the first sports game included with the launch of a Nintendo system since Mario's Tennis for the Virtual Boy in 1995. Wii Sports is a collection of five sports simulations designed to demonstrate the motion-sensing capabilities of the Wii Remote.

The five sports included are tennis, baseball, bowling, golf, and boxing. Players use the Wii Remote to mimic actions performed in real-life sports, such as swinging a tennis racket or rolling a bowling ball.

The game also features training and fitness modes that monitor players' progress in the sports. Wii Sports was well-received by critics and audiences, and is considered one of the greatest games of all time.

On 25 June 2009, a sequel, Wii Sports Resort, was released, which contained 12 sports, 10 of which were not seen in the original. A high-definition (HD) remake of Wii Sports titled Wii Sports Club was released on 11 July 2014 for the Wii U.

Another sequel, Nintendo Switch Sports, was released for the Nintendo Switch on 29 April 2022. Wii Sports consists of five separate sports games—tennis, baseball, bowling, golf, and boxing—accessed from the main menu.

The games use the motion sensor capabilities of the Wii Remote to control the player's dominant arm and/or the appropriate sports equipment it wields. Wii Sports also features a fitness test that calculates a player's fitness age (ranging from 20 to 80 years old, 20 being the best possible).

The challenges from the fitness test are taken from another mode in Wii Sports, the training mode. Wii Sports was produced by Katsuya Eguchi, who managed Software Development Group 2 at Nintendo Entertainment Analysis and Development.

Wii Sports was designed as a simple introductory title meant to offer something for both gamers and non-gamers. Sports were chosen as the theme because of the widespread familiarity with them.

Initially, Shigeru Miyamoto and Nintendo President Satoru Iwata were hesitant to include the game as a pack-in title, but were convinced by Nintendo of America President Reggie Fils-Aimé, who believed that Wii Sports would be key to the success of the Wii console. It was later announced, at Nintendo's press conference prior to E3 2006, it would be part of a sports package.

At the Nintendo World event on 14 September 2006, Reggie Fils-Aimé confirmed the game's five playable sports and announced that Wii Sports would be included free with every Wii console. Wii Sports received "generally favorable" reviews from critics, according to review aggregator website Metacritic.

In early May 2007, game-industry research firm Media Create placed Wii Sports third in their list of top-20 games in Japan. Following its release, Wii Sports received multiple awards from various organizations, websites, and magazines.

IGN awarded it "Best Sports Game of 2006" and second best game of 2006. At the 10th Annual Interactive Achievement Awards, Wii Sports won "Outstanding Achievement in Gameplay Engineering", "Outstanding Achievement in Game Design", and "Outstanding Innovation in Gaming", while also receiving nominations for "Console Game of the Year" and "Overall Game of the Year".

At the 2007 British Academy Video Games Awards, Wii Sports won six out of seven award nominations: Sports, Innovation, Gameplay, Multiplayer, Casual, and Strategy and Simulation. The New York Times named Wii Sports as the Game of the Year, while Gamasutra proclaimed it as the most important game of 2006.

Wii Sports, a major factor in the Wii's worldwide success, was the first game among a number of core Wii games being developed at the same time, with the same philosophy; other games were released as Wii Play, Wii Fit, and Wii Music. A direct sequel to Wii Sports, titled Wii Sports Resort, was released in 2009.

Due to this popularity, Matt later appeared in another sequel, Nintendo Switch Sports, where a cheat code can unlock him as a boss opponent in Chambara. Wired included Wii Sports in its list of "The 15 Most Influential Games of the Decade" at #8, for its role in popularizing motion controls and having a major impact on the "videogame landscape." In 2009, Guinness World Records ranked the game at number 25 on their list of the top 50 console games of all time based on initial impact and lasting legacy.

Wii Sports was later inducted into the World Video Game Hall of Fame in May 2023. Wii Sports has become a popular means for social gatherings and competitions.

Residents at senior centers and retirement homes have formed leagues using Wii Sports bowling. After its Australian release, Nintendo and Myer, an Australian department store chain, held a Wii Sports tennis tournament in January 2007 in Melbourne, Australia.

An unofficial Wii Sports tennis tournament titled "Wiimbledon" was held in the Brooklyn, New York bar Barcade on 23 June 2007. An episode of Late Night with Conan O'Brien featured host Conan O'Brien competing against his guest, tennis star Serena Williams, in a match of Wii Sports tennis.

At the 80th Academy Awards Show, host Jon Stewart and Jamia Simone Nash were caught playing Wii Sports tennis on one of the event's gigantic projection screens after a commercial break as part of a joke. Wii Sports has also been featured in mainstream movies such as Tropic Thunder, and in commercials for products such as Kellogg's Smart Start.

Other injuries include muscle, tendon, and ligament injuries from excess playing of simulated sports on the Wii—dubbed "Wii-itis". Wii Sports has also been cited as a game that can provide a bonding experience among family members, and as a means of exercising and losing weight when played regularly.

Wii Sports has been used to aid in the physical therapy of a boxer at the Glenrose Rehabilitation Hospital in Canada, stroke victims in Minneapolis, Minnesota and Raleigh, North Carolina, and injured soldiers in Prescott, Arizona; Washington, D.C.; and Landstuhl, Germany. Wii Sports was also used to the benefit of Scandinavian stroke victims and for children with hemiplegic cerebral palsy.

A sequel, Wii Sports Resort, was first revealed at Nintendo's E3 2008 presentation. The game features 12 sports, (2 from the original) including swordplay, wakeboarding, Frisbee, archery, basketball, table tennis, golf, bowling, power cruising, canoeing, cycling, and air sports (parachuting and piloting).

On 18 September 2013, Nintendo announced Wii Sports Club for the Wii U's Nintendo eShop. The game features the five games of Wii Sports remade in high-definition graphics, with support for the Wii MotionPlus (similar to Wii Sports Resort) and online multiplayer.

On 9 February 2022, during a Nintendo Direct presentation, it was announced that a sequel titled Nintendo Switch Sports would be released for the Nintendo Switch on 29 April 2022. It features some of the sports from Wii Sports and Wii Sports Resort such as Bowling, Tennis, Golf, Chambara (a type of Swordplay), and Basketball with some new additions like soccer, volleyball, and badminton.

On 9 June 2026, during a Nintendo Direct presentation, it was announced that a new entry in the Wii Sports series titled Nintendo Switch Sports Resort would be released exclusively for the Nintendo Switch 2 on 22 October 2026. Set on the return of Wuhu Island, it features 12 sports and activities using the Joy-Con 2's motion controls, including Boxing, Table Tennis, Archery, Tennis, Volleyball, Bowling, Basketball, Golf, Thumb Wrestling, Skateboarding, Power Cruising, and Prop Plane, with Jump Rope also available as a warm-up activity.
