Christopher Blair fights in the conclusion of the war against the Kilrathi. Big-budget FMV with Hollywood actors symbolized the CD-ROM era of the "interactive movie."

Between missions Christopher Blair talks to actors full screen, and choices in the conversation change whom you will fly with next. The shooting is still in the middle, but the frame is a film. The Kilrathi are at the end of the war, and you are not an anonymous pilot. A name, an actor, and a branch of the campaign make you a character in a serial.

Chris Roberts shot the game in 1994 at Origin, already under the wing of Electronic Arts. Mark Hamill plays Blair, Malcolm McDowell and John Rhys-Davies sit in supporting roles, and there are several discs, because video eats space. The engine still computes a space battle, but the CD-ROM carries a feature-film ambition. FMV is not a reward after a mission; it is the way the campaign branches.

The decision to cast Hollywood names has a consequence for the budget and for the tone. The game moves closer to the interactive movie, and the simulation has to carry the expectation the faces set. In 1994 this is the peak of a wave in which the CD drive pulls the industry toward video. Beside adventures with actors, Wing Commander III is the case of video growing over a simulator.

The branching still continues from the first entry. Failure and choice are meant to change the war, not just to play a different clip with the same result. What has aged are the image compression and the theatrical sets. The actors play against the blue backgrounds of the time, and today's eye sees the seam before it sees the war.

What has not aged is the fantasy of a pilot's career, which Roberts will keep chasing, even outside this series. The player wants to be someone with a name, not just a HUD. The missions between the films are still the game. Whoever skips them as an obstacle between clips will miss why Origin kept the cockpit at all.

Compare the spectacle with X-Wing. LucasArts keeps service discipline; Origin here keeps the cast and lets the discipline be the second half of the evening. In a seminar, play one scene without sound and then with sound. You will see how much of the contract the actor holds and how much is still held by the mission that comes after him.

Wing Commander III: Heart of the Tiger is the third main game in Chris Roberts' Wing Commander science fiction space combat simulation video game series, developed and released by Origin Systems in December 1994. Wing Commander is a space combat simulator intercut with live action cutscenes.

Wing Commander III dispensed with the issuing of medals after such missions and relied more on cutscenes to drive the story along, making much more use of CD technology. As in Wing Commander, some wingmen can be killed permanently in combat.

Blair, by orders of Admiral Geoffrey Tolwyn, is transferred as Wing Commander to the TCS Victory, a Ranger-class carrier twice as old as Blair. If Blair wins the duel, he forces Dillon to request reassignment to the Victory 's flight wing.

Blair and his wing are scrambled to defend Locanda against several of these missiles. Wing Commander III: Heart of the Tiger was developed and released by Origin Systems.

Wing Commander III made the move from the sprite-based graphics used in previous titles to software-driven texture-mapped polygonal 3D, and used FMV for cutscenes. Wing Commander III featured an entirely new line of ships and fighters, abandoning the technology of Wing Commander and Wing Commander II.

A Pentium (then a very high-end processor) was required to get optimum performance out of Wing Commander III. Roberts said, "We're not afraid to lead hardware sales a little, and we believe that Pentium will soon be the standard." In June 1995, Atari Corporation realized a deal with EA to bring select titles to the Atari Jaguar CD, with Wing Commander III among the selected games.

The sequel, Wing Commander IV: The Price of Freedom, was released in 1996. In September 2011, the source code of Wing Commander III was handed to the fan community by a former developer for the purpose of digital long-time preservation.

Wing Commander III: Heart of the Tiger was another major hit for the Wing Commander series, the PC versions alone selling over 500,000 copies. Trunzo reviewed Wing Commander III: Heart of the Tiger in White Wolf Inphobia #56 (June, 1995), rating it a 5 out of 5 and stated that "Wing Commander III is quite simply the most stunning game around.

A sure Hall of Famer, Wing Commander III is the ultimate gaming experience." Reviewing the PlayStation version, a reviewer for Maximum praised the "intricate" plot but criticized that the combat is simplistic and dull and that the FMV sequences lack any interaction beyond the occasional multiple choice response. The editors of PC Gamer US nominated Wing Commander III for their 1994 "Best Action Game" award, although it lost to TIE Fighter.
