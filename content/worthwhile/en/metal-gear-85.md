Originally released in Japan and parts of Europe in July 1987, it is the first game in the Metal Gear series and the first video game developed by Hideo Kojima. Initially envisioned as a pure action game, Metal Gear was reworked under Kojima to focus on avoiding enemies, contrary to the then-popular shoot 'em up genre, with an emphasis on evasion similar to Pac-Man (1980).

The MSX2 version of Metal Gear was released to positive reception, with praise directed towards the gameplay and graphics, while the Famicom/NES version received mixed reviews. Metal Gear is retrospectively recognized as the first mainstream stealth game, and is credited as a pioneer in stealth mechanics and complex storytelling via the radio transceiver.

A direct sequel made by Kojima, Metal Gear 2: Solid Snake, and a sequel to the NES port, Snake's Revenge, were released in 1990. The MSX2 version was ported with a revised translation and additional gameplay features in Metal Gear Solid 3: Subsistence (2005), and this version was re-released along with the NES version as part of the Metal Gear Solid: Master Collection Vol.

Metal Gear is a two-dimensional stealth game, played from a flip-screen overhead perspective. Metal Gear takes place in 1995, which is not defined in the Japanese MSX2 version of the game, and referred to the year as "19XX", the release of Metal Gear Solid established the date within its narrative.
