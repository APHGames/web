Mario jumps between small planets with their own gravity. Variable gravity made it possible to rediscover the basic jump in locally curved 3D spaces.

In Super Mario Galaxy, Mario jumps between planetoids, each with its own gravity. The jump you know from flat ground bends according to the surface under you, and the camera does not have to get lost in an open kingdom. The stars and Rosalina's observatory are the hub. Between planets there is a short trip and then a self-contained task, so the game is a collection of episodes, not one continent.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/376-screenshot-1.jpg)

Yoshiaki Koizumi directed it at Nintendo EAD Tokyo and Shigeru Miyamoto produced it. It came out in 2007 on the Wii. Mahito Yokota and Koji Kondo wrote the music, and the orchestral tone is unusually grave for Mario. The consequence of the small planets is that the camera has a solved problem: the ground is also the ceiling of a neighboring path, and the player orients by the body, not by a map.

In 2007, a year after Wii Sports, Nintendo shows that the same console can carry a precise platformer. The motion controller serves the spin and the collecting here, not a replacement of the jump. Some of the stars are filler, and that has aged. Gravity as a way to rediscover the basic jump has not, and it is still a peak of three-dimensional Mario.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/376-screenshot-2.jpg)

The story of the Lumas and the library is a melancholy the series does not usually hold. Koizumi thereby gives the jump a reason that is not merely a princess in another castle. For camera students the planetoid is the class after Mario 64 and Sunshine. Instead of solving a free view in an open park, the game shrinks the world so the view can hold the surface.

Galaxy 2 in 2010 tightens the system further. The first is still the one that in 2007 proved the Wii is not only a wave in the living room. Lives and a fall into the abyss are classic. What is new is that the abyss can be the far side of the sphere you are still standing on.

The spin attack ties the Wii Remote to the old jump. The gesture is seasoning, not a replacement of the craft, and therefore the game survives even in the hands of a player who skipped Sports. When you play it today, run around the first planetoid until the direction makes you dizzy. The whole design is in that dizziness.
