Miner Willy passes through twenty bizarre rooms full of precise jumps. It made the most of the Spectrum both technically and aesthetically, and embodied the British 8-bit auteur scene.

Miner Willy jumps through twenty caverns over precisely placed enemies, collects keys, and looks for the exit before his air runs out. Each room is one screen with its own joke: a different enemy, a different rhythm, no continuous world, but a catalogue of ideas.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/047-screenshot-1.png)

Matthew Smith wrote it as a teenager for Bug-Byte, and it was released on the ZX Spectrum in 1983. It is a British eight-bit auteur game in the full sense: one person, one machine, one distinctive hand. The Spectrum has no hardware scroll and no rich palette. Smith turns flickering attributes and chunky sprites into an aesthetic, not an apology, and the music and the color are part of the gag.

The decision to build rooms as self-contained puzzles about the jump had the consequence that the player takes death as a test of one screen, not as the loss of a path. Progress is a list of jokes that have been cleared. Some caverns make fun of the British scene of the time and of its names. The game is also a comment on the environment in which it was made, not just an obstacle course.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/047-screenshot-2.jpg)

In 1983 the Spectrum is the main British home machine, and beside imported arcade games a local school of platformers is forming. Manic Miner is its loudest signboard. Smith is not a studio employee with a producer. He is an author the audience knows by name, which on a tape of the time is still unusual next to the large companies.

What has aged is the millimeter severity of the jump and a punishment that, after the twentieth death on the same ledge, stops teaching. What has not aged is the courage for every room to have an idea of its own, even if the world between them is only a menu.

Jet Set Willy, a year later, takes Willy and opens a house for him in which the rooms now neighbor one another. In a seminar, clear one cavern and merely freeze another. The students should recognize that the design is in the placement, not in the hero's movement.

Manic Miner is a platform game written for the ZX Spectrum by Matthew Smith. The first game in the Miner Willy series, the design was inspired by Miner 2049er (1982) for the Atari 8-bit computers.

Retro Gamer called Manic Miner one of the most influential platform games of all time, and it has been ported to numerous home computers, video game consoles, and mobile phones. The player must avoid enemies, listed in the cassette inlay as "...Poisonous Pansies, Spiders, Slime, and worst of all, Manic Mining Robots..." which move along predefined paths at constant speeds.

Manic Miner was developed by Matthew Smith following a request for a Donkey Kong style arcade game from Alan Maton, Bug-Byte's despatch manager. Both games were among nine titles launched by Bug-Byte Initially Manic Miner had sixteen screens until Smith found a way to add four more, finally finishing the game in August 1983.

In August 1983, sales of Bug-Byte's original ZX Spectrum release of Manic Miner took the game to the top of the UK video games charts replacing Jet Pac. Manic Miner was the winner of "Best Arcade Style Game", and placed third in the "Game of the Year" category at the 1983 Golden Joystick Awards voted for by readers of Computer and Video Games magazine.

Manic Miner was placed at number 25 in the "Your Sinclair official top 100" Spectrum games of all time and was later voted number 6 in the Readers' Top 100 Games of All Time. In 1991, ACE magazine listed Manic Miner and its sequel Jet Set Willy, along with Hunchback, Impossible Mission, and the Mario series, as the greatest platform games of all time, calling Manic Miner "the first great home computer platform game".

The sequel to Manic Miner is Jet Set Willy and was followed by Jet Set Willy II. Software Projects also released a game in the style of Manic Miner for the VIC-20 called The Perils of Willy.

Manic Miner 360 was released for the Xbox 360 as an Xbox Live Indie Game in June 2012. A homage to the Manic Miner loading screen appears in one episode of the 2005 British sitcom Nathan Barley.
