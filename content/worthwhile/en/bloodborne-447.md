A Hunter uncovers cosmic horror hidden beneath gothic Yharnam. The rally system rewards immediate counter-aggression after a hit and shifts the Souls formula toward faster, more offensive combat.

The Hunter in Bloodborne walks Yharnam, and after a hit the player has a short window in which lost health can be fought back. An attack is therefore healing, and hesitation is a second blow on top. Trick weapons change from one form to another in the middle of a combo. The shield almost disappears, and the gun serves to interrupt, not to hide behind cover as in a slower Souls game.

FromSoftware and Hidetaka Miyazaki released the game in March 2015 as a PlayStation 4 exclusive in cooperation with Sony. Gothic horror opens across the game into the cosmic, and the hunter stops understanding what they are actually curing. Thirty frames a second and long loads belong to the game's early reputation as much as the combat. The studio bets on the density of animation and on a city that is both a level and an atmosphere, not on an open map.

The decision to reward immediate aggression after a hit, the rally system, shifts the Souls language from cautious circling to attack as survival. The consequence is a faster, bloodier rhythm and a player who is afraid to step back. Insight changes what you can see at all. Information about the world is both a statistic and a curse, which is a rare joining of a Lovecraftian theme with the interface.

In 2015 this is proof that FromSoftware can repeat its formula in a different body, not only in a different skin. Beside the open RPGs of the time, Yharnam is narrow, cruel, and very careful. Chalice dungeons add a procedural layer under the hand-built city. Some players see extra content in them, some see repetition the game's main dream does not need.

The frame-rate ceiling has aged, as have some of the load times. The later repetition of rituals in the dungeon layers is also thinner than the first path through the city. The rally, the trick weapons, and the passage from the gothic to the cosmos all hold. The Old Hunters the same year shows that the fights are the part that can be tightened further without a new world map.

Death and blood echoes still teach the layout, but the city is more aggressive than Lordran. A safe lamp here is not so frequent an embrace. In class, set it immediately after Dark Souls and ask what happens to the player's body when healing requires a step forward, not a drink at a fire.

Bloodborne began development in 2012 under the working title of Project Beast. Bearing many similarities to FromSoftware's Dark Souls series, Bloodborne was inspired by the literary works of authors H.

Bloodborne has been cited as one of the greatest video games ever made, with praise directed at its gameplay, atmosphere, sound design, Lovecraftian themes and interconnected world design. Bloodborne is an action role-playing game played from a third-person perspective.

Bloodborne also features Chalice Dungeons, optional dungeons accessed through ritual items. Bloodborne is set in Yharnam, a decaying Gothic city known for blood ministration, a medical practice built around the healing properties of blood.

Bloodborne soundtrack contains over 80 minutes of original music by Ryan Amon, Tsukasa Saitoh, Yuka Kitamura, Nobuyoshi Suzuki, Cris Velasco, and Michael Wandmacher, featuring performances by a 65-piece orchestra and a 32-member choir. However, Miyazaki later stated that Bloodborne was never considered to be Demon's Souls II, due to Sony Computer Entertainment wanting a new intellectual property (IP) for the PlayStation 4.

In January 2015, Bloodborne became Game Informer readers' most anticipated game of 2015. A song to promote Bloodborne was recorded by the Hit House featuring Ruby Friedman for a trailer and TV spot of the game, titled "Hunt You Down", written by Scott Miller and William Hunt, and recorded by Wyn Davis in Los Angeles and at Word of Mouth Recording Studios in New Orleans.

Sony Denmark teamed up with Danish organization GivBlod in order to encourage blood donations through a program where donators who donated on March 23, 2015, would receive a chance to win Bloodborne as a gift. Bloodborne had long been a title that players sought a remake for modern consoles and to fix some of the technical issues with the game's original release.

Bluepoint Games, a Sony first-party studio, had previously done a Demon's Souls remake in 2020, and had looked into doing one for Bloodborne. Following the cancellation of a live service God of War game in 2025, Bluepoint pitched the Bloodborne remake, but were told that FromSoftware was not interested in pursuing it.

Bloodborne received "universal acclaim" from critics, according to review aggregator Metacritic. Bloodborne sold 152,567 physical retail units within the first week of release in Japan, ranking first place within the Japanese software sales charts for that particular week.

Bloodborne debuted at number two in the United Kingdom software retail chart, behind Battlefield Hardline by 22,500 units. In the United States, Bloodborne was the second best-selling software in March, despite being released at the end of the month.

By April 2015, Bloodborne had sold over one million units, and by September 2015, the game had over two million units sold. Bloodborne was awarded the 2015 Game of the Year by several video game review sites, including GameTrailers, Eurogamer, Destructoid, and Edge, along with being awarded the "2015 PlayStation 4 Game of the Year" by IGN.

A 2023 poll conducted by GQ which surveyed a team of video game journalists across the industry ranked Bloodborne as the fourth best video game of all time.
