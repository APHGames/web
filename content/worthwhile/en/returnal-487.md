Selene repeats a deadly cycle on the planet Atropos. Bullet-hell density joins precise 3D TPS movement and the haptic and adaptive functions of the DualSense.

Selene in Returnal starts the cycle on Atropos again after death, and in third person the player weaves between projectiles as dense as in an old arcade game. A weapon learns traits if you extract it alive, and biomes change as a fixed chapter, not as an endless menu. The DualSense is part of the readability: trigger resistance and haptics say what the weapon is doing without you having to read an icon in the corner. The studio counts on the PlayStation 5 controller as a tool, not as extra rumble.

Housemarque, a long-time arcade workshop of games such as Resogun and Nex Machina, released Returnal through Sony in April 2021 as its big narrative game. Harry Krueger's direction takes bullet hell and gives it a body, a story, and a planet. The decision to join precise third-person movement with bullet patterns means the camera must not lie about distance. The consequence is a demanding action game that does not retreat from arcade readability even in cinematic biomechanical halls.

Long runs and the originally limited saving belonged to the early criticism, because a death after an hour hurts differently from a death in a short arcade game. The studio later adds a way to suspend a run, which is a concession to players, not a change of the combat. The story of the cycle and of Selene is fragmentary on purpose. Audio logs and the changes of the house between attempts are a second map beside the one you shoot through.

In 2021 this is a showcase game of a new console: not because it has a higher resolution, but because the controller and the weapon are one thing. Beside multiplayer services it is a solo campaign with repetition. Persistent unlocks and temporary weapons keep the roguelike contract. You carry something away in your head and at the base, and the planet takes something else back.

That first hardness of saving has aged, as have some biomes that lose their horror once the patterns are learned. The story stays elliptical, and not everyone wants to assemble it. The density of projectiles and the feel of the controller hold. It is still the measure of when haptics are design and when they are only a vibration after a shot.

Biomes have their own grammar of enemies and terrain. They are not faceless random rooms; they are places you learn as levels. In class, use it as the passage of the arcade into AAA: ask what remained of Resogun when the studio added a story and a third person.
