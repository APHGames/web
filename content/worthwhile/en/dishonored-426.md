Corvo takes revenge in Dunwall by means of stealth, magic, and violence. The Blink teleport, verticality, and combinable systems let the player improvise a solution instead of following an intended route.

Corvo in Dishonored returns to Dunwall and can pass every mission across roofs, through rats, by the blade, or with no corpse at all. The player shortens distance with Blink and composes abilities the studio did not prescribe into one correct path. Chaos, the measure of violence, changes the city, the speech of characters, and the ending. It is not a subtle moral score; it is a visible plague and a different Dunwall.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/426-cover-1.jpg)

Arkane, under Raphaël Colantonio and Harvey Smith, an author with a past on Deus Ex, released the game through Bethesda in October 2012. The art direction of Viktor Antonov, known from City 17, gives the industrial whaling city a face. Unreal Engine 3 carries the vertical levels, the physics of bodies, and abilities that are meant to combine. Blink, stopping time, possession, and a swarm of rats are verbs, not only effects on a cooldown.

The decision to build a mission as a building with several lawful entrances means the player improvises. The consequence is that two passes through the Boyle masquerade need not share a single corridor. Verticality is part of the contract. Anyone who stays in the street plays a worse game than the one who goes by the roof and the chandelier.

In 2012 the immersive simulation is still a risky genre for a big publisher budget. Dishonored returns it to the mainstream in a costume that photographs well and still lets the systems run. Whale oil and plague make exhaustion the city's theme. The world's technology is the dirty magic of industry, not a clean future.

Binary chaos has aged, and after a while you read it as two tracks, as have some of the face animations. Some side streets between the big missions are also only a road. The levels as toys hold. The ball, the flooded district, and the empress's seat still serve as examples of how to let the player write their own solution.

The quiet path and the massacre path are both supported, not one correct and one endured. That is the difference from games that merely tolerate nonviolence. In class, open it on a single building and forbid the students the same entrance. They will see that the design is in how many sentences the same architecture can carry.
