John Marston hunts former gang members at the end of the American Wild West. It uses the open-world structure for the theme of the frontier's end and for the contrast between player freedom and an inevitable fate.

John Marston in 1911 rides a country that is just ceasing to be the Wild West, and he hunts former members of the gang. The player shoots, rides a horse, plays poker, and finishes contracts between which the landscape pretends it was not waiting for you. Dead Eye slows a firefight into a deliberate choice of targets. Honor changes how strangers speak to you, but the main story has a direction that the open map is not allowed to cancel.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/412-screenshot-1.jpg)

Rockstar San Diego wrote the game with Dan Houser and released it in May 2010. Rob Wiethoff plays John Marston. The studio at that time also carries the technical continuity of Grand Theft Auto IV. The RAGE engine and Euphoria animation hold falling bodies, riding, and shooting in one world. Mexico and the American territories are large, but missions still often close the world into a corridor once the scenario starts.

The decision to use an open world for the theme of the frontier's end means the player's freedom and the character's fate run against each other. You can ride off into the desert, and the story still pulls you back to the debt Marston is paying. The consequence is a rare western: the landscape invites wandering and at the same time keeps reminding you that historical time is closing, and that the railroad and federal power are already present in the country.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/412-screenshot-2.jpg)

In 2010, three years after GTA IV, Rockstar shows that the same technological skeleton can carry a slower genre. A western on a big console has no competitor of the same scale at that time. Camps, hunting, and the roads between towns make pace part of the theme. Anyone who hurries only from icon to icon misses why the map is so wide.

The horse has aged, as have some of the shooting and the way missions punish a departure from the script. Some side characters are also genre types, not people at Marston's level. The theme and the ending hold. The game can still use all the freedom it granted so that the final loss of choice hurts.

Undead Nightmare, still in 2010, manages to turn the same world into a horror game without touching the seriousness of the base campaign. That is a demonstration of how firm the geography is. In class, set it beside open games that only promise freedom. Here freedom is the theme, because the story eventually revokes it.
