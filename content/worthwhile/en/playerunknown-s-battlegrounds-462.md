One hundred players land without equipment and fight in a gradually shrinking zone. Battle-royale rules turned a large map into tension among looting, position, and inevitable encounters.

In PlayerUnknown's Battlegrounds you drop with a hundred people onto an island without equipment, and the circle shrinks until one remains. The player loots houses, chooses whether to drive through the center or the edge, and spends most of the match in tension, not in shooting. The sound of a shot is information about distance. The Erangel map and later maps are large so that the path to the next cover is a decision, not only a sprint across a street.

Brendan Greene, known as PlayerUnknown, carried the battle royale design out of mods, and in 2017 PUBG Corporation, tied to the Korean Bluehole, turned it into a standalone game on Unreal Engine 4. Early access begins in March and version 1.0 comes out in December. The circle is a technology of pace. A shrinking zone replaces a screenwriter who would have to make the players meet by hand, and at the same time it punishes camping by making the ground under you illegal.

The decision to start without a weapon and without a fair loadout means the first minute is a lottery and the rest is work with what the house gave you. The consequence is a genre in which inequality at the start is a rule, not a bug. A hundred players keep the bet that even a long silence is content. The studio bets against arena shooters, where the shooting never stops.

In 2017 battle royale becomes mainstream. Fortnite arrives with a building mode the same year and the market fills quickly, but the tension of the circle is named here as the first big standalone game. The technical state of early access, cheaters, and performance are part of the memory of 2017 as much as a winning match. The game is a cultural peak before it is fully finished.

That rawness of the service has aged, as has the position in the market, which later more accessible or faster battle royales took over. The maps themselves are no longer the only place the genre is played. The tension curve holds: loot, relocation, inevitable contact. A new battle royale is still judged by whether it understands why the circle exists at all.

Third person and first person are two different readabilities of cover. Greene and the team let players argue about which is fairer instead of choosing one forever. In class, take it as the year a mod rule became an industry. The circle is a simple sentence with an enormous consequence for how you play for time and for position.
