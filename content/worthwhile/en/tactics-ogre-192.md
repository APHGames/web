A political civil war branches according to the protagonist's moral decisions. Height-based tactics, permanent consequences of choices, and political grayness fundamentally influenced Final Fantasy Tactics.

In a war that has no clean side, you lead units on a grid with height, and a decision in the story closes a path you will not walk again as if nothing had happened. Politics is not a meter in a menu that could be caught up by a side quest. It is a branch on which the people you did not save are missing.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/192-cover-1.jpg)

Yasumi Matsuno wrote and directed the game in 1995 at Quest on the Super Famicom. Before he leaves for Square to make Final Fantasy Tactics, he is already saying here that tactics can be a tragedy, not just chess. The engine is turn-based and height-based. An attack from above is not the scenery of a battle; it is a number that changes whether you may stand where you are standing at all.

The decision that a moral choice has a permanent consequence for the campaign influences the whole genre. The player stops expecting that a bad chapter can be rewritten by another battle with the same result. In 1995 this is a Super Famicom game that will reach the West properly only in later versions. For the history of the design, the Japanese original and its gray politics are what matter.

Classes and height hold the tactical depth even when the story is briefly silent. The system is not only a carrier of branching; it can also be a match. What has aged is the density of the interface and a strictness that can break a new player before they understand why a choice is gray.

What has not aged is the joining of politics and loss. Final Fantasy Tactics will take its seriousness from here, even if it will speak of a different world. Matsuno's text is not looking for a hero without guilt. The commander is part of a war that spoils even those who arrived with a plan.

Compare the permanence with Fire Emblem. There the law is the death of a unit; here the law is also the death of a possibility, a political branch that is no longer in the game. In a seminar, describe one choice without the detail of a massacre and ask what the students would expect to be able to take back. If they expect a return, Matsuno disappoints that expectation on purpose.
