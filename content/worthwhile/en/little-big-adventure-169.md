Twinsen escapes a dictatorship in the isometric world of Twinsun. Character behavior modes and a fluid joining of exploration, dialogue, and action give the world a distinctive systemic rhythm.

Twinsen on the planet Twinsun switches between behaviors: normal, athletic, aggressive, and discreet. One body is several modes, not several genre minigames. The isometric world joins a walk, a sentence, and a brawl without throwing you into another menu screen. The rhythm is in how you are behaving at the moment.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/169-cover-1.jpg)

Frédérick Raynal, after leaving Infogrames, founded Adeline, and Electronic Arts published the game in 1994. In America it came out as Relentless: Twinsen's Adventure; in Europe as Little Big Adventure. The engine is isometric and fluid on DOS. The character has an animation that holds the mood of the mode, so aggression is not just a higher attack number.

The decision to divide the controls into behaviors has a consequence. The same street is a different game when you walk quietly, and a different one when you enter it with a fist. In 1994 the European action adventure looks for its own camera between Doom and point-and-click. Adeline chooses a fairy tale that can also be political.

The dictatorship on Twinsun is not scenery for a joke. Escape and rebellion give the modes a reason: discretion is survival, not a talent in a menu. What has aged are the camera and the combat. Some blows are awkward, and orientation in isometry asks for a patience the fairy tale on the box does not promise.

What has not aged is the idea that a character's personality is a switch of rules. Later games will divide behavior differently; here it is still legible in four positions. The two names of the game are a lesson in localization. The same Twinsen arrives in the West as a different title, which confuses the family tree, not the rules.

Compare the space with Alone in the Dark, Raynal's previous game. There the fear comes from a fixed camera; here the freedom comes from an isometric town that is alive. In a seminar, switch mode in the middle of one street and change nothing else. The students will see the design without further exposition.
