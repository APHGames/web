Physicist Lester is teleported into a hostile alien world. Minimalist direction narrated without words, and vector animation, prove that atmosphere can arise directly from movement and editing.

The physicist Lester falls into an alien world and, without a single line of exposition, learns to run, jump, and trust a being that does not speak his language. The game lets you die so that you understand a rule. A trap is a cut, not a tutorial table.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/121-cover-1.jpg)

Éric Chahi made it in 1991 at Delphine almost on his own, for the Amiga and the Atari ST. It is an auteur game in a time when European computers still feed small studios. The characters are vector polygons, not hand-drawn sprites. Thanks to that the camera can cut as in a film and the body still moves in perspective.

The decision to throw out the text has a consequence. The story is in the walking, in a mutual look, and in the moment the game lets you go on. In 1991 the Amiga is still a canvas for the European experiment. Another World shows on it that atmosphere can be editing, not an illustration in the corner.

The partner you meet replaces dialogue. Cooperation is animation: he opens a door, you hold a fall, and none of it is in the manual as a sentence. What has aged is the repetition of death. Some puzzles are a single correct step, and the player finds it only by falling.

What has not aged is the speech without words. Later games, from Ico to today's quiet platformers, still owe Chahi this editing. The letterbox and the short scenes keep the feeling that you are playing a film that hands you the controller only in the action windows.

Compare it with Prince of Persia. Mechner gives the body weight and the text a minimum; Chahi takes even the remaining words and lets the camera speak. In a seminar, do not start with the word cinematic. Start with the question of what the player knows without anyone having written it down.

Another World is a cinematic platform action-adventure game designed by Éric Chahi and published by Delphine Software in November 1991. The game tells the story of Lester, a young scientist who, as a result of an experiment gone wrong, finds himself on a dangerous alien world where he is forced to fight for his survival.

Another World was developed by Chahi alone over a period of about two years, with help with the soundtrack from Jean-François Freitas. Another World was innovative in its use of cinematic effects in both real-time and cutscenes, which earned the game praise among critics and commercial success.

Because he wanted to create a dramatic, cinematic experience, the game features no HUD or dialog, giving the player only a representation of the surrounding game world during both gameplay elements and the cutscenes progressing the story. One ending captured on these storyboards, but abandoned, was Lester becoming the leader of the alien world.

Another World took two years"; Chahi noted his own exhaustion at completing this project is mirrored in the near-death of Lester at the end of the game. The game was published in North America under the title Out of This World in order to avoid confusion with the popular but unrelated soap opera television series Another World.

Coincidentally, the science fiction sitcom Out of This World aired at the same time as the game's USA-Canada release. Chahi said: "I like this extra level a lot because it reinforces the close relationship between the hero and the alien by developing their mutual aid." Another World is the only game directly ported from the Super NES to the Apple IIGS, which has the same 65C816 microprocessor.

Chahi acquired the rights to Another World s intellectual property from Delphine Software International after they closed down in July 2004. The administrator's letter to stakeholders mentions that its assets will be sold, but does not indicate to whom or mention Another World assets by name.

A Jaguar port of Another World was originally in development and planned to be published by Interplay in September 1994, but it was never released. This version is part of the Another World 15th Anniversary Edition CD-ROM released in 2007, which also includes a development diary, an exclusive postcard autographed by Chahi and a separate soundtrack CD.

According to Digital Lounge, the goal was to "simply to deliver the original experience of Another World faithfully, with the benefits of today's hardware and a high level of polish". Another World, along with Flashback, shipped as a single retail package by Microids for the PlayStation 4, Switch, and Xbox One on April 16, 2020 in Europe.

Another World was commercially successful, selling about one million copies during the 1990s. Among many other accolades, Another World was named as number one top new Amiga game of 1992 by Amiga World and received the award for the Most Innovative New Game of the year from Electronic Gaming Monthly.

Retrospectively, Kristan Reed of Eurogamer called Another World "one of the most visionary and memorable games of its time." Reviewing the 15th Anniversary Edition in 2010, Eurogamer s John Walker called it "still utterly beautiful", adding that the game's art style is "just fantastic. The game can be brutal and heartbreaking, but Chahi's amazing vision makes [it] a thoroughly gripping experience." That same year, Lucas Sullivan of GamesRadar named it as number one top "cult-classic franchise" that should be rebooted in a way Prince of Persia was, commenting that "despite a small cast of characters, minimalist gameplay, and some truly grueling difficulty, Another World&nbsp;...

They described the game as "A moody but amazingly fluid game which faultlessly takes you between action sections and plot scenes drawing you into the excellent story." In 1995, Flux listed the game 73rd in its Top 100 Video Games writing: "Another giant leap in cinematic gameplay, every bit as cool and smooth as the later Flashback, but far weirder. In the same year, MegaZone included the game on their Top 50 Games In History, summarizing: "Great depth and strange but functional graphics give it a movie-like feel." IGN listed Out of This World 69th in their "Top 100 SNES Games of All Time." The alien "Beast" creature from the game's first level was ranked sixth on the GameSpot s 1999 top list of best monsters in gaming and IGN ranked the game's laser gun as the 86th best weapon in gaming history in 2012.

The company that produced Another World, Delphine Software, has since gone into administrative receivership and Another World remains their most recognized game. Paul Cuisset's best-selling 1992 game Flashback, also released by Delphine but created without any involvement from Chahi, features similar gameplay and graphics and makes a few nods to Another World, including the use of personal force fields in combat, a nearly identical end text in the ending cutscene, and an almost exact motion recreation of the gun pickup cutscene.

In 1998, he and his company Amazing Studio made Heart of Darkness, which is in many ways a spiritual successor to Another World.
