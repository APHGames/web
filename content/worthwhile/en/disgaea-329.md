The demon Laharl fights through an absurd Netherworld. Deliberately exaggerated levels, damage numbers, and resettable Item World progression make grinding itself the game.

In Disgaea you lead the demon Laharl through an absurd netherworld, and the tactical battles are only a vestibule. The real game begins when you enter an item and level it floor by floor. The Item World is a randomly assembled dungeon inside a piece of equipment. Grinding thereby stops being a tax on the story and becomes a toy you switch on deliberately.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/329-cover-1.jpg)

Nippon Ichi Software published it in 2003 on the PlayStation 2. Takehito Harada drew the characters, and the damage numbers are meant from the start as a joke that grows into a system. Character and item levels climb into the thousands. The consequence is that ordinary tactics of surviving a round give way to the question of how high you dare to go after the story has long ended.

Geo panels and the switching of their colors make the map a puzzle. It is not only whom to kill but which square to let explode first. In 2003, beside Final Fantasy Tactics and Fire Emblem, this is a deliberate exaggeration. The series takes serious genres and shows that their numbers can bear a parody without ceasing to function.

The density of jokes and menus has aged into fatigue. The bet that repetition can be the main content has not aged as a stance. Reincarnating a character erases the level and keeps the learned abilities. Progress is a loop, not a ladder with one end.

For students of RPG economy it is an extreme on which you can see when a reward becomes the activity itself. Whoever waits for the grind to end is playing a different game from the one that was released. Etna and Flonne hold a comic tone even inside the statistics. Character is the way a number laughs at you, not only a portrait in the corner.

Later games inflate the system further. The first is still the one that in 2003 said a level cap of 99 was unnecessary modesty. When you play it today, do not finish it with the story. The story is an introductory course. The game is the Item World, which it lets you into only afterward.

Some maps in Disgaea contain Geo Panels, which are represented as squares on the floor of the map of a particular color. Disgaea has only one pair of stores where items may be purchased, consisting of an armor shop and a weapon shop within Laharl's castle.

The story of Disgaea opens with Laharl rising out of an open coffin in his room. Disgaea's North American release allows the player to select either English or Japanese for the audio and soundtrack.

Souhei Niikawa and Yoshitsuna Kobayashi, the game's producer and main programmer, have explained the intended humor of Disgaea; they give the example of Captain Gordon being a satire of American comic book characters. The makers of Disgaea have responded that characters represented by 3-D models in other games are often limited by a "set pattern of motions", and they were able to make the characters of Disgaea more expressive with sprites.

In the PS2 category of IGN s The Best of 2003 awards, Disgaea: Hour of Darkness won "Best Game No One Played", and won Reader's Choice and runner-up awards for "Best Strategy Game". The game received a score of 8.5/8/7.5 from Electronic Gaming Monthly, and the magazine's Shane Bettenhausen said, "Here's an instant cult classic—Disgaea is so creative, challenging, and funny that it totally deserves the attention of RPG gamers".

The success of Disgaea: Hour of Darkness has launched a franchise that has seen seven sequels, several spin-offs, and adaptations into other media. It was later released in North America on October 30, 2007, under the name Disgaea: Afternoon of Darkness.

There are some extra boss battles such as Overlord Zetta from Makai Kingdom and Rozalin and Adell from Disgaea 2. To reflect Etna's recent voice actor change, her lines have been re-recorded to match Michelle Ruff's portrayal of Etna in Disgaea 2 and the anime.

On March 7, NIS America released a statement on the North American release of a new edition of Disgaea: Hour of Darkness for the Nintendo DS titled Disgaea DS.
