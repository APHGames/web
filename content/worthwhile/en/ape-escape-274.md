The hero catches monkeys using various gadgets. The first major title to require the DualShock worked with both analog sticks as the core of the design.

With one stick you run, with the other you swing a net or another gadget, and the monkeys you catch are not one wave of enemies. Each wants a different tool and a different joke in space. The DualShock is not extra support. The game requires it, and in 1999 that is still a distinct gesture: a controller with two analog sticks is a condition of the design, not a comfort.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/274-cover-1.jpg)

Japan Studio and Sony released it in 1999 on the PlayStation. The studio builds the game around a peripheral that the console is only just teaching the audience to treat as standard, and the monkeys are the pretext for why you need two sticks at once. The decision to split movement and action between the two hands had a consequence that looks obvious today. In 1999 it is a textbook: the right stick is not a camera, it is a verb, and that is why the game cannot be finished on the older digital controller.

The gadgets, the net, the stun club, the propeller, the slingshot, unlock, and a level becomes a set of locks. It is a platformer that takes the progression of abilities from the metroidvania and keeps the comedy. In 1999, beside Ape Escape, stand games that merely tolerate the analog stick. This one needs it, and that is why it belongs in a class on hardware as much as in a class on level design.

What has aged is the camera and the repetition of the hunt once you understand the joke of a gadget. What has not aged is the sentence that two sticks can be two different verbs, which the whole action game will then take over. In seminar, let students play for a while with movement only. Without the second hand the monkeys stay in the tree, and the design names itself.

Against Crash, which solved the corridor with one camera direction, there is a freer space here, and the tax on it is exactly orientation. Sony is trying a mascot here that is not only a running model. The comedy of the monkeys is a tone, not a system. The system is the gadget as a key, and a seminar should not end at the cuteness.

The game is also important negatively: it shows how much of the 1999 audience does not yet have two sticks in hand. The DualShock requirement is a social fact, not only a sentence in the manual. When students pick up any later action game after it, they will see the inheritance in the fact that the right thumb is no longer only looking, it is acting.

Ape Escape is a 1999 platform game developed and published by Sony Computer Entertainment for the PlayStation. The first game in the Ape Escape series, the game tells the story of an ape named Specter who gains enhanced intelligence and a malevolent streak through the use of an experimental helmet.

Development of Ape Escape lasted over two years, and was generally focused on adapting to the use of the controller, which was a significant challenge for the development team. Ape Escape was met with critical acclaim from critics, with praise particularly directed at the innovative use of the dual analog controls, as well as the graphics and music; the voice acting received minor criticism.

The game also spawned numerous sequels and spin-offs, beginning with Ape Escape 2 in 2002. A remake, Ape Escape: On the Loose, was released for the PlayStation Portable in 2005 to mixed reviews.

Ape Escape is a platform and action-adventure game viewed from a third-person perspective.
