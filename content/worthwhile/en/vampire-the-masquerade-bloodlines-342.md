A vampire navigates the Los Angeles politics of several clans. Clan dramatically changes both dialogue and the way of playing; reactive quests preserved the immersive-sim spirit despite technical problems.

In Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines you are a new vampire in Los Angeles, and the clan you choose at the start rewrites both the dialogue and the way you move through the city. A Nosferatu cannot walk the street like the others. A Malkavian hears lines the other clans do not get. Quests have more than one solution: speech, break-in, violence, a clan discipline. The spirit of the immersive sim survived here even in a year when big RPGs begin to flatten into one path.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/342-cover-1.jpg)

Troika, the studio of Tim Cain, Leonard Boyarsky, and Jason Anderson, published it in 2004 through Activision on an early Source engine. The game shipped notoriously unfinished and the studio ended soon after. The consequence of the clan is that a build is not only a combat statistic. It is a social contract with a city that either lets you into the club or hunts you already at the door.

An unofficial community patch became the way to finish the game at all. Historically it is a case where players completed the work, because the publisher's date arrived before stability. In 2004, beside smooth console RPGs, this is a PC game that bets on reactive text. It lost commercially and won in the canon precisely by that text.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/342-screenshot-2.jpg)

The shooting and some of the animations have aged into awkwardness. The dialogues, Santa Monica, Hollywood, and the politics of the clans have not. The Ocean House hotel shows that a level can be a horror exception inside an RPG. The authors can change genre for a while without breaking the city around it.

For students of choice the clan is a textbook. The choice on the first screen has to be expensive enough that it is still being discussed in the last act. White Wolf's World of Darkness is a license that carries rules, not only names. The Masquerade, the ban on revealing yourself to humans, is both system and theme.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/342-screenshot-3.jpg)

When Troika ended, one branch of the immersive sim outside Looking Glass and Ion Storm left with it. Bloodlines is its testament on Source. When you play it today, install the patch first and only then choose a clan. Without the patch you are studying a crash. With it you are studying a design that survived the crash.

Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines is a 2004 action role-playing video game developed by Troika Games and published by Activision for Microsoft Windows. Set in White Wolf Publishing's World of Darkness, the game is based on White Wolf's role-playing game Vampire: The Masquerade and follows a human who is killed and revived as a fledgling vampire.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/342-screenshot-4.jpg)

Bloodlines is presented from first-person and third-person perspectives. The player assigns their character to one of several vampire clans each with unique powers—customizes their combat and dialog abilities, and progresses through Bloodlines using violent and nonviolent methods.

Troika's 32-member team began developing Bloodlines in November 2001 as an indirect sequel to the previous year's Vampire: The Masquerade – Redemption. After three years in development with no end in sight and running over budget, Activision set a strict deadline for completion, and Bloodlines was released incomplete in November 2004.

Released in competition with Half-Life 2 and several other titles, Bloodlines sold fewer than 80,000 copies during its initial release, which was considered a poor performance. Since its original release, Bloodlines received post-release support from fans, supplying unofficial fixes and re-adding unused content.

A sequel, Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines 2, was released in 2025. Bloodlines is an action role-playing video game optionally presented from the first- or third-person perspective.
