The player flies over a digitally reconstructed Earth in real aircraft. Cloud data, satellite source material, and procedural reconstruction of the terrain brought a simulation of Earth at an unprecedented geographic scale.

In Microsoft Flight Simulator from 2020 you sit in a specific aircraft, and beneath you is an Earth that does not load from one hand-glued map. The player takes off from a real airport, holds a pattern, and can land a continent away if they have the weather, the fuel, and the patience. Assists can be turned up to a game that almost flies itself, or turned off into procedures that require a checklist. The studio counts on two hands: a tourist, and a person who wants to know the relationship of flaps and speed.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/484-cover-1.jpg)

Asobo Studio released the game with Xbox Game Studios in August 2020. The series reaches back to the flight simulator of 1982, and this version stands on Bing map data, on photogrammetry of cities, and on the Azure cloud, which pulls the landscape in during the flight. The decision not to build the planet as a game level, but to assemble it from satellites and from procedural buildings where detail is missing, means an unprecedented scale. The consequence is also a dependence on the network, on disk space, and on the fact that one city is sharp and the next is only a model.

Weather and live data, which accumulate in updates, turn a flight into a report on the day, not only on a scenario. The same route yesterday and today is not the same game. The cockpits of licensed aircraft are part of the contract with a serious audience. The accessible mode beside that keeps the door open for people a checklist would drive out.

In 2020 this is a technical manifesto in the middle of a year that otherwise belongs to battle royales and islands. A forty-year-old brand returns as a demonstration of what the cloud can do with geographic memory. Asobo had not previously been a studio of big flight simulators. The move from smaller games to a planet is a production risk that shows in the scale, not in a single mission.

The launcher has aged, as have the demands of the installation and the places where a procedural house or tree feels foreign. The online dependence also means that without a network the game is not the planet it promises. The scale holds. It is still the clearest example of how to stop pretending a game world is a closed map, and instead to stream a world that already exists.

A VFR flight along a river and an IFR flight by instruments are two pedagogies. The game can bear both, which is why it can be used in class and also as a sightseeing flight. In class, open it over a place students know from the window. The difference between a memory and the model is a whole lecture about data, not about the aircraft.

Microsoft Flight Simulator, within PC as well as Xbox Series X and Series S, received "universal acclaim" from video game critics, according to review aggregator website Metacritic. On OpenCritic, another aggregator, it was rated "Mighty" based on 86 critics, along with the summary: "Microsoft Flight Simulator is a technical marvel, with an insane amount of polish, incredible realism, and phenomenal controls." Many reviewers have placed it among the best games of the year and a gaming essential, with Kat Bailey of USGamer awarding it August 2020's Game of the Month.

Reviewers considered the graphics and realism of Flight Simulator as the reason it stands out, with Tokarev Kirill of 80 LEVEL and Chad Sapieha of Common Sense Media describing its authenticity as unprecedented, and other critics calling it an aesthetic feat within PC gaming. The mainstream media also viewed Flight Simulator in a positive light.

According to Paul Sillers, writing for CNN, Flight Simulator might be "the safest way to travel" during the COVID-19 pandemic, and because of the global economic conditions in the wake of COVID-19, is being used by furloughed pilots to keep their skills fresh. As "a comprehensive simulator that steadily guides players from square one to being able to fly, combined with kitchen-sink graphics", The Guardian concluded that Flight Simulator "captures the wonder of flight, and the spiritual and emotional rush of seeing the world in a different way".
