A new trainer travels Johto and later the original Kanto as well. A real-time clock, breeding, and the return of the entire first region dramatically expanded the idea of a handheld sequel.

In Johto the clock runs even when the Game Boy is off. Day and night change whom you meet, and after the championship the whole of Kanto opens, so the sequel does not throw the previous country away, it connects it. Breeding on the farm and the split of the special stat into attack and defense are systems, not seasonal novelties. The collection becomes a project across generations of creatures, and combat reads different numbers than in Red and Green.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/277-cover-1.jpg)

Game Freak, again with Satoshi Tajiri, Ken Sugimori, and Junichi Masuda directing, released it in Japan in 1999 on the Game Boy Color. A cartridge with a battery carries the clock, and that is the technical condition of the day, not only a calendar in a menu. The decision to return the entire first region had a consequence for the scale of a handheld. Children who grew up on Red and Green get their map back as a reward, and the sequel becomes a reunion, not a replacement.

In 1999 the Game Boy Color is a small machine beside the PlayStation and the N64. Game Freak puts two countries and time onto it and bets that the pocket will still beat the showcase if the world continues in it even while the console is in a bag. What has aged is the level curve in Kanto, which can be a stroll after the victory, and the still-present need for some trades over the cable. What has not aged is the rhythm of the day and the reason to turn the game on because it is evening, not because you have time.

In seminar, compare one hour by day and by night on the same route. Students will see that content can be a schedule, not another city. Johto is slower and more intimate than later regions. The pace is a tone, and a seminar should not apologize for it as a lack of content.

Against Red and Green there is less shock here that the collection is social, and more shock that the world has time. The first game invented the trade, the second invented that the game lives when you are not playing it. Masuda and the team keep the rules readable. Types, a team of six, and badges are still enough for a school bench, and the clock is a new sentence, not a new tax.

The return of Kanto is also a production courage on a small cartridge. The machine's memory turns into sentiment here, and the sentiment has an address on the map. The game is the peak of the sentence that a handheld sequel should be a larger life, not only larger graphics. Later generations will handle that promise differently, and this entry remains the measure.
