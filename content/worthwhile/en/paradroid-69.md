The player takes control of robots aboard a spaceship. The "transfer" mechanic turns enemies into temporary classes of the player character and creates strategy from the choice of body.

Aboard an alien ship you are only a small transfer device, and you survive by taking over robots whose number states their power; the higher the hull, the harder it is to control. The transfer is not a menu. It is a short duel over a circuit in which you can fail and remain in a weak body in the middle of a deck that does not want you.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/069-cover-1.jpg)

Andrew Braybrook wrote it at Graftgold for Hewson, and it was released on the Commodore 64 in 1985. It is a British game that turns a limited machine into a deck with dozens of enemy classes. The view combines a map of the ship and action movement across the deck. A robot is not just a sprite with health, it is a temporary class you put on.

The decision that your power should be someone else's body had the consequence that progress is a risky exchange. Killing a strong robot is sometimes worse than becoming it. The numbers, from weak servants up to heavy guards, form a ladder you read on the run. The inventory is what you are currently walking around in.

In 1985 the C64 is full of arcade ports. Paradroid looks among them like a systems game: fewer explosions, more choice of which body you can afford. Braybrook's signature, later known from other shooters as well, is not here in the density of shots, but in the enemy being an economy.

What has aged is the confusion of the deck once a newcomer does not understand why they should not shoot everything they see. What has not aged is the exchange of a body as progress, and the fact that the best weapon in the room may be the one you are not yet wearing.

From this ship, games in which taking over an enemy is a better sentence than deleting them learn something. In a seminar, forbid the students to stay in the first body. Once they have to risk a transfer, they understand why the game is not named after a weapon.

Paradroid is a Commodore 64 video game written by Andrew Braybrook and published by Hewson Consultants in 1985. It was remade as Paradroid 90 for the Commodore Amiga and Atari ST home computers and as Paradroid 2000 for the Acorn Archimedes.

In the same series, he revealed that they had redone the Paradroid graphics in the new (Morpheus) style, which was later released as Heavy Metal Paradroid. Andrew Braybrook did another diary during the development of Paradroid 90 for Amiga Action.

Also in a 2002 Zzap!64 tribute publication, Paradroid via a community vote was ranked the best C64 game ever with the comment "there is something about Paradroid that sets it apart from other C64 games". Praising how all of the large game fit into the Commodore 64's memory, the magazine concluded that "if you like science fiction, arcade games, inspiring graphics, and intricate gameplay, you will LOVE Paradroid!".

Paradroid 90 received "C+VG Hit" with a score of 93% in Computer and Video Games Other clones and games inspired by Paradroid. Andrew Braybrook's Graftgold partner, Steve Turner, wrote a version of Paradroid for the ZX Spectrum called Quazatron in 1986, also published by Hewson.
