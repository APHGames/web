A wizard conquers two fantasy worlds with armies and magic. Civilization-like expansion joined with tactical battles and an enormous spell system.

A wizard occupies two worlds, Arcanus and Myrror, leads armies in tactical battles, and instead of technologies researches a spellbook that is almost a game of its own. A city, a hero, and a spell compose one another. The journey on the map is not a decoration of the battle; the battle is a consequence of whom you met on the map.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/170-cover-1.jpg)

Steve Barcia and Simtex released the game in 1994 through MicroProse, a year after Master of Orion. The same studio, a different element: instead of the diplomacy of stars, there is magic as a tree. The engine is a turn-based map and a tactical field. On DOS the first version is notorious for instability, so part of the game's history is in the patches without which the system is hard to judge.

The decision to replace science with spells has a consequence. Two empires differ not only in units; they differ in which sentence from the book they can cast before the opponent. In 1994 this is a fantasy 4X beside Civilization and Orion. Barcia bets that the player wants a tactical battle, not only a result calculated in a window.

Two planes of the map, light and dark, double the space without a new planet. A portal between them is a strategic rule, not scenery. What has aged are the interface and the fragility of the release. Whoever judges the game from the first version without fixes is also judging errors the community later kept in check.

What has not aged is the combinatorics of the spells. There is still something to discover in how a buff, a phantom, and a city compose an evening's match. Compare the compactness with Orion. There, a galaxy and diplomacy; here, a book that can be longer than the map.

In a seminar, separate what is Civilization and what is a tactical battle. Master of Magic is interesting precisely for that seam, not because it carried one genre the furthest. If you mention the patches, mention them as the fate of a system that was richer than the build in which it left the house.

Master of Magic is a single-player fantasy turn-based 4X strategy game developed by MuHa Games and published by Slitherine Software, in which the player plays as a wizard attempting to dominate two linked worlds. The gameplay of the game, both the original and the remake, has been compared to the classic video game Civilization, enhanced with features like heroes and magic.

The game is a remake of the 1994 MS-DOS game Master of Magic by MicroProse. Both the original and the remake feature five schools of magic, which many reviewers believe was inspired by the card game Magic the Gathering.

The player - a wizard in control of newly founded magic kingdom - can customize the skills, spell choices and appearance of their wizard, choosing one of 14 races for the starting city. During exploration, the player defeats monsters that are guarding treasure, finds the best locations for new cities, conquers existing ones, recruit heroes, equip them with magical artifacts, and steadily expands their magic kingdom, eventually coming into conflict with other kingdoms controlled by AI-run wizards.
