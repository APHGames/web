Geralt of Rivia resolves political and moral conflicts after losing his memory. Often deliberately delayed consequences of choices undermine easy "good/evil" decision-making.

In The Witcher, Geralt is a witcher with a hole in his memory, and decisions you make in one chapter come back a chapter later, when you no longer remember them as a button. Good and evil here are not a bar at the end of a dialogue. Alchemy is drunk in meditation, not in the middle of a blow. Preparation is part of combat, and whoever walks into a swamp without oil on the blade plays a different, worse game.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/380-screenshot-1.jpg)

CD Projekt Red of Warsaw published it in 2007 through Atari as the studio's first large game, based on the books of Andrzej Sapkowski. It runs on a heavily modified Aurora from Neverwinter Nights, and that is felt in the camera and in the division of locations. The consequence of the delayed consequence is that the player cannot optimize morality in one window. A choice is a bet, and the game lets you live with the fact that you did not know, then, whom you were actually protecting.

In 2007 it is a rough European RPG beside the smoother BioWare games. The studio that will grow from it into a global publisher is still small here, and the game is still raw. The combat of timed clicking and some of the English dub have aged. The moral structure and the decision to tell a new story in the world of the books, not to retell a book, have not.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/380-screenshot-2.jpg)

The cards for romance scenes are a period, adolescent signature, and today they feel like a price for an adulthood the game otherwise means seriously. It is worth separating them from what actually works in the choices. The Polish dub is the primary version for many players. The English localization is a translation of a work that thinks in a Central European myth, not in a generic fantasy map.

For adaptation students it is a class about fidelity. Sapkowski's tone, the lesser evil, and a world that has no clean king can be carried into a system without Geralt walking the plot of the books again. The later games exchange Aurora for their own technology and the combat for action. The first is still the one that in 2007 said consequences can wait.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/380-screenshot-3.jpg)

The chapters are closed cases. A detective structure holds an RPG that will spill over in the open world of the third game, and here it is still readable as a series of decisions. When you play it today, do not save before every dialogue. The game was written for a player who does not take a choice back, and only that player will see why a chapter returns.

The Witcher ( ) is a 2007 action role-playing game developed by CD Projekt Red for Windows and Mac OS X. It was based on the fantasy novel series The Witcher by Polish author Andrzej Sapkowski.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/380-cover-4.jpg)

The Witcher is powered by a modified version of the Aurora Engine by BioWare, but using its own rendering engine. New realistic skyboxes and water effects designed specifically for The Witcher were added to the engine.

On 2 December, CD Projekt Red officially confirmed that The Witcher would be ported to PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 consoles and released as The Witcher: Rise of the White Wolf. For many years, CD Projekt Red did not publicly disclose detailed sales figures for The Witcher.

According to Nowakowski, The Witcher (2007) has sold over 10 million copies worldwide.
