The player mines, builds, and fights in a procedural 2D world. It joins Minecraft-like material freedom with bosses, equipment, and a long action-RPG progression.

In Terraria you dig a pixel world in every direction, build houses for NPCs, and gradually discover that bosses are waiting beyond the next biomes, not only more ore. The player mines, crafts, fights, and at night checks whether the doors are in place. Progression is a long action RPG: equipment, altars, and finally a change of the world after which the map starts to behave differently.

![Artwork](/img/worthwhile-games/media/424-artwork-1.jpg)

Andrew Spinks and Re-Logic released the game in May 2011. It began as a two-dimensional answer to Minecraft, but with a clear sequence of bosses and a feel for items closer to an action RPG than to a pure construction toy. It originally runs on Microsoft XNA, a common path for small PC games in those years and later a technical ceiling as well, because the platform ends. The studio keeps patching and porting the game for years instead of shipping a one-off sequel.

The decision to join free digging to an order of bosses means freedom has a threshold. The consequence is a world you can rebuild, but in which a direction still exists: you defeat a biome's guardian and a harder layer opens. The Corruption or the Crimson are not only a palette. They are an infection of the map that you have to read, or it will eat your base.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/424-cover-2.jpg)

In 2011 it feels like Minecraft's smaller sibling. Over years of updates it becomes its own standard of two-dimensional survival with RPG length. NPCs who move into suitable houses turn architecture into a quest. A room is not decoration; it is the condition of a shop and a service.

The early onboarding has aged, as has some of the interface, which assumes a wiki. The quantity of items in later updates can also overwhelm a newcomer. The joining of a construction toy and combat holds. It is still the best example of how to give Minecraft's cube a thirst for a sword and a boss.

Hardmode after defeating the Wall of Flesh is a bold decision to change the rules of biomes in the middle of the game. The world you had made safe becomes dangerous again. In class, set it beside Minecraft as two answers to the same year: one believes in freedom, the other in freedom with a ladder of enemies.

Initially released in May 2011 on the Steam digital store, Terraria has received continuous content updates that have considerably changed it from its first version. Terraria has since seen the addition of new items, NPCs, enemies, and world difficulties, as well as quality-of-life improvements and crossovers with different games.

Despite announcing in 2020 that the game's fourth major update would be its last, Re-Logic has continued developing Terraria. As of 2026, Terraria has sold over 70 million copies, making it one of the best-selling video games of all time.

Re-Logic developed Terraria: Otherworld from 2015 until it was canceled in 2018. Terraria world]] Terraria is a sandbox, action-adventure, role-playing, and platformer video game.

Terraria is noted for its classic exploration-adventure style of gameplay, similar to games such as Minecraft. Terraria has several difficulty modes: the "Classic" mode, and "Expert" and "Master" modes, both of which increase the difficulty in exchange for exclusive items.

Terraria has support for modifications, which is facilitated by the third-party tModLoader, an open source modification of Terraria that allows players to use other custom-made modifications. Others, such as Terraria Overhaul, add new game and combat mechanics, including seasons, fire spread, and modify the player's movement.

The Terraria community has also developed "modpacks", a type of a modification that bundles several different mods together, such as The Bereft Souls. Terraria was developed by the independent video game studio Re-Logic and published by 505 Games.

Andrew Spinks started developing Terraria in 2011 with support from a team of game testers and designers, including some volunteers. In September 2012, Spinks announced that Engine Software and 505 Games would port (convert the game to a different platform) Terraria to Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3.

Shortly after the initial console releases, 505 Games announced Terraria release for PlayStation Vita; it was released in Europe on December 11, 2013, and in North America on December 17 the same year. Spike Chunsoft promoted Terraria with broadcasts and by sharing video footage of the game on Niconico.

According to the company, most players who downloaded a demo version of Terraria for PlayStation 3 bought the full game. 505 Games announced a mobile version of Terraria in May 2013, ported by the Dutch studio Codeglue for Android, iOS, and Windows Phone.

Terraria was released for iOS on August 29, 2013, for Android on September 13, and for Windows Phone on September 12, 2014. In February 2021, Spinks announced the cancellation of Terraria Google Stadia version due to the unexplained suspension of Re-Logic's Google account, which had been ongoing for over three weeks.

Google restored the company's accounts later that month, and Re-Logic released Terraria for Stadia on March 18. In November 2021, a crossover update added content from the survival game Don't Starve Together (which received Terraria-themed content in return).

On the April Fools' Day of 2024, an early version of Terraria became available on Steam in a separate branch titled Terraria Undeluxe Edition, with another branch coming in May that contained the 1.1 update, dubbed the "First Final Update". Terraria replayability was praised by Jeffrey deMelo of TouchArcade and Ben Reeves of Game Informer; Christian Donlan saw the game as "an extremely enjoyable one" for Eurogamer, praising its inclusion of an in-game map.

Since tModLoader received official support from Re-Logic in 2020, modding has become an important part of the Terraria fan base. IndieWire wrote that modifications have "added hundreds of hours of new content" and that they effectively serve as "free advertising for Re-Logic and Terraria".

When the Opera GX browser ranked the 100 best video game soundtracks of all time in 2024, the soundtrack of the Calamity mod was ranked ninth; Terraria s soundtrack was not included in the list. Terraria sold over 50,000 copies on its first day, The game sold over 70 million copies by May 2026, making it one of the best-selling video games of all time.

Terraria 2 was announced in October 2013, Later, in 2022, Spinks published concept art for Terraria 2 that was created in its early development. In an interview with Famitsu, Ruud van de Moosdijk, the head of development of Terraria: Otherworld said that unlike Terraria, Otherworld would have a story.

In July 2022, Re-Logic announced the release of a Terraria graphic novel in a collaboration with 50 Amp Productions. A year later, Re-Logic announced cooperation with Paper Fort Games on creating a Terraria board game.
