A bartender serves several counters at once: pours a beer, sends it down the bar, and collects the empty glasses before the customer reaches the end and leaves angry. Each counter is the same process with different timing. The difficulty is not a new type of enemy, but the number of processes you have to hold in your head.

![Poster](/img/worthwhile-games/media/049-poster-1.jpg)

Marvin Glass and Associates designed the machine for Bally Midway, and it was released in 1983, originally with a prominent Budweiser brand on the cabinet. Later versions exchange the beer for root beer, so the game can pass into places and onto home ports where alcohol on the cabinet is a problem. The rule stays, the sign changes.

The decision to build the game out of repeated queues had the consequence that the master is the one who switches attention, not the one who presses one thing faster. A customer you fail to serve does not hurt you with a shot. They hurt you by slowing the counter and breaking the rhythm of the others. The punishment is the collapse of the system.

The cabinet's joke is social: the spectators see which counter is on fire before the player does. The comedy is part of the tension.

What has aged is beer advertising as a taken-for-granted decoration of the arcade, and a simplicity of graphics that now feels like a minigame. What has not aged is the cleanliness of the multitasking: four identical lines, different phases, and one character who cannot be at every end at once.

Tapper, also known as Root Beer Tapper, is a 1984 action video game developed by Marvin Glass and Associates and published by Bally Midway for arcades; in Japan, it was distributed by Sega. The re-themed Root Beer Tapper followed in 1984, which was developed specifically for arcades because the original version was construed as advertising alcohol to minors.

Despite retaining the original beer theme, it used the same Budweiser censorship as Root Beer Tapper. Music and sound effects for the arcade version of Tapper were created by Rick Hicaro of Marvin Glass & Associates.

Tapper was ported to the Apple II, Atari 8-bit computers, Atari 5200, Atari 2600, BBC Micro, ColecoVision, Commodore 64, MSX, ZX Spectrum, IBM PC, and Amstrad CPC. Most of the home versions of Tapper featured the Mountain Dew logo, while the ZX Spectrum and Amstrad CPC versions had the Pepsi logo, but they retained the bartender character of the original arcade game instead of the soda jerk in Root Beer Tapper.

The Root Beer Tapper version also appears on the Midway Legacy Edition Arcade1Up cabinet. In Japan, Game Machine listed Tapper as the most successful table arcade unit of March 1984, tied with 10-Yard Fight and Vs.

Compute!'s Gazette called the Commodore 64 version of Tapper "one of the most addictive games we've seen lately ... In fact, the main character in Timber is a rework of the main character in Tapper.

A reinterpretation of the game for mobile devices, Tapper World Tour, was released in 2011. A slightly altered version of Tapper appears as the Milk Bar minigame in Barnyard (2006).

A clone called Nuka Tapper is included in Fallout 76 as a minigame with Fallout-themed graphics. Tapper is one of the games included in Disney's 2012 film Wreck-It Ralph and its 2018 sequel Ralph Breaks the Internet with the bartender voiced by Maurice LaMarche.
