A deity alternates platform battles with the administration of growing cities. An unusual bond between two distinct genres gave progress both a macro level and a micro level.

As a deity you alternate platform battles in the body of a statue come to life with the administration of cities that send you offerings for their growth. In the action half you jump and strike. In the simulation half you build and watch a settlement fill, because your strength depends on its faith.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/119-cover-1.jpg)

Quintet developed the game in 1990 for the Super Famicom, and Enix published it. Yuzo Koshiro wrote the music, and it is part of why the game is remembered. The two modes do not share one engine in today's sense. They share a campaign: the city feeds the fight, and the fight opens the next region.

The decision to glue a platformer to divine administration has a consequence. Progress has the macro level of population and the micro level of the jump, so neither half is a bonus. In 1990, in the early SNES library, this is an unusually ambitious mixture. Most games pick one genre and stay with it.

The action difficulty is hard and the simulation is shallow. Quintet bets on contrast, not on both halves being equally deep. What has aged is precisely the shallowness of the cities. Construction is clear and short, and a player today recognizes a sketch in it, not a competitor to SimCity.

What has not aged is the music and the nerve to let the player be a god who still has to earn the dirt in person. No indirect cursor will save you in the cave. The enemy at the end is mythological, but the road to him leads through the houses of ordinary people. The scale is the point.

When you look for games that do not mix genres like party minigames, take ActRaiser. Here one half manufactures the condition for the other. It combines traditional side-scrolling platforming and sections with city building elements.

In 2007, ActRaiser became available on the Wii's Virtual Console download service in Europe, North America, and Japan. A remake, Actraiser Renaissance, was released for several platforms in 2021.

A remake under the title of Actraiser Renaissance was released in September 2021, for Android, iOS, Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 4, and Windows. A shorter arranged soundtrack titled Symphonic Suite from Actraiser was released on September 21.

The soundtrack was re-released by Wayô Records on vinyl and CD in 2021 as ActRaiser Original Soundtrack & Symphonic Suite. Koshiro would later return to not only fully recreate the original soundtrack for Actraiser Renaissance, but also composed fifteen new tracks, in both the original SNES sample quality and Renaissance's own higher quality audio.

The Japanese publication Micom BASIC Magazine ranked ActRaiser eighth in popularity in its March 1991 issue, and it received a 23.68/30 score in a 1993 readers' poll conducted by Super Famicom Magazine, ranking among Super Famicom titles at the number 26 spot. Quintet reported that ActRaiser sold 620,000 copies worldwide, with 400,000 copies sold in Japan, 180,000 copies sold in North America, and 40,000 copies sold in Europe.

ActRaiser was inducted into GameSpot s Greatest Games of All Time in December 2003. In 2007, ScrewAttack ranked ActRaiser #1 on their "Top 10 Big Names That Fell Off", which listed games that were popular in days past, whether good or bad, but have since faded into relative obscurity (ActRaiser was discussed positively).

In 2018, Complex ranked ActRaiser 33rd on their "The Best Super Nintendo Games of All Time" list. In 2017, Gamesradar ranked ActRaiser 23rd on its "The best SNES games of all time" list.
