The player earns licenses, buys cars, and races with extensive tuning. "Real Driving Simulator" joined car-collecting culture with more serious physics for a mass console audience.

You first take licenses on an empty track, and only then are you allowed to buy the car you wanted, and later still you take it apart into tires, a gearbox, and weight. The race is a consequence of the test. The game refuses to let you at the speed until you show that you can brake among cones.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/223-cover-1.jpg)

Kazunori Yamauchi and Polyphony Digital released it in 1997 on the PlayStation with the slogan real driving simulator. It is not an arcade driving game with a license in the title; it is a collection of real cars for a home console. The physics on the PS1 are simplified against later entries, and they are still a leap. Weight, surface, and tuning are read in the corner, not in a table you could ignore.

The decision to hide the powerful cars behind license tests had a consequence for the pace of the career. The collection is a promotion, and a console that knew arcades behaves here like a driving school with a catalog. Body damage is essentially absent. That is a conscious boundary of the simulation: the seriousness is in the driving and the maintenance, not in the demolition that Carmageddon takes for itself in the same year.

In 1997 a car game on a console is still often an arcade game. Gran Turismo brings the culture of tuning and factory marques to an audience that does not have a PC simulator. What has aged is the grind of some cups and the music, which holds the period more tightly than the physics. Learning with the brake has not aged, nor has the respect for the fact that cars differ by more than color.

In seminar, compare one B-license test with one race. You will see that the tutorial is not a separate chapter; it is a gate into the economy. Hundreds of cars are both content and a system. The more of them there are, the more important it is that the differences can be felt, otherwise the catalog is only wallpaper.

Yamauchi's team builds the game as a long project, not as a seasonal arcade game. The consequence is a series that will defend simulation on a console for decades. Against Mario Kart there is fairness here instead of an item. Two car games of the year teach two different reasons to turn a wheel.

After five years of development time, Gran Turismo was well-received both commercially and critically, shipping a total of 10.85 million copies worldwide (making it the best-selling PlayStation game), and scoring an average of 95% in GameRankings' aggregate, making it the highest rated racing video game at the time of the site's closure in 2019. Gran Turismo was the first game designed to fully support the PlayStation's DualShock controller.

Gran Turismo features 140 cars and 11 race tracks (as well as their reversed versions). The development personnel were largely the same team which was behind the earlier PlayStation racers Motor Toon Grand Prix and Motor Toon Grand Prix 2, and Gran Turismo uses parts of the Motor Toon game engine, such as the physics model.

Kazunori Yamauchi said that the development of Gran Turismo started in the second half of 1992. Since Motor Toon Grand Prix 2 was still in development when work on Gran Turismo started, several people only joined the development team after the completion of Motor Toon Grand Prix 2 freed them up to work on Gran Turismo.

When asked how difficult it was to create Gran Turismo, Yamauchi remarked: "It took five years. I estimate I was home only four days a year." At the time of Gran Turismo s Japanese release on December 23, 1997, Polys Entertainment was still a development group within Sony Computer Entertainment's internal development team.

Yamauchi estimated that Gran Turismo utilized around 75% of the PlayStation's maximum performance. Gran Turismo was released in Europe on May 8, 1998, The PAL and NTSC versions of Gran Turismo notably contain a soundtrack which features major label artists: Garbage, Manic Street Preachers (with a song remixed by The Chemical Brothers), Ash, Cubanate, Feeder (on PAL versions only), and TMF (on NTSC versions only).

In its first month of release in the United States, Gran Turismo was the second best-selling home console game, behind Tekken 3. By March 1999, Gran Turismo had sold over six million units worldwide, of which two million were derived from the United States.

It remains the best-selling video game for the PlayStation and the fifth highest-selling game in the Gran Turismo franchise, behind Gran Turismo 4, Gran Turismo 5, Gran Turismo Sport and Gran Turismo 3: A-Spec respectively. Gran Turismo received widespread acclaim, with praise for its graphics, It was classified as "universal acclaim" by review aggregator website Metacritic.

At the 1999 edition of the Milia D’Or Awards in Cannes, Gran Turismo won in the "Racing Game" category. The staff felt that the racing genre had not offered as "complete [a] package" as Gran Turismo.

In 2017, Gran Turismo was declared the best driving game ever by Top Gear. In 1999, Next Generation listed Gran Turismo as number 15 on their "Top 50 Games of All Time", commenting that, "Gran Turismo features cars that handle better than any other racing game ever made".

In 2006, Gran Turismo was inducted into GameSpot s list of the greatest games of all time. In 2015, IGN listed Gran Turismo as the second most influential racing game of all time (after Pole Position), calling it "the grandfather of all modern console racing sims." Notes.
