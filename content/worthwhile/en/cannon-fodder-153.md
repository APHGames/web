A small group of soldiers is led by the mouse through a series of fast missions. Simple RTS-like control contrasts with the permanent names and graves of the fallen, giving losses a human weight.

With the mouse you lead a small group of soldiers through short missions. A click is both an order and gunfire, so the tactic is fast to the point of an arcade game. Whoever dies has a name and a grave on the hill. The game first sells you the fun of war and then shows you the list of those you sent.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/153-cover-1.jpg)

Sensible Software, Jon Hare's studio, released the game in 1993 through Virgin on the Amiga. It is their tone after Sensible Soccer: tiny figures, a serious point hidden in a joke. The engine is a view from above and simple ballistics. There is no base and no harvesting; it is one screen, a few bodies, and a grenade.

The decision to give the dead names has a consequence for tone. The same controls that feel flippant start to hurt when the hill of graves grows between missions. In 1993 real-time strategy teaches armies. Cannon Fodder teaches that four soldiers and a mouse are enough if the loss stays on the screen.

Friendly fire is on. The simplicity of an order is also a trap, because a grenade does not know whom you meant to protect. What has aged is precisely that spareness when you play alone and without laughter at the table. Some missions are a test of a pixel, not of a tactic.

What has not aged is the tension between jokey controls and a permanent grave. Few games of the time can do both on one hill. You keep receiving new recruits. You are not punished with the end of the game for every death; you are punished by knowing whom you are replacing.

Compare the squad with Syndicate from the same year. Bullfrog gives the agents cybernetic bodies; Sensible gives the soldiers names and leaves them in the ground. In a seminar, read the hill aloud. If the class laughs and then goes quiet, the design landed as it was meant to.

In 2004, readers of Retro Gamer voted Cannon Fodder as the 61st top retro game. Cannon Fodder was included in the 2011 list of the best violent video games of all time by The Daily Telegraph, with a comment that "this got a lot of people very upset" but "it was a relentlessly playable game, filled with dark humour and cartoony graphics."
