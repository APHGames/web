A party infected with mind-flayer larvae seeks a cure and decides the fate of Faerûn. An enormous number of simulated combinations, fail-forward solutions, and reactive scenes expands the CRPG toward "tabletop improvisation"; in 2025 PC Gamer placed it first in its Top 100.

In Baldur's Gate 3 a party with a larva in their heads wakes after a nautiloid crash, and the player rolls the visible dice of fifth-edition Dungeons and Dragons, talks, betrays, and often continues even after a failure, because failure has a scene of its own. Up to four people can play co-op in a campaign that reacts to whom you left alive, whom you sent away, and which absurd path you used to open a door. Companions such as Shadowheart or Astarion have an agenda of their own, not only a line for combat.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/491-cover-1.jpg)

Larian under Swen Vincke kept the game in early access from 2020 and released the full computer version in August 2023, with PlayStation 5 arriving immediately afterward. It stands on a developed Divinity Engine and on a license of the rules from Wizards of the Coast. In 2023 it takes The Game Awards' game of the year, and in PC Gamer's 2025 ranking it finishes first. The decision to show the die roll and to let failure keep narrating continues from Divinity, but in someone else's rules language. The consequence is the feeling of a tabletop narrator who says yes even to a plan the designer did not rehearse in one room.

The camp between days is a social level. A rest is not only a refill of slots; it is a place where relationships argue about what you did in the field. The reactivity has a ceiling, and the third act showed it at release: the city is larger and more fragile than the first act, which early access had polished. The studio then patches for months, which is part of the reception, not an appendix outside the game.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/491-screenshot-2.jpg)

In 2023 the game returns big single player to the center of the argument in a year of services and live operations. A CRPG that for years was predicted to stay on the margin becomes the main cultural event here. The fifth-edition rules are simplified where a digital game would break, and kept where the die makes the drama. A tabletop player recognizes the language and at the same time runs into places where Larian deliberately swerved.

That inequality of the acts in the first weeks has aged, as have some exploits the community found before the authors. The density of systems still means not every combination is equally deep. The willingness to let you break a scene and continue holds. It is still the measure of what a reactive RPG means when a studio has early-access time and the courage to say its own yes to licensed rules.

Mind flayers, the gith, and the Sword Coast are familiar scenery the game uses as a shared language, not as museum fidelity. Faerûn here serves the party's conflict, not an encyclopedia. In class, compare one dialogue with Divinity: Original Sin 2. Ask what was added when an in-house elemental simulation became tabletop rules and a joke became more serious acting.
