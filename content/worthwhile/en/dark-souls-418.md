The cursed Undead passes through interconnected Lordran and seeks the meaning of the cycle of fire. Precise stamina combat, death as a learning process, shortcuts, and fragmentary lore created a new dominant design language.

The cursed Undead in Dark Souls rises at the asylum and descends into Lordran, where shortcuts matter more than a map. The player fights over stamina, drinks Estus at a bonfire, and after death runs back for the souls they left on the ground. Combat is the reading of an animation: block, dodge, one or two strikes, retreat, because the stamina ran out. Death is a lesson in layout, not the end of the file.

FromSoftware and director Hidetaka Miyazaki continue from Demon's Souls in 2009, which stayed more tightly bound to the PlayStation 3. Dark Souls comes out in September 2011 also on Xbox 360 and later on PC, and turns a local cult into an international language. The world is a hand-built vertical model. An elevator, a key, and a door opened behind your back are the technology of level design, not a procedural map. The online layer places other players' messages on the ground and sometimes their invasion as well.

The decision to explain as little as possible and to leave the lore in item descriptions means orientation is part of the difficulty. The consequence is a community that assembles the story together, and a player who feels lost in Blighttown with good reason. Bonfires and shortcuts turn the repeated run into a ritual. Once you open the door to the boss from the other side, the map shrinks psychologically even though the geometry stayed.

In 2011, beside Skyrim, this is the opposite bet on fantasy. Bethesda gives freedom and quantity; FromSoftware gives punishment, precision, and silence. The first half, from the asylum to Anor Londo, is a dense network. Later areas, especially Lost Izalith and the boss Bed of Chaos, have long been read by critics as a rushed end of development.

That ending has aged, as has the original 2012 PC version with a locked frame rate and some of the interface, which still speaks like a manual from another decade. Stamina, the readability of attacks, and the sense that one land borders another all hold. The language of bonfires, Estus, and messages on the ground passed into games that otherwise have nothing in common with Lordran.

Covenants and invasions make other players both help and weather. You never know whether the orange mark on the ground is a gift or a trap. In class, take it as a watershed: after it, difficulty in big action RPGs may again be named as a design, not as an accessibility mistake, and at the same time a fair argument about that accessibility begins.

It is the first game in the Dark Souls series and a spiritual successor to FromSoftware's Demon's Souls. Dark Souls has been cited as one of the greatest video games ever made.

Its success led to two sequels, Dark Souls II (2014) and Dark Souls III (2016). Dark Souls is set in Lordran, a decaying kingdom near the end of the Age of Fire.

The First Flame then appears, bringing disparity: heat and cold, life and death, and light and dark. Several beings find powerful souls within the flame, including Gwyn, Lord of Sunlight; the Witch of Izalith; Gravelord Nito, the first of the dead; and the Furtive Pygmy, who finds the Dark Soul.

Gwynevere instructs them to succeed Gwyn by collecting the four Lord Souls needed to rekindle the First Flame: Seath’s Soul, in the Duke’s Archives above Anorlondo; the Witch’s Soul, found in Lost Izalith, even deeper than Blighttown; Nito’s Soul, found in the Tomb of Giants beyond the Catacombs; and the Soul of the Four Kings, found in the ruins of the city of New Londo. Kaathe urges them to let the flame die out and bring about the Age of Dark.

After collecting the Lord Souls, the player character enters the Kiln of the First Flame and defeats Gwyn, who has lost his mind after sacrificing himself to preserve the Flame. The Player then either links the flame, preserving the Age of Fire, or leaves it to fade, beginning the Age of Dark.

The game followed FromSoftware's 2009 action role-playing game Demon's Souls. Before release, Miyazaki said that Dark Souls was not a sequel to Demon's Souls, but a new game with similar concepts made by the same director and producers.

Miyazaki said that the team intended to retain and increase the challenge associated with Demon's Souls, but not simply by making the game harder. Dark Souls was first released for PlayStation 3 in Japan in September 2011, followed by releases for PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 in Western regions the following month.

In April 2012, Namco Bandai Games confirmed Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition for Windows; Game Informer reported that an online petition with almost 100,000 signatures had played a major role in the decision to port the game. In April 2016, Dark Souls became available on Xbox One through backward compatibility.

A remastered version, titled Dark Souls: Remastered, was released for PlayStation 4, Windows, and Xbox One on May 25, 2018, and for Nintendo Switch on October 19, 2018. Game Informer Phil Kollar opined that "the frustration in Dark Souls arises from how [late '00s and early 2010s] games have conditioned us.

Game writer Erik Wolpaw, an early proponent of the Souls series going back to 2009's Demon's Souls, said the game was his favourite of 2011. Eurogamer commented on the quality of the port, stating: "Dark Souls: Prepare to Die Edition does not come with the technical options you would expect from a well-engineered PC game, because it's a port of a console game, and that's all FromSoftware ever promised to deliver.

Anyone who passes up Dark Souls for this reason is cutting off their nose to spite their neckbeard of a face." One of the producers of Dark Souls II, Takeshi Miyazoe, responded to the criticism of the PC version by saying: This is going to sound bad but our main priority was to get the game onto the PC as fast as possible, because people wanted it on the PC. It was more of a publisher (Namco Bandai) decision to say, "Guys, don't worry about this – let's just get it out and see how this works on PC." Dark Souls: Remastered received "generally favorable" reviews from critics, according to review aggregator Metacritic.

Game Revolution gave Dark Souls the Community Choice Game of the Year award. Q-Games' Dylan Cuthbert and Double Fine Productions' Brad Muir chose Dark Souls as Game of the Year.

Awards), the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences nominated Dark Souls for "Role-Playing/Massively Multiplayer Game of the Year". In 2013, Digital Spy named Dark Souls the best game of the seventh console generation.

In 2014, Edge magazine named Dark Souls the best game of the seventh generation of game consoles, noting that while some may initially tire of it, "We've yet to meet a single player for whom persistence has not been enough to transform apathy into all-consuming love." In September 2015, Dark Souls topped the Edge special issue The 100 Greatest Videogames. In 2016, Dark Souls placed fifth on PC Gamer "Best RPGs of all time" list.

Dark Souls has been cited as one of the greatest video games of all time. Games cited to have been influenced by Dark Souls include Destiny, Alienation, Lords of the Fallen, The Surge, Salt and Sanctuary, Shovel Knight, Titan Souls, Enter the Gungeon, The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, Nioh, and God of War, as well as features used on the PlayStation 4 console.

Dark Souls was also cited as an inspiration for the television show Stranger Things.
