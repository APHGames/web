Ideological factions colonize the planet Chiron after the fall of the Earth expedition. Diplomacy built on philosophy, terraforming, and modular units give the Civilization model unusual thematic depth.

On the planet Chiron you do not found America or Rome. You found a faction with a philosophy, Morgan, Gaia, the Believers, or the University, and the diplomatic table argues about what humanity should be, not about a historical card. The terrain gets rewritten. A farm, a borehole, and a condenser are not only city improvements; they are a sentence saying the map is material and the planet under the fungus is not a neutral board.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/279-cover-1.jpg)

Brian Reynolds designed it at Firaxis, which he founded with Sid Meier and Jeff Briggs, and EA published it in 1999. Meier's name is on the box, and the design hand is Reynolds's again, as with Civilization II. The decision to exchange a national stereotype for an ideology had a consequence for diplomacy. The opponent has an opinion you hear in a speech, and research, terraforming, and social engineering are three ways to push that opinion into the landscape.

The unit workshop assembles weapons from parts. The technology tree is not a list of finished tanks, and two factions with the same science can roll out with different doctrines, which is a lesson from Master of Orion II carried into an alien ecology. In 1999, when strategy likes to take the Middle Ages or a space opera, Alpha Centauri is unusually literary. Speaking leaders turn a 4X into an argument and not only a race for score.

What has aged is the interface and the late micromanagement of bases. What has not aged is the faction writing, terraforming as a fantasy, and the feeling that a Civilization game can be about an idea, not about a flag. In seminar, give students the same map and two factions with opposite ethics. The first hour will already be a different game, and that is the point.

The mind worms and a planet that is not a dead deposit are an enemy outside diplomacy. The design says that colonization has a victim that is not only the second civilization. Against Civilization II there is less of a history textbook here and more of a science-fiction essay. Reynolds carries the systems with him and changes the theme, which is a cleaner authorial step than another nation on the list.

The social choices, politics, economics, values, are a turn you do not see on the map immediately and that still changes what the map is allowed to be. It is a hidden board, and a seminar should pull it onto the table. The game is proof that Sid Meier's licensed name can carry a work that peels away from Civilization. The peeling is the reason it is taught, not a reason to hide it under the brand.

A month after Alpha Centauri s February 1999 release, the Firaxis team began work on the expansion pack, Sid Meier's Alien Crossfire. In 2000, both Alpha Centauri and Alien Crossfire were ported to Classic Mac OS by Aspyr Media and to Linux by Loki Software.

Alpha Centauri received wide critical acclaim upon its release, with reviewers voicing respect for the game's pedigree, especially that of Reynolds and Meier.
