Nights flies dream circuits and links objects into chains. Analog curves of movement and scoring according to fluidity created a game more about graceful flight than about a target route.

You are not flying a race track from start to finish. You are flying a curve: Nights collects rings and links objects into a chain for as long as the smoothness holds. The score and the grade say more than merely reaching the end. You can fly through the game quickly and still not have played it if the chain keeps breaking.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/207-screenshot-1.jpg)

Sonic Team released it on the Saturn in 1996, with Yuji Naka and with a character designed by Naoto Ohshima. The Saturn analog controller belongs to it, because the direction of flight is a curve, not an eight-way click. The Saturn is not strong at general 3D, and yet this game is one of its most convincing. It stays inside a limited space where the hardware can keep up with smooth movement.

The decision to score smoothness instead of finishing order has a consequence that takes the game out of both the platformer and the race. The goal is grace, and a mistake is a break in the arc, not a fall into a pit. Two children, Claris and Elliot, enter the dream as a pretext. The actual system is the flight, the chain, and a night landscape that reacts to how you live in it.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/207-screenshot-2.jpg)

In 1996, when 3D on a console often means walking and jumping in a corridor, Sonic Team offers control as the pleasure of an analog stick. It is a showcase for the pad, not for a map. What has aged is the abstract fairy tale if you expect a modern explanation of the world, and also the shortness of the campaign, which today's market measures in hours.

The arc has not aged. A well-flown chain still explains that a game can be about the quality of movement, not about a list of tasks. In seminar, put it beside F-Zero X. Both sacrifice scenery to speed and to the readability of movement, each in a different sport.

Anyone who plays it on a digital pad will not see the design. The controller here is not a peripheral; it is the condition of the curve. For the history of the Saturn, Nights is proof that a losing console can carry a game the winning platform that year could not imitate in feel.
