Mercenaries liberate Arulco sector by sector. Personalities, relationships, ballistics, and an open strategic map create one of the deepest squad simulations.

You hire mercenaries with a salary, an opinion, and a rifle, and you take Arulco sector by sector. The strategic map is not a campaign rail; it is an order you choose, and each sector is a small dangerous task. The ballistics deal with stance, cover, aiming time, and interrupts. You respect bullets because a shot is not an abstract hit from an action game; it is time you spent in the turn.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/269-cover-1.jpg)

Sir-Tech Canada released it in 1999 with TalonSoft. It continues the first Jagged Alliance and pushes it toward personalities who may not want to serve together, and toward a more open war. The decision to let the mercenaries be people, not only statistics, had a consequence for the plan. The roster is the cast of the game, and a quarrel in the team will break your tactics as reliably as bad cover.

In 1999 a tactical RPG is often isometric fantasy. Here it is a modern rifle, a militia in a liberated sector, and a humor that does not deny that a bullet will remove you from the fight. What has aged is the dense interface and the graphics, which hide rules that are otherwise readable. What has not aged is the interrupt in a turn and the map, on which progress is visible as a front, not as a percentage of the story.

In seminar, let them play one sector twice, the second time without cover. Students will count what aiming time means better than from a weapon table. Training the militia is the strategy between battles. Liberate a town and leave, and the enemy returns, and that is why the war is an economy, not only a gunfight.

Against Fallout there is less dialogue simulation here and more shooting. Both games of the end of the decade take the turn seriously, and each punishes a careless step differently. The mercenaries' personalities are text the system needs. Without them the game would be an excellent ballistics board, and with them it is a campaign people return to for the crew.

The difficulty of the opening is part of the contract. The game does not believe it should lead you by the hand for the first hour, and a seminar should say that before students pass the first sector. It is one of the deepest tactical simulations of its time precisely because the sector, the bullet, and the character pull on one rope.
