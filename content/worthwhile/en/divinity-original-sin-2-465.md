A group of Source users competes for divinity in a reactive world. Co-op, origin characters, elemental simulation, and a willingness to accept unplanned solutions prepared the technological and design foundation of BG3.

In Divinity: Original Sin 2 a group of sourcerers flees Fort Joy, and on their turn the player combines surfaces, height, and a dialogue that can break even a fight if you said the right sentence earlier. Up to four people can play together, and origin characters have a past of their own. The world reacts even to a solution the designer considered unlikely, which is the reputation on which Larian keeps building.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/465-cover-1.jpg)

Larian under Swen Vincke released the game in September 2017 again after a Kickstarter, on the Divinity Engine. The Definitive Edition a year later rewrites the last act according to what players managed to break. Physical and magical armor is a controversial decision: much mixed damage pays less than a pure single school. The consequence is a more tactical and at the same time narrower party build, which Baldur's Gate 3 will later not want to repeat in the same way.

Fort Joy is a prison that teaches the systems before it lets you onto the island. The studio thereby admits that open reactivity needs a narrow first act, or the player gets lost in the possibilities. Teleportation pyramids, beds, and elements keep the road between fights a game. Rest is not only a refill of health; it is a choice of place and risk.

In 2017 this is a peak of the independent, yet large, CRPG. Beside the first game it is more serious, more voiced, and more willing to let you be a monster, a hero, or both in one sentence. Co-op changes the pace of dialogues, because partners can speak against you. That is a different contract from shared shooting, and in a classroom you can tell from it who wants to be the narrator.

The double armor system has aged, as have inventory tetris and some of the jokes that drown out more serious quests. The third act in the original release also failed to match the density of the first. The reactivity holds, and so does the feeling that a bad plan is still a plan. Without this game you cannot explain why this particular studio gets Baldur's Gate.

Origin stories give statistics a biography in a way similar to Dragon Age once, but with a greater willingness to let the past break the present scene. In class, use it as a bridge: take one room from Original Sin and one from the second game and show what was added in speech, in co-op, and in the willingness to tell the player yes.
