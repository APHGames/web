After releasing Doom&nbsp;3 in 2004, id Software began working on a new intellectual property, Rage, and unsuccessfully sought to license the Doom franchise to another developer as it had with Wolfenstein.

The studio, which had 19 employees at the time of Doom&nbsp;3 s release, struggled to simultaneously develop Rage and Doom&nbsp;4. Development of Doom was first revealed via job listings on id's website on May&nbsp;7, 2008, for a project titled Doom&nbsp;4.

Asked in April&nbsp;2009 about whether Doom&nbsp;4 would be a sequel to Doom&nbsp;3 or a reboot, id CEO Todd Hollenshead stated that it was neither. Doom&nbsp;4 was intended to feature a story written by British science fiction writer Graham Joyce.

On April&nbsp;3, 2013, Kotaku published an exposé that described Doom&nbsp;4 as being trapped in "development hell" and allegedly mismanaged. Bethesda's vice president of marketing, Pete Hines, acknowledged difficulty in the development of Doom&nbsp;4 that same day.

In an August&nbsp;2013 interview with IGN, Willits said that the pre-2011 Doom&nbsp;4 "had a bit of schizophrenia, a little bit of an identity crisis." Marty Stratton, Doom s director, described the period between 2011 and 2013 as a "rolling reboot". In an interview by Nathan Grayson of Rock, Paper, Shotgun on August&nbsp;6, 2013, Willits stated that there was no publicly available timeline for updates on Doom&nbsp;4.

Stratton, Martin, and id used the original Doom games as their template for Doom s artwork and gameplay, and abandoned the slower pace and survival horror themes of Doom&nbsp;3. Development of Doom focused primarily on refining its combat, dubbed "push forward combat".

Speaking to Noclip, Martin said that the story was one of the last elements of the game to be implemented, and that he and Gascoine aimed for a lighter, self-aware narrative; In his direction for Doom s story, Martin was inspired by action movies such as RoboCop&nbsp;(1987), Evil Dead 2&nbsp;(1987), and The Last Boy Scout&nbsp;(1991), and paintings by American artist Frank Frazetta. Patches for Doom after its release introduced a new photo mode, a new game option for holding weapons in the center of the screen as in the original games, and support for the Vulkan API. A sequel to Doom, Doom Eternal, was released on March 20, 2020, and is set years after, showcasing the Slayer's combat against demonic forces on Earth. Post-release analysis by id and the Doom playerbase showed a reliance on the Super Shotgun to the exclusion of all other weapons by a large segment of the game's players.

To encourage players to use other weapons, id reduced the amount of ammo the player could carry in Doom Eternal and reworked the other weapons, weapon mods, and enemies in the game, and added weaknesses to specific weapons to specific enemies.
