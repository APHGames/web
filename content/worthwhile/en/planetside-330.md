Hundreds of players fight over continents in a persistent war. It demonstrated MMO scale for a first-person shooter long before modern "massive battle" games.

In PlanetSide, hundreds of players fight over continents that do not reset after twenty minutes. The war is a shift, not a match with a final scoreboard. Three factions, the Terran Republic, the New Conglomerate, and the Vanu Sovereignty, have different technology and a different doctrine. Infantry and vehicles share the same map until someone holds a base through the night.

![In the game](/img/worthwhile-games/media/330-screenshot-1.jpg)

Sony Online Entertainment launched it in 2003. It is a shooter at MMO scale at a moment when most shooters still end the round and start again. The consequence of persistent territory is logistics. Victory is not a kill count but whether your side still holds, in the morning, the factory the tanks come out of.

Certifications and roles force the player to specialize. A lone shooter without a driver and without a technician is of little use on a continent. In 2003, beside Battlefield 1942, PlanetSide shifts the scale from one map to a war that has no break between matches.

The look and the controls have aged into the early years of the genre. The idea of a persistent front has not, and PlanetSide 2 will later bring it back on newer hardware. For students of massive games it is proof that a first-person shooter did not have to wait for a modern marketing slogan about giant battles. The scale was here a decade earlier.

The front moves even while you are offline. The world has a shift of its own, and that is a feeling an instanced game cannot sell. Long-range fighting and travel between bases are the price of a continent. Whoever wants only an arena will find it elsewhere and miss the reason the map is so large.

The economy of the war is more fragile than in EVE, because this is about shooting, not about a market. Even so, territory is a social contract, not a script. When you talk about it, do not measure it by the number of missions. Measure it by whether a base is still being fought over an hour after you logged out.

PlanetSide is a series of massively multiplayer online first-person shooter video games published by Daybreak Game Company. PlanetSide 2, a sequel, was released in 2012, featuring faster-paced gameplay, modern first-person shooter mechanics and high graphical fidelity.

Rather than featuring small, instanced matches like many other first-person shooters, the PlanetSide series features massive, seamless continents that allow battles potentially thousands of users can participate in. Both PlanetSide and PlanetSide 2 have set the Guinness World Record for "Most players in an online FPS battle," with the latter still holding the record to this day; this record has since been unofficially surpassed by PlanetSide 2 players in both 2020 and 2022.

PlanetSide Next was a version of PlanetSide with updated assets developed by Sony Online Entertainment's SOGA studio for release in Asian markets. PlanetSide Arena was a spin-off of PlanetSide 2 released for early access on Steam for Microsoft Windows on September 19, 2019.
