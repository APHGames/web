Paradise City joins races and crashes in an open road network. It removed the traditional race menu: an event starts directly at an intersection, and the city functions as the central interface.

In Burnout Paradise the city is an intersection, not a menu. You stop at the lights, hold the throttle, and a race starts from where you are, and after a crash you drive back through the city, because a restart is not the game's first thought. A takedown, a billboard, and a gate are a collection scattered across the road network. Progress is exploration, not a list of tracks in a garage.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/394-cover-1.jpg)

Criterion, still in the era of Alex Ward and Fiona Sperry, published it in 2008 through Electronic Arts on the PlayStation 3 and the Xbox 360. The cars are fictional, as in the earlier Burnouts, and therefore the comedy of metal will not age with the end of a license. The consequence of the city as an interface is freedom and a price. You do not have to click a race in a menu, and at the same time a failure can cost you a long road back to the start, which players that year felt as the price of openness.

In 2008, beside Test Drive Unlimited, it is the second open racer, but another temperament. There, a social island. Here, an aggressive city that wants you to take someone down at an intersection. The driving still feels contagious, and that has not aged. The missing instant restart has aged as a price that later updates and player habit worked around in various ways.

Online Freeburn is a city shared with other drivers. Criterion thereby continues its own track aggression and gives it an audience, not only a score. For interface students the cancelled menu is a class. When the start of a race is a gesture at an intersection, the city has to be readable as a button, or the player gets lost among the traffic lights.

Showtime crashes and aftertouch remain from the third game. Openness did not add them; it only gave them more intersections on which they can be triggered. Paradise City is one mood, not three cities as in San Andreas. The density is intentional: everywhere there should be a reason to drive fast and a reason to hit someone.

A remaster later brings the game back without some of the original licensed songs. The driving survives the exchange of the radio, because the center of gravity is in the intersection, not in the track. When you play it today, after a fall let yourself drive to the start without a shortcut at least once. That road is the game's argument, not its defect, even if it tastes like a defect.

Upon release, Burnout Paradise was met with very positive reviews, according to review aggregator website Metacritic. Additionally, Paradise won GameSpot's award for Best Downloadable Content in 2008.

During the 12th Annual Interactive Achievement Awards, the Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences awarded Burnout Paradise with "Racing Game of the Year".
