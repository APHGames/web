Empires colonize the galaxy and develop technology and diplomacy. It popularized the compact space 4X model and a term that became one of the main strategies of the PC.

You found colonies, choose research, and at the negotiating table you hold empires that do not play the same strategy as you. One match is a whole galaxy in a relatively short time. This is not Civilization across a thousand turns; it is a compact arc from the first jump to the final clash.

![Cover](/img/worthwhile-games/media/151-cover-1.jpg)

Steve Barcia and Simtex released the game in 1993 through MicroProse on DOS. With it, and with Master of Magic, the small Texas studio founds its 4X reputation. In a review, Alan Emrich names the model 4X: explore, expand, exploit, exterminate. The word is born with this game, not as a genre label decided in advance.

The engine is a map of stars and management screens. A battle can be tactical, or you can let it be calculated, so the macro layer is not always punished with micro clicking. The decision to give the races sharp differences has a consequence. Diplomacy is a reading of a foreign interest, not just a relationship number on a bar.

In 1993, beside Civilization, Master of Orion shows that the same appetite for a system can be spatial and shorter. The player finishes an empire's story in evenings, not in a month. What has aged is the interface and some random events that feel like a table with an illustration.

What has not aged is the asymmetry and the feeling that a technology is a choice that closes other doors. The tree is not one correct ladder. Orion as a threshold at the end holds the finale. The game can look like diplomacy for a long time and then demand that you have a fleet.

Compare the length with Civilization. Meier builds a history it is a pity to leave; Barcia builds a match that has a conclusion. In a seminar, explain 4X from here, not from a later encyclopedia. The students will see that the label describes a loop, not the theme of space.
