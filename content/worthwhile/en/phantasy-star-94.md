Alis leads a party among several planets against tyranny. A sci-fi/fantasy mix, a distinctive heroine, and pseudo-3D dungeons set it apart from the dominant medieval JRPG model.

After her brother's death, Alis leads a party across planets, gathers allies in turn-based battles, and in a dungeon that is in first person looks for a way against a tyranny that is not a medieval warlock. The planets are not just a new color of the map. They have different terrain, different technology, and a different reason why a walking fantasy suddenly is not enough.

Sega published the game in 1987 on the Master System. Rieko Kodama took a major part in the visual and design side, and Yuji Naka in the programming; on a console that is losing to the Famicom in Japan, an RPG appears that does not want to look like Dragon Quest. The first-person dungeons are slow and easy to get lost in. The Master System's technique reaches here for the computer crawler and pays for it with a pace that the console's home action otherwise does not have.

The decision to give the quest a heroine and a spacefaring civilization had the consequence that the Japanese RPG stops being one fairy tale about a king and a slime. Alis is not a sprite swap in a male story; she is the reason the quest begins. Combat is still a menu and a statistic. The difference is not that the genre disappears, but that the scenery and the protagonist stop being obvious.

In 1987 Final Fantasy on the Famicom is building the second great pillar of the medieval party. Phantasy Star, on Sega's weaker console, answers with a different sky and a different face at the head of the party. First person in the dungeon is a commitment that becomes familiar quickly. Even so, in 1987 on a console it feels like proof that a cartridge can also do a view that had belonged to the Apple II.

What has aged is the grind of random battles and wandering in a grid that does not say why you should come back. What has not aged is Alis as the heroine of the quest and the willingness to mix a sword with a planet without turning it into a joke.

A line runs from this party that will keep the science-fiction fantasy even at the moment Sega moves to stronger machines. In a seminar, set the opening of Final Fantasy beside the opening of Phantasy Star. The students should name what is the genre and what is only a habit of scenery.

The series debuted in 1987 on the Master System with Phantasy Star, and continues into the present with Phantasy Star Online 2 and other extensions of the Phantasy Star Online sub-series. There are currently four distinct Phantasy Star subseries, each set in its own universe.

With the closure of Phantasy Star Universe servers in 2012, the online-only "Episode 3" can no longer be played at all, and "Episode 2" cannot be played on the Xbox 360 or PC versions. The plot, setting, and themes of the Phantasy Star series vary dramatically from the franchise's early installments to the multiplayer titles of today.

Phantasy Star, Phantasy Star II, and Phantasy Star IV all deal with the concept of evil as a living, sentient entity that takes an active interest in galactic events. In Phantasy Star IV, the source of this being appears, known as the Profound Darkness.

Palma is destroyed during the events of Phantasy Star II, when the prison satellite Gaira crashes into the planet. Several colony ships fled from the disaster, and one of those ships, the Alisa III, is the setting of Phantasy Star III.

Phantasy Star Online and Phantasy Star Universe take place in different planetary systems. The original Phantasy Star was released for the Master System in Japan on December 20, 1987.

Phantasy Star, along with Dragon Quest, and Final Fantasy, distinguished itself as a pioneer of what came to be defined as console role-playing. Phantasy Star is recognized as featuring one of the first female lead characters, Alis Landale, alongside Samus Aran of Metroid.

James Fudge of GameSpy said of the game that "everything about Phantasy Star was uncommon, fun, and strange." and is regarded as "a game of many firsts" according to Nintendo Power. Phantasy Star III: Generations of Doom featured an innovative and original branching storyline, which spans three generations of characters and can be altered depending on which character the protagonist of each generation marries, leading to four possible endings.

IGN writer Jeremy Conrad stated that "anyone who played video games through the 8 and 16-bit eras would know that name [Phantasy Star]" and called the first four main series games "epic". Many of the series' games, including Online Episodes I & II and Phantasy Star Zero, have generally favorable scores on Metacritic.

Phantasy Star Online 2 became a commercial success for Sega as it has made over 900 million dollars since its release in 2012. Two anime series have been created around the Phantasy Star Online 2 world: Phantasy Star Online 2: The Animation, a prequel to that game's "Episode 4" storyline; and Phantasy Star Online 2: Episode Oracle, a retelling of the events of the first three "Episodes" of the game (known as the "Oracle" arc in the game).
